The best free PDF app for Android that requires no sign-up might not be a Play Store app at all. Browser-based tools in Chrome handle most common PDF tasks directly on your phone, with no account, no installation, and no files sent to a server. But if you work with PDFs every day, a native app has its place too.

Here's an honest look at both options.

## What you actually need a PDF app to do

Most Android PDF searches come down to a handful of tasks: compress a file before emailing it, merge a couple of documents, sign a form, convert phone photos to PDF, or view a PDF without fighting a clunky built-in viewer.

Each task has different requirements. Some benefit from offline access. Some just need a quick result without friction. Knowing which you're dealing with helps you pick the right tool.

## Native Android PDF apps that don't require an account

**Xodo** is the best all-round native PDF app for Android if you want no mandatory sign-in. It handles annotation, form filling, signatures, page reordering, and cloud sync with Google Drive and Dropbox. Most features work without creating a Xodo account, and you can [download it from Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.xodo.pdf.reader) for free.

**MuPDF mini** is for reading, full stop. It collects no data, shows no ads, and has no in-app purchases. If you just need a clean viewer that stays out of the way, this is it.

**PDF-X** does editing and conversion entirely offline. It handles merging, splitting, compressing, and annotating on-device with no uploads, no account, and no subscription prompts.

None of these three require an account to get started. You can open a PDF immediately after installing.

## Browser-based tools: a good option for occasional tasks

If you compress a PDF once a month or merge two files before submitting a job application, installing a dedicated app adds more overhead than the task warrants. A browser tool removes that friction.

Open Chrome on your Android phone, go to the tool page, do the task, and download the result. The whole thing takes under a minute. No storage space is used, nothing needs updating, and no permissions are requested beyond what the browser already has.

The privacy angle matters too. Many PDF apps request broad storage permissions and, depending on the service, upload your document to a remote server for processing. Browser tools that run entirely in the browser tab never transmit your file. It stays in Chrome's memory on your phone and downloads directly when you're done.

## How to compress a PDF on Android (and when not to)

Before reaching for a compression tool, know this: compression only helps if your PDF contains images or scanned pages.

The [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) works by converting every page to a [JPEG image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG). That's useful for a scanned receipt or a photo-heavy presentation. A 913.4 KB six-page image file came down to 315.3 KB at Medium quality in testing, a 65% reduction.

For a text-only PDF, the same approach does the opposite. A 20-page text report went from 23.5 KB to 1.4 MB. Text stored as vector characters is compact; a photograph of text is not. If your PDF is an invoice, a contract, or a form, don't compress it.

To compress a scanned PDF on Android:

1. Open Chrome and go to [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf).
2. Tap the file picker and choose your PDF from storage or Google Drive.
3. Select a quality level. Medium is a reasonable starting point.
4. Wait for the compression to finish, then tap Download.

One more thing: compressing the same file twice makes it bigger. If Medium isn't enough, go back to your original and try High instead.

## How to merge PDFs on Android

Merging in a browser is straightforward. The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) works in Chrome on Android without any installation.

1. Open [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) in Chrome.
2. Tap to add your PDF files. You can drag to reorder them.
3. Tap Merge and download the combined file.

Merging doesn't inflate the output size. In testing, three files totalling 971.9 KB came out at 970.5 KB. So if your individual files add up to 20 MB, the merged result will be essentially the same size.

One thing to watch: if any of your PDFs has interactive form fields that were filled in, those fields get stripped during the merge. The field values may not survive. The fix is to use the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) on each filled form before merging, which converts the field content into static page content that does survive.

## Other PDF tasks on Android

**Sign a PDF:** The [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) lets you draw a signature with your finger and place it on the page. Quick for informal signatures, and your signed document downloads directly to your phone.

**Images to PDF:** If you've taken photos of a document with your phone camera and need to send them as a single PDF, the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) combines multiple JPGs or PNGs into one file, one image per page.

**PDF to images:** Need to pull a page out of a PDF as an image file? The [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) converts each page to a JPG you can share on any app.

**Split a PDF:** If you only need certain pages from a larger document, the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) lets you specify which pages to keep and saves them as a new file.

## How to add a PDF tool shortcut to your Android home screen

Android lets you save any website as a home screen shortcut. Open the tool in Chrome, tap the three-dot menu in the top right corner, and tap "Add to Home screen." The icon appears on your home screen and opens the tool directly in Chrome, without any app installation.

## Which should you choose?

Go with a native app (Xodo or PDF-X) if you annotate PDFs regularly, work offline, or read long documents on your phone.

Go with browser tools if your needs are occasional. Compress a scan before emailing it, merge files for a job application, sign a form every few weeks. The browser handles all of that without taking up space on your phone or asking you to create an account.
