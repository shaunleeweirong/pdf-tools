## The best free Nitro PDF alternatives run in your browser

If you're looking for Nitro PDF alternatives, you're not alone. Nitro stopped offering a free version some time ago, and the company has been retiring perpetual licenses in favor of subscription-only pricing. For individuals and small teams that use PDF tools a few times a month, a recurring subscription doesn't make sense.

The practical question is: what do you actually use Nitro for? For most people, the answer is a short list: combine files, shrink a PDF before attaching it to an email, sign something, maybe add a password. All of those are available for free, in your browser, with no account required.

## Why people are leaving Nitro PDF

Nitro built its product for enterprise document workflows: team management, audit trails, e-signature routing, analytics. That's powerful for large organizations, but it means individual users and small teams pay for a lot they don't use.

The pricing structure has also shifted. Where users once could buy a perpetual desktop license, Nitro moved to subscription plans. [According to G2](https://www.g2.com/products/nitro-pdf/pricing), prices start around $15 per month and scale significantly for team plans. For a tool most people use occasionally, that's a high ongoing cost.

The result is a wave of users looking for something simpler: free, browser-based, no account.

## What browser tools handle well

Most of what people use Nitro for falls into a handful of categories. Here's how free browser tools compare for each:

### Merging PDFs

The [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool lets you combine multiple files in any order, with a simple drag-and-drop interface. In our testing, merging three files totaling 971.9 KB produced a 970.5 KB output, so merging doesn't bloat your file. The merged PDF comes in marginally smaller than the sum of its parts.

One thing to know: if you merge a filled PDF form with another document, the form fields don't survive. The page content comes through fine, but interactive fields are dropped. The fix is to flatten the form first using the [Flatten PDF](/flatten-pdf) tool, which bakes the field values into the page permanently. Then merge.

### Compressing PDFs

The [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) tool shrinks file size by rasterizing each page to a JPEG image. For image-heavy PDFs and scanned documents, the results are significant. A six-page image deck shrank from 913.4 KB to 315.3 KB at Medium quality, and to 175.6 KB at High compression.

The important caveat is the type of document. This approach makes text-only PDFs larger, not smaller. A 20-page text report grew from 23.5 KB to 1.4 MB in the same test run. So only compress PDFs that are primarily images or scans. Text documents, invoices, and contracts shouldn't go through a rasterizing compressor. For those, try splitting or zipping instead.

Also: running compression twice doesn't help. A second pass at the same level actually increased the already-compressed file from 315.3 KB to 598.9 KB in our tests. If Medium isn't enough, start fresh from the original at a higher compression level.

### Signing a document

The [Sign PDF](/sign-pdf) tool lets you draw a signature in your browser and place it on the first page. No account, no downloads, no watermarks. For forms that need typed input first, use [Fill PDF Form](/fill-form) to enter text into the fields, then use Sign PDF to add your signature.

### Adding a password or removing one

The [Protect PDF](/protect-pdf) tool uses [AES-256 encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Encryption_Standard) to lock a PDF with a password, entirely client-side. If you have a password-protected PDF and know the password, [Unlock PDF](/unlock-pdf) removes the protection and returns the file to within a few bytes of its original size.

### Converting PDF pages to images (and back)

The [PDF to JPG](/pdf-to-jpg) tool exports each page as a JPG image. Going the other direction, [Images to PDF](/jpg-to-pdf) takes JPG and PNG files and turns them into a PDF, one image per page.

### Splitting and organizing pages

The [Split PDF](/split-pdf) tool extracts specific page ranges into separate files. If you want to pull pages out without splitting the original, [Extract Pages](/extract-pages) does that. To reorder or remove pages visually, [Organize PDF](/organize-pdf) gives you a page thumbnail view to rearrange and delete.

## How to handle the most common Nitro workflows

**Combining several PDFs into one:**
1. Open the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool.
2. Add your files and drag them into the order you want.
3. Click Merge and download the result.

**Shrinking a PDF before emailing:**
1. Check whether your PDF is image-heavy or text-based. If it's mostly text, don't compress it.
2. Open the [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) tool and upload the file.
3. Choose Medium for a reasonable balance of size and quality.
4. Download the compressed file and check the size before sending. Gmail and [Outlook](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/file-size-limits-for-microsoft-365-messages-a3f29f29-0064-4f79-beb0-30a23f449bff) both cap attachments at 25 MB.

**Signing a document:**
1. Open the [Sign PDF](/sign-pdf) tool and upload your PDF.
2. Draw your signature in the provided canvas.
3. Place it on the page and download.

**Password-protecting a confidential PDF:**
1. Open the [Protect PDF](/protect-pdf) tool.
2. Upload the file and set a password.
3. Download the encrypted version.

## What browser tools don't replace

Browser-based tools cover everyday tasks well, but they're not a complete Nitro replacement for every use case. A few things they don't match:

**Multi-party e-signature workflows.** If you need to send a document to multiple signers, track who signed, and receive notifications, that requires a platform like DocuSign or a dedicated e-sign service. Free browser tools let you sign your own documents, not manage a signing workflow for others.

**Optical character recognition (OCR).** Making a scanned PDF fully searchable, with selectable text, requires OCR processing. That's a computationally heavier task that most free browser tools don't offer. If you need OCR, dedicated tools or desktop apps handle it better.

**Enterprise features.** Team management, document tracking, and compliance reporting are Nitro Pro features aimed at legal, finance, and HR teams. Those aren't replicated by free individual tools.

For personal use and small teams handling everyday tasks, browser-based tools cover about 80 to 90% of what most Nitro users actually do. If your workflow is merge, compress, sign, and the occasional conversion, the free tools handle it.

## Comparing types of free Nitro alternatives

There are a few categories worth knowing:

**Browser-based tools (client-side)** run entirely in your browser. Your files don't go to any server. This is the best choice for sensitive documents. The trade-off is that advanced features like OCR and multi-party signing aren't available.

**Free desktop apps** like [PDF-XChange Editor](https://www.tracker-software.com/product/pdf-xchange-editor) offer a deeper feature set with a free tier, closer to what Nitro provides. They require installation and process files locally on your machine.

**Cloud-based platforms** like Smallpdf or DocHub handle more complex workflows but upload your files to their servers. Most offer a limited number of free operations per day before pushing you toward a paid plan.

For people switching away from Nitro, browser-based tools are the fastest and safest starting point. There's nothing to install, your files stay private, and the core operations are free without any daily caps or watermarks.
