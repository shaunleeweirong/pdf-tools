You can convert a PDF to JPG on Android right now by opening the [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) in Chrome. No app to download, no account to create. Tap to select your PDF, wait a moment for each page to process, and download the JPG files to your phone.

## The quickest way: use a browser tool in Chrome

A browser-based converter is the simplest approach on Android. Open Chrome, visit a PDF to JPG converter, and the whole thing happens inside your browser tab. You pick the file, the tool processes each page, and you save the results. Nothing stays on your phone after you close the tab.

Our [PDF to JPG converter](/pdf-to-jpg) is free and doesn't need a sign-up. It converts each page of your PDF to its own JPG file, so you can share, upload, or message individual pages without dealing with the whole document.

## Step-by-step: convert a PDF to JPG in Android Chrome

1. Open **Chrome** on your Android phone.
2. Go to the [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg).
3. Tap **Select PDF** and choose your file from the picker. You can browse your Downloads folder, Google Drive, or any location the Files app can reach.
4. The tool converts each page to a JPG. For longer documents, a short wait is normal.
5. Tap the download button next to each page image to save it to your phone.

Your downloaded files land in the **Downloads** folder, which you'll find in the **Files** app (or any file manager on your device).

## What happens when the PDF has multiple pages?

Each page becomes its own JPG file. A 10-page PDF gives you 10 separate images, labeled by page order.

This is worth knowing before you start. If you only need one or two pages from a long document, it's faster to extract those pages first with the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf), then convert just that section. Fewer files to manage and a quicker conversion overall.

## Where do the converted JPG files go on Android?

Chrome saves downloaded files to the **Downloads** folder on your phone. To find them:

1. Open the **Files** app (pre-installed on most Android phones; look for "Files" or [Files by Google](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.nbu.files)).
2. Tap **Downloads**.
3. Your JPG files are there, named by page number.

If you want the images to appear in Google Photos, open Google Photos, tap **Library**, then **Device folders**, and select **Downloads**. The images show up there even if they don't land in the main Photos feed automatically.

On Samsung phones, the default Gallery app typically picks up your Downloads folder and groups images there without any extra steps.

## How to access PDFs from Google Drive or email

When you tap **Select PDF** in the converter, Android opens the standard file picker. From there you can reach:

- **Google Drive** and any folder in your Drive
- **Downloads** on your phone
- Any other storage location the Files app shows

If your PDF came as a Gmail attachment, tap the attachment, then tap the download icon or share icon and choose **Save to Files** or **Download**. Once it's in Downloads, the converter will find it.

On older Android versions, the file picker may look different or show fewer locations. Installing [Files by Google](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.nbu.files) gives you a reliable file manager that works well with the browser picker on any Android version.

## What about Adobe Acrobat Reader on Android?

[Adobe Acrobat Reader](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adobe.reader) is free on the Play Store and handles viewing, annotating, and signing PDFs well. But exporting a PDF to JPG requires an Acrobat Pro subscription, which carries a monthly fee.

A browser tool gives you the same conversion for free. If you're converting the odd PDF here and there, paying for a subscription just to export images doesn't make sense.

## Does it work in browsers other than Chrome?

Yes. The [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) works in any modern Android browser. Chrome is the most common and its file picker integrates cleanly with Google Drive and Downloads. Firefox on Android also works without issue. The conversion happens inside the browser, so the output is the same regardless of which browser you use.

If you're using a browser other than Chrome, the file picker may look slightly different, but you'll still have access to your Downloads folder and any cloud storage apps installed on your phone.

## What if the PDF is very large?

Typical documents, a handful of pages up to about a dozen, convert quickly in a mobile browser. A larger file, say a 50-page illustrated report, will take noticeably longer because each page is processed separately.

If speed matters and you only need part of the document, split it first. Pull out the specific pages you want with the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf), then convert just that smaller file. You get the same images in less time and with fewer files to sort through.

Very large PDFs in the hundreds of megabytes can also be slow to upload on a cellular connection. If your file is particularly heavy, waiting until you're on Wi-Fi before starting the conversion is worth it.

## A note on text PDFs

One thing to know: the converter works by turning each PDF page into an image. That's perfect when your PDF contains scans, photos, or illustrations. For a PDF that's mostly text, such as a contract or a report, the JPG output will be a photograph of the text rather than selectable characters. If you need to copy or search the text later, converting to JPG isn't the right step. For those cases, keeping the original PDF and using it directly is usually the better choice.

For anything that started life as an image, such as scanned receipts, photos, or illustrated pages, the converter does exactly what you need.
