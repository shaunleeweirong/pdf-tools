## Converting Word to PDF without paying for software

If you need to convert a Word document to PDF free, the simplest path depends on whether you already have Microsoft Word. If you do, the job is done in about ten seconds through Word's own export. If you do not, Google Docs, LibreOffice, and Mac's built-in Print dialog all handle it for free, no sign-up needed.

This guide covers four reliable methods and explains what actually causes formatting to break, so you can pick the right approach for your situation.

## Why formatting breaks (and how to avoid it)

Before picking a method, it helps to know the main reason Word-to-PDF conversions go wrong.

Your Word document uses fonts installed on your computer. Calibri, Arial, Times New Roman, and any custom fonts you have added are referenced by name in the .docx file. When you upload that file to an online converter, the converter's server may not have those same fonts installed. It substitutes the closest match. A substituted font may have slightly different character widths, and a document that fits neatly on two pages in Calibri can overflow to three pages in the substitute.

The fix: use a conversion method that runs on your own machine, where your fonts already exist. Methods 1, 3, and 4 below all do this.

## Method 1: Microsoft Word's built-in export (best quality)

This is the cleanest method and works on both Windows and Mac.

1. Open your .doc or .docx file in Word.
2. Click **File**, then **Save As**.
3. In the file format dropdown, choose **PDF**.
4. Click **Save**.

On some versions of Word you will see **File > Export > Create PDF/XPS** instead of Save As. Both paths produce the same result.

Word also lets you optimize the output. In the Save As dialog, a small "Options" button lets you choose between "Standard (online and printing)" and "Minimum size (online publishing)." The standard setting keeps image quality high. The minimum size setting compresses images in the PDF, which is useful if the resulting file is too large to email.

## Method 2: Google Docs (free, no desktop app needed)

Google Docs is the best option if you do not have Word installed, or if you are working on a Chromebook, iPad, or shared computer where you cannot install software. You will need a [free Google account](https://accounts.google.com/signup).

1. Go to [Google Drive](https://drive.google.com) and click **New > File upload** to add your .docx file.
2. Right-click the uploaded file and choose **Open with > Google Docs**.
3. Once the file opens, click **File > Download > PDF Document (.pdf)**.

Google Docs handles most Word documents well. Very complex formatting, such as custom fonts not available on Google's servers, may shift slightly. For standard documents the output is clean and the process is completely free.

## Method 3: LibreOffice (free, no upload, works offline)

[LibreOffice](https://www.libreoffice.org/) is a free, open-source office suite that reads .docx files and exports them to PDF without sending your file anywhere. This is the right option for sensitive documents, tax returns, medical forms, or anything you prefer not to upload to a cloud server.

1. Download and install LibreOffice from libreoffice.org (free, no sign-up).
2. Open your .docx file in LibreOffice Writer.
3. Click **File > Export as PDF**.
4. Leave the default settings and click **Export**.

LibreOffice produces faithful PDFs for most Word documents. Very advanced features like complex macros or embedded charts may need a quick visual check, but for the vast majority of documents the output is accurate.

## Method 4: Mac's Print to PDF (works from any app)

On a Mac, every application that can print can also save to PDF. You do not need Word, Google Docs, or LibreOffice at all. This method works whether the document is open in Pages, Word for Mac, or TextEdit.

1. Open your document in any app.
2. Press **Command + P** to open the Print dialog.
3. Click the **PDF** dropdown in the lower-left corner of the Print window.
4. Choose **Save as PDF**.

The PDF follows what your document looks like when printed, so check your page size and margins before saving. If the document was set up for A4 but your Mac defaults to Letter, the page layout will be off. Adjust the paper size in **File > Page Setup** first.

## What about online converters?

Online tools like Smallpdf, PDF24, and Adobe's web converter all convert Word to PDF, and they work fine for most files. The trade-off is that they work by uploading your document to their servers, converting it, and letting you download the result.

For a class assignment or a recipe, that is completely fine. For a contract, a tax return, or anything containing personal or financial data, check the tool's privacy policy before uploading. Adobe notes in their [privacy documentation](https://www.adobe.com/privacy/policy.html) that files are deleted after processing, but practices vary by service and it is worth verifying.

The four methods above all run locally and never transmit your file.

## What to do with the PDF after converting

A few things commonly come up once you have the PDF:

**Combine multiple files.** If you converted several Word documents separately and want one PDF, the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool combines them in your browser. It runs without uploads and handles files of any length. One thing to know: if any of your documents contained fillable form fields and you filled them in before converting, flatten the form first using our [Flatten PDF](/flatten-pdf) tool. Merging strips interactive fields, and a flattened form preserves the values as permanent page content.

**Add a password.** To lock the PDF before sending it, use [Protect PDF](/protect-pdf). The tool uses AES-256 encryption and runs in your browser, so the file never leaves your device.

**Compress before emailing.** If your PDF contains embedded photos or images and the file is too large to attach, [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) can reduce the size. A quick note on text documents: our compressor rasterizes pages to JPEG, which shrinks image-heavy files but makes text-only PDFs dramatically larger. A 23.5 KB text report grew to 1.4 MB in our testing. If your converted PDF is mostly text and under the [25 MB Gmail or Outlook attachment limit](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6584), you do not need to compress it.

The [PDF format (ISO 32000)](https://pdfa.org/resource/iso-32000/) is an open standard, which is why a PDF made in Word opens cleanly in Preview on a Mac, in Chrome's PDF viewer, in Adobe Reader, and in practically any other app without any compatibility issues.
