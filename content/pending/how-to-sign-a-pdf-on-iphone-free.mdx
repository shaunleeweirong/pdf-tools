You can sign a PDF on your iPhone for free right now, without downloading anything. Every iPhone already has a Markup tool built in that lets you draw your signature and place it on any page of a PDF. Here's exactly how to use it, what to do when it doesn't work, and when a browser-based alternative makes more sense.

## How to Sign a PDF on iPhone Using the Files App

The Files app is the quickest path for most people. No account, no install.

1. Open the **Files** app and locate your PDF.
2. Tap the PDF to open it.
3. Tap the **Markup** icon (a pencil tip in a circle) in the top-right corner.
4. Tap the **+** button at the bottom right, then choose **Signature**.
5. If you haven't set up a signature yet, tap **Add or Remove Signature**, draw your signature with your finger on the screen, then tap **Done**.
6. Your signature appears on the page. Drag it to the right spot and use the corner handles to resize it.
7. Tap **Done** to save the signed PDF.

That's it. The file saves back to the same location in Files, ready to share.

iOS saves every signature you draw, so you only need to do step 5 once. After that, tapping Signature shows your stored signatures and you just tap to insert.

## How to Sign a PDF That Came in an Email

If someone sent you a PDF to sign and return, you don't need to save it to Files first.

1. Open **Mail** and tap the PDF attachment.
2. Tap the **Markup** icon in the top-right corner of the preview.
3. Tap **+**, then **Signature**, and choose your saved signature (or draw a new one).
4. Drag the signature where it needs to go, then tap **Done**.
5. Tap the **Share** icon and choose **Mail** to reply with the signed file attached.

Mail uses the same Markup tool as Files, so the steps are identical once you're inside the document.

## What if the PDF Won't Open in Markup?

This is one of the most common frustrations. A few things can cause it:

**The file opened in a third-party reader.** If you have a PDF app installed, your iPhone may route PDFs to that app instead of Files. The fix: go to the Files app, long-press the PDF, and choose **Markup** directly from the action menu that appears. This bypasses whatever app would normally open the file.

**The PDF came from Mail and you tapped instead of saving.** Tap and hold the attachment in Mail, then choose **Save to Files**. Once it's in Files, open it from there and Markup will be available.

**The Markup icon is greyed out.** This usually means the PDF has editing restrictions. The owner locked it to prevent changes, and Markup treats that as a restriction on annotations too. You'd need to remove those restrictions to proceed. The [remove-password tool](/unlock-pdf) can clear it if you know the password.

## How to Save Multiple Signatures for Reuse

You can store several signatures in iOS, which is useful if you sign both with your full name and with initials.

1. Open any PDF in Markup.
2. Tap **+**, then **Signature**.
3. Tap **Add or Remove Signature**.
4. Tap the **+** in the top-right corner to draw and save another one.
5. Tap **Done** when you're finished.

Each stored signature shows up as a thumbnail. Tap the one you want to insert.

## Signing a PDF in Your Browser on iPhone

If you want a fully browser-based option with no app install at all, the [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) works in Safari on your iPhone. Open the page, upload your PDF, draw your signature on the touch screen, then download the signed file. Nothing gets uploaded to a server.

One thing to know before you use it: the tool places your signature on the first page of the document. If you need to sign a specific page that's not page one, Apple Markup is the better choice since you can scroll to any page and place the signature there.

## What About Filling Out Form Fields Before Signing?

A lot of documents people need to sign are also forms, like employment agreements, rental applications, or contractor contracts. There's a difference between filling in form fields and placing a signature on top of the page.

Markup handles it by letting you add text boxes anywhere. Tap **+** in Markup, choose **Text**, tap where you want to type, and type your answer. This works, but it's laying text on top of the page rather than filling an actual form field.

If the PDF has real interactive form fields (where a cursor appears when you tap), the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) can detect and fill those properly. After filling, you'd then open the saved file in Markup to add your signature.

## Is an iPhone Signature Legally Valid?

For most everyday documents, yes. [Electronic signatures are recognized under the U.S. ESIGN Act and UETA](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/electronic-signatures-global-national-commerce-act), which treat a drawn digital signature the same as ink for contracts, agreements, and most other documents.

A few exceptions exist. Some contexts, like wills, adoption papers, or court filings, require a notarized or witnessed signature. High-value financial or real-estate transactions may require a qualified electronic signature standard, such as those defined in the [EU's eIDAS regulation](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eidas-regulation). If you're unsure, check with the party receiving the document.

For everyday use, though, your finger-drawn signature in iOS Markup is perfectly sufficient and widely accepted.
