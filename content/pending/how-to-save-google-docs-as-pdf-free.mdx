Saving a Google Doc as a PDF takes three clicks on a laptop and about five taps on your phone. The export is built into Google Docs, it's free, and you don't need Acrobat or any third-party app to get it done.

## How to save a Google Doc as a PDF on desktop

This is the quickest method for Windows and Mac.

1. Open the document in [Google Docs](https://docs.google.com).
2. Click **File** in the top menu.
3. Hover over **Download**.
4. Click **PDF Document (.pdf)**.

The file downloads to your default downloads folder. That's it. Fonts, images, headings, and page breaks all transfer correctly for documents that use standard Google Fonts.

### The print-to-PDF method (more control)

If you need to adjust paper size, margins, or scaling before you export, use the print dialog instead.

1. Click **File** then **Print** (or press Ctrl+P on Windows, Cmd+P on Mac).
2. In the Destination field, choose **Save as PDF**.
3. Click **Save**.

This approach is useful if your document is formatted for a non-standard page size or if you want to check how the layout will look on a specific paper dimension before committing.

## How to save a Google Doc as a PDF on Android

1. Open the Google Docs app and tap the document.
2. Tap the **three-dot menu** (the icon at the top right).
3. Tap **Share & export**.
4. Tap **Save as** and choose **PDF Document**.
5. Tap **OK** to confirm, then choose where to save the file.

The PDF lands in the location you picked, ready to share via email, WhatsApp, or any other app.

## How to save a Google Doc as a PDF on iPhone or iPad

1. Open the document in the Google Docs app.
2. Tap the **More** icon (three horizontal dots, top right).
3. Tap **Share and export**.
4. Tap **Send a copy**.
5. Select **PDF** as the format, then tap **OK**.
6. Choose how you want to send or save it (Mail, Files, AirDrop, etc.).

This routes through iOS's share sheet, so you can save directly to Files, iCloud Drive, or share to another app in one step.

## Why does the PDF look different from the original?

The most common reason is font substitution. If your document uses a custom or system font that isn't embedded in the PDF, viewers on other devices substitute a fallback font. Different fonts have different character widths, so a line that fit perfectly on your screen wraps onto a new line in someone else's viewer.

**How to avoid it:** Use a [Google Font](https://fonts.google.com) (such as Roboto, Open Sans, Lato, or Merriweather). These are embedded correctly during export. If you need a specific custom font for brand reasons, test the export before you share it.

A second cause is tab stops and manually sized text boxes. Google Docs and PDFs handle these differently. Converting your formatting to standard paragraph styles (Heading 1, Heading 2, Normal Text) before exporting usually produces a cleaner result.

## Why is my Google Docs PDF so large?

Two things add the most to file size:

- **Embedded images.** Every image in your document is included at export quality. If you pasted screenshots or high-resolution photos, those inflate the file.
- **Embedded fonts.** Fonts are bundled into the PDF so they display correctly on any device.

If your exported PDF has a lot of photos and images and is too large to attach to an email (most providers cap attachments at [25 MB for Gmail and Outlook](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6584)), our [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) can reduce the size significantly for image-heavy exports. An image deck that was 913 KB came down to 315 KB at Medium quality.

One important note: compression works by rasterizing each page. That process helps when pages are photographs, but it makes text-heavy documents much larger, not smaller. If your Google Doc is mostly text, compression is the wrong tool. In that case, check whether you have large images or logos embedded in the document and remove or resize them before exporting.

## How to combine your Google Doc PDF with other files

Once you've exported, you might want to merge it with other PDFs. Say you're assembling a job application from a cover letter (Google Doc), a resume (PDF), and a portfolio (PDF). Our [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) handles this in a few clicks, in your browser, with no upload to a server.

Merging doesn't inflate the combined file beyond the sum of the individual parts. Three files totaling 971.9 KB merged to 970.5 KB in our tests. The one thing to know: if any of the files has interactive form fields, those fields are stripped during merging. If you filled out a form, [flatten it first](/flatten-pdf) so the values are baked into the page and won't disappear.

## How to save just certain pages as a PDF

Google Docs exports the whole document. If you only need a page range, export the full file first, then use the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) to extract the pages you want into a separate file. You can pick a range (say, pages 3 to 7) and download just those pages.

## Quick checklist before you export

- Use a Google Font to avoid font substitution.
- Check **File > Page setup** to confirm the paper size and margins look right.
- Remove or resize large embedded images if you need a smaller file.
- If someone else will fill out a form embedded in your document, export it before they fill it in, or flatten the filled form before merging with other PDFs.

With these steps in place, your exported PDF will match what you see on screen and arrive at its destination ready to be opened, printed, or submitted.
