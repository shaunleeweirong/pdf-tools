Teachers deal with [PDFs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) all day. Lesson plans from one site, worksheets from another, forms from the district office, and somehow they all need to live in one tidy document before class. The best free PDF tools for teachers let you handle all of that in a browser, with no account, no app to install, and no files leaving your device.

Here is what actually works, and what to watch out for.

## What PDF tasks come up most in the classroom?

The most common classroom PDF jobs fall into a few categories:

- **Combining files.** Merging a reading passage, a comprehension worksheet, and a rubric into a single handout students can download once.
- **Pulling pages out of larger documents.** Extracting a chapter from a 200-page textbook PDF so you only share the relevant section.
- **Shrinking large files.** Getting a lesson deck small enough to upload to Google Classroom or email home to parents.
- **Adding page numbers.** Making multi-page packets easier to follow.
- **Locking answer keys.** Password-protecting a teacher edition so students can't open it.
- **Scanning to PDF.** Turning photos of whiteboard notes or hand-drawn diagrams into a proper PDF.
- **Flattening forms.** Baking in filled-in responses so they can't be edited after submission.

You can do all of these in a browser right now, with no sign-up.

## Merge worksheets and handouts into one file

The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) combines any number of PDF files into one document. Drag in your reading passage, your worksheet, and your rubric, reorder them if needed, and download a single file.

One thing worth knowing: merging doesn't inflate the file size. In testing, three files totalling 971.9 KB produced a merged output of 970.5 KB. You won't push a file over an upload limit just by combining it with other documents.

There is one thing to watch: if you merge a PDF that contains interactive form fields (a fillable attendance sheet, for example), those fields disappear after the merge. If you need to lock in the form data first, run the filled form through the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) before merging. That bakes the typed values into the page so they can't be lost.

## Extract pages from larger documents

Textbooks, resource packs, and district-issued PDFs are often hundreds of pages long. The [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) lets you define page ranges to extract (pages 12 to 18, for example). The [Extract Pages tool](/extract-pages) gives you a thumbnail view so you can pick individual pages by clicking.

If the order of pages in a packet isn't quite right, the [Organize PDF tool](/organize-pdf) lets you drag pages into a different order before saving. To remove pages you don't want, the [Delete Pages tool](/delete-pages) works the same way.

## Compress image-heavy lesson decks (but not text PDFs)

This one comes with an important warning: the compressor only helps certain types of PDFs.

The [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) rasterizes every page to JPEG before saving. That's useful if your PDF is full of images. A photo-heavy image deck went from 913.4 KB down to 315.3 KB at the Medium setting in our tests, a 65% reduction. But it makes text-based PDFs much larger. A 20-page text report grew from 23.5 KB to 1.4 MB when compressed.

The same applies to contracts, invoices, lesson plans typed in Word, and most forms. The compressor works against you on those.

Use the compressor on scanned page images, photo decks, or PDF slide shows where the content is already rasterized. Don't use it on typed documents. If a typed PDF is too large for an upload, the likely culprit is embedded fonts or a large logo. Split it into smaller parts or zip it instead.

One more note: running compression twice doesn't help. A second pass at the same level actually makes the file bigger, not smaller. Pick the right setting the first time, and if Medium isn't enough, start again from the original at High.

## Add page numbers and watermarks to handouts

Two small tools that save a lot of student confusion:

The [Add Page Numbers tool](/add-page-numbers) inserts page numbers into any PDF. You can choose the position (top or bottom, left, center, or right) and adjust the starting number if you're working with part of a larger packet.

The [Watermark PDF tool](/watermark-pdf) stamps text across every page. Teachers use this to mark drafts ("DRAFT", "DO NOT DISTRIBUTE"), add a class period label, or brand materials with a school name. It takes about ten seconds and works on any PDF.

## Lock answer keys with a password

The [Protect PDF tool](/protect-pdf) adds an [AES-256](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Encryption_Standard) password to any PDF. Send the teacher edition to colleagues and they can open it; a student who gets hold of the file sees a password prompt instead.

Password protection adds almost no file size overhead (around 1 KB), so a protected file is essentially the same size as the original.

## Convert scanned worksheets and whiteboard photos to PDF

If you've taken photos of whiteboard notes, student work, or hand-drawn diagrams, the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) turns them into a proper PDF. Upload your JPGs or PNGs in the order you want, and download a single PDF with one image per page.

This also works well for multi-page scans from a phone camera or a department scanner. PDF is a reliable format for school distribution because it looks the same on every device, whether a student opens it on a Chromebook, a phone, or a shared classroom tablet.

## How to build a custom worksheet packet in under two minutes

Here is a step-by-step workflow teachers use to put together a packet from scratch:

1. Open the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) and extract just the pages you need from any large source document.
2. Gather the extracted PDF along with any other files you want to include.
3. Open [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf), drag all the files in, and reorder the pages.
4. Open [Add Page Numbers](/add-page-numbers) and number the packet from page 1.
5. Download the finished packet and upload it directly to your LMS or share it by email.

The whole process takes a couple of minutes and produces a clean, consistently formatted file.

## Reorder and reorganize pages

The [Organize PDF tool](/organize-pdf) shows every page as a thumbnail. Drag pages into a different order, delete the ones you don't need, and save. It's the fastest way to build a custom packet from an existing resource without printing and rescanning anything.

If you just need to remove a few pages from a longer document, [Delete Pages](/delete-pages) is more direct: pick the page numbers you want gone and download the cleaned-up file.

All of these tools run in your browser with no account required. Files are processed locally, so student documents, exam papers, and school records don't pass through a third-party server.
