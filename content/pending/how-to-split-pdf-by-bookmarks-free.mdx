Splitting a PDF by bookmarks turns a long document into separate chapter files automatically, without counting a single page number. If your PDF has a built-in outline or table of contents, free online tools can handle this in under a minute.

## What "split by bookmarks" actually means

PDF bookmarks are not the yellow sticky-note kind. They are navigation anchors embedded in the [PDF document structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF#Outline), also called the document outline. When you open a PDF and see a sidebar listing chapters you can click on, those are bookmarks.

A bookmark-aware splitter reads that list and cuts the PDF at each bookmark's start page, producing one file per section. You end up with one file per chapter, named (in some tools) after the bookmark text itself.

## How to check if your PDF has bookmarks

Open the file in Chrome. Press `Ctrl+Shift+H` on Windows or `Cmd+Option+B` on Mac to open the bookmarks panel. In Adobe Reader, click the bookmark icon in the left sidebar.

If the panel shows a list of named sections, you're good. If it's empty, the PDF has no embedded outline, and you'll need the page-range approach described later in this post.

## Three free tools that split PDFs by bookmarks

### Sejda

Sejda's split-by-outline tool is the most polished free option. Upload your PDF and it reads the outline, then lists the sections it found. You can split at the top level only or include sub-levels. Files download in a ZIP archive, named after the bookmark text.

Free plan limits: files up to 50 MB and a maximum of 200 pages. Most reports and ebooks fit within these limits.

### DeftPDF

DeftPDF shows you the bookmark tree before splitting, so you can confirm the structure looks right before committing. No watermarks, no subscription required. Good choice when you want to verify the outline first.

### i2PDF

i2PDF's bookmark splitter handles larger files and produces clean output. Upload, review the bookmarks it detected, and download the ZIP. No account needed.

## Step-by-step: how to split a PDF by bookmarks

1. Check that your PDF has bookmarks (open it in Chrome and press `Ctrl+Shift+H` on Windows or `Cmd+Option+B` on Mac).
2. Go to Sejda, DeftPDF, or i2PDF in your browser.
3. Upload your PDF.
4. Review the bookmark list the tool displays and confirm the sections are correct.
5. Choose whether to split at the top level only or include nested bookmarks.
6. Click Split and wait a few seconds.
7. Download the ZIP file containing your individual section PDFs.

## What to do if your PDF has no bookmarks

Many scanned PDFs and older documents have no embedded outline. Bookmark-based splitting won't work because there is nothing for the tool to read.

The reliable fallback is splitting by page range. Find the chapter start and end pages from the PDF's printed table of contents (if it has one on the page), then use our [Split PDF](/split-pdf) tool to extract each range. You specify the pages you want and the tool saves them as a new PDF. It takes a few more steps, but works on any PDF.

## A note on nested bookmarks

Most free tools only split at the top level of the bookmark tree. If your PDF has a chapter structure like "Chapter 1 > Section 1.1 > Section 1.2", the tool puts all of Chapter 1 (including 1.1 and 1.2) into one file.

If you need finer cuts, check whether the tool explicitly supports sub-level splitting. DeftPDF gives you this option. For everything else, extract sections by page range once you have the top-level chapters separated.

## Why some PDFs have bookmarks and others don't

Bookmarks are added during the authoring process, typically by the tool that created the PDF. Word, InDesign, and LaTeX can all embed a document outline automatically when you export to PDF, provided you've used proper heading styles or structured the document correctly.

Scanned PDFs have no structure at all. They are essentially photographs of pages. PDFs created by printing to a PDF printer from unstructured documents also tend to have no outline.

The [ISO 32000 standard](https://www.iso.org/standard/75839.html) that defines the PDF format includes a full specification for the document outline, but whether a given PDF actually uses it comes down to how the file was made. If you receive a PDF without bookmarks but want to add them before splitting, Adobe Acrobat Pro and some desktop tools like PDFsam can do this, though it is a manual process.
