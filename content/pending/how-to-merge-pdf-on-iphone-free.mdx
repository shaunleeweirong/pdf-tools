You can merge PDF files on iPhone for free without installing anything extra. iOS includes two built-in ways to do it, and a browser tool in Safari adds a third option with more control over file order. Here's how each method works.

## Method 1: Files App (built-in, no download needed)

The Files app has a Create PDF option that combines multiple PDFs into a single file. It's part of iOS 15 and later, so if you've updated your phone in the last few years you already have it. [Apple's overview of the Files app](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105080) documents what else it can do.

1. Open the **Files** app and go to the folder where your PDFs are stored.
2. Tap the **More** button (three dots, top right) and choose **Select**.
3. Tap each PDF you want to combine.
4. Tap **More** at the bottom right of the screen.
5. Choose **Create PDF**.

The merged file appears in the same folder.

**Important:** The Files app combines PDFs in alphabetical or numerical order based on the file name. If the order matters to you, rename the files before you start. Something like `01-cover-letter.pdf`, `02-resume.pdf`, `03-references.pdf` puts them in exactly the right sequence. Once you've done that, the Files approach is genuinely fast.

## Method 2: Shortcuts App (custom order, still no third-party app)

The Shortcuts app is also built into iOS, and it has an action called Combine PDFs that lets you tap files in whatever order you want them merged. [Apple's Shortcuts user guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/welcome/ios) explains how actions work if you'd like to build on this further.

1. Open the **Shortcuts** app.
2. Tap **+** to create a new shortcut.
3. Search for **Combine PDFs** and add the action.
4. Tap the action and set it to receive input from **Ask Each Time**.
5. Save the shortcut and give it a name you'll recognize.

To use it: tap the shortcut, then select your PDFs from the Files app in the order you want them joined. The result lands as a new PDF wherever you point it.

You can also search the Shortcuts Gallery inside the app for pre-built merge shortcuts that others have shared, which skips the setup entirely. Either way, once the shortcut exists you can run it from your Home Screen without opening any app.

This method takes a few minutes to configure, but if you merge PDFs regularly it's worth the effort.

## Method 3: Merge PDF in Safari (no install, drag to reorder)

If you'd rather skip the naming workaround and the shortcut setup, a browser-based [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool is the most direct path. It works in Safari on any iPhone and gives you a drag-to-reorder interface before you combine.

1. Open **Safari** and go to the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool.
2. Tap **Select files** and choose your PDFs from Files, Photos, or iCloud Drive.
3. Drag to reorder them if you need a specific sequence.
4. Tap **Merge** and download the combined PDF.

Your files are processed in the browser on your iPhone and never sent to a server, so nothing leaves your device. No account needed, no watermark.

This is the right choice when you want page-order control without renaming files or building a Shortcut. It also works on borrowed phones, school iPads, or any device where you can't install apps.

## Will merging PDFs on iPhone make the file bigger?

The combined file comes out at roughly the sum of the inputs and sometimes a hair under. We measured our merge tool on three PDFs totalling 971.9 KB: the output was 970.5 KB, slightly smaller because document structure shared across the files is written once rather than repeated.

If the merged result is still too big for email or an upload limit, the fix depends on what the PDF contains. For **scans and image-heavy documents**, the [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) tool can cut the size substantially. For **typed text documents or forms**, don't use it: that tool rasterizes pages into images, and a text PDF can grow to many times its original size as a result. Send fewer pages, or split the document with [Split PDF](/split-pdf) instead.

## What happens to form fields after merging?

Merging removes interactive form fields from the output. If you've filled in a PDF form and want to combine it with other files, use the [Flatten PDF](/flatten-pdf) tool first. Flattening bakes the filled values into the page as regular content, so nothing is lost when you merge.

## Can I merge PDFs from my email or cloud storage on iPhone?

Yes. When the file picker opens in any of the three methods above, you can pull PDFs from iCloud Drive, Google Drive, Dropbox, or any app that integrates with the iOS Files picker. For PDFs received by email, save them to Files first by long-pressing the attachment in Mail and choosing **Save to Files**, then pick them up from there.

## Can I combine more than two PDFs at once?

All three methods handle as many files as you have. Select everything at once rather than merging in stages. Two passes are not necessary, and running an already-merged PDF back through [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) is harmless but adds an extra step with no benefit.
