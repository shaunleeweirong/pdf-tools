Converting an Apple Pages document to PDF is free and built right into Pages, whether you're on a Mac, iPhone, or iPad. No third-party software needed. If you're on Windows and don't have access to a Mac, there are two solid free options, including one that works offline.

## How to export Pages to PDF on Mac

This is the fastest method if you have Pages installed.

1. Open your document in Pages.
2. Choose **File** from the menu bar, then **Export To**, then **PDF**.
3. In the dialog that appears, you can choose image quality for any photos or graphics in the document. For most documents, the default setting is fine.
4. If you want to include comments or annotations, tick the option before you export.
5. Click **Next**, choose where to save the file, and click **Export**.

That's it. The PDF opens in Preview immediately. [Apple documents these steps in full](https://support.apple.com/guide/pages/export-to-word-pdf-or-another-file-format-tance1161f26/mac) if you want the official reference.

You can also use **Print to PDF** as a fallback: File > Print, then click the PDF dropdown in the bottom-left corner and choose "Save as PDF." The result is nearly identical, though the Export path gives you the image-quality option.

## How to convert Pages to PDF on iPhone or iPad

The Pages app on iOS and iPadOS has the same export capability.

1. Open the document in Pages.
2. Tap the **three-dot menu** (More) in the top-right corner.
3. Tap **Export**.
4. Tap **PDF**.
5. Choose **Save to Files** to keep a copy locally, or tap a share option to send it directly.

On iPad the layout is slightly different: look for the wrench icon or the three-dot menu depending on your version, then follow the same Export path.

## How to convert Pages to PDF on Windows (iCloud.com)

Pages is Mac-only software, but Apple provides a free browser-based version at [iCloud.com](https://www.icloud.com). You need a free Apple ID, which takes about two minutes to create.

1. Go to iCloud.com and sign in.
2. Open Pages from the app grid.
3. Click the **upload icon** to import your .pages file.
4. Once the file opens, click the **three-dot menu** in the top toolbar.
5. Choose **Download a Copy**, then select **PDF**.

The file downloads to your computer. No Mac needed, no paid software.

## The ZIP trick: get a PDF without converting at all

Here's something most guides skip. Every .pages file is actually a ZIP archive. Inside it, Apple stores a PDF preview called `Preview.pdf` in a folder named `QuickLook`. That file was created automatically the last time you saved the document in Pages.

To extract it on Windows (or Mac, without opening Pages):

1. Make a copy of your .pages file first.
2. Rename the copy so its extension changes from `.pages` to `.zip`.
3. Unzip the file (right-click > Extract All on Windows, double-click on Mac).
4. Inside the extracted folder, open the **QuickLook** folder.
5. Open **Preview.pdf**.

Two caveats: the preview is generated when Pages saves, so very recent unsaved changes might not appear. And on older documents or those saved without the preview option, the QuickLook folder may be empty. If that happens, use the iCloud.com method instead.

This trick is particularly useful if you're handling a sensitive document and don't want to upload it to any service.

## What about fonts, images, and layout?

Apple's export preserves the document's appearance. Fonts are embedded in the PDF, images are included at the quality you selected, and page dimensions match what you set in Pages. If you used a custom font that isn't on the system, Pages will substitute or embed it, so the recipient sees the intended look regardless of what fonts they have installed.

[The PDF format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) was designed specifically for this: a self-contained file that looks the same everywhere. Pages export takes full advantage of that.

One thing to watch: if your Pages document has interactive elements, like clickable links or forms, those come through as active links in the PDF. But any Pages-specific features, like animated text or objects, won't survive the conversion. PDFs are static by design.

## What to do with your PDF next

Once you have the PDF, a few common next steps:

**If it's too large to email:** Our [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) reduces file size in your browser. It works best on image-heavy PDFs, which is exactly what Pages exports with lots of photos tend to produce. Note that purely text-based PDFs don't compress well with a rasterizing compressor, so if your Pages doc is text-only, splitting it or zipping it is a better approach.

**If you need to combine it with another document:** The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) lets you drag in multiple files and combine them in any order. Useful when you're submitting a Pages-written report alongside a separate cover sheet or reference document.

**If you need to split out specific pages:** The [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) lets you pull out a range of pages as a separate file.

All three tools run in your browser. Nothing is uploaded to a server.

## Quick comparison of methods

| Situation | Best method |
|---|---|
| You have a Mac with Pages | File > Export To > PDF |
| You have an iPhone or iPad | Pages app > Export > PDF |
| You're on Windows, have an Apple ID | iCloud.com > Download a Copy > PDF |
| You're on Windows, no Apple ID, document was recently saved | Rename .pages to .zip, extract Preview.pdf |
| You need to share the document with someone on Windows | Send the PDF, not the .pages file |

The built-in export is the most reliable path. Use iCloud.com or the ZIP trick when you don't have a Mac handy.
