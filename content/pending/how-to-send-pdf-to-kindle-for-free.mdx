You can send a PDF to Kindle for free in three ways: email it to your Kindle address, upload it through Amazon's website or desktop app, or drag it onto the device via USB. Each method is free. None requires a subscription.

## Method 1: Email the PDF to your Kindle address

Every Kindle device and the Kindle app on your phone or tablet has a unique `@kindle.com` email address. Send a PDF to that address and it appears in your library within a few minutes over Wi-Fi.

**Step 1: Find your Kindle address**

1. Sign in to Amazon.
2. Click Account and Lists > Your Devices and Content > Manage Devices.
3. Select your Kindle from the device list.
4. The Send to Kindle email address appears in the device summary.

**Step 2: Approve your sending address**

Amazon only delivers documents from approved senders. On the same Devices page, find Personal Document Settings and add the email address you plan to send from. Skip this and your message will be silently discarded.

**Step 3: Send the PDF**

Attach the PDF to a new email and send it to your Kindle address. Leave the subject blank to keep the original PDF layout. Type **Convert** in the subject line if you want Amazon to try reflowing the text for smaller screens. Conversion works only on text-based PDFs, and results vary; scanned pages and image-heavy documents will not reflow.

Your file shows up on the Kindle or app the next time it connects to Wi-Fi. You can check delivery status at Amazon's Manage Your Content and Devices page.

**Size to watch for:** Email attachment limits depend on your provider. [Gmail caps outgoing attachments at 25 MB](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6584), and Outlook.com enforces a similar cap. For larger PDFs, use Method 2 instead.

## Method 2: Use the Send to Kindle web uploader or desktop app

Amazon's Send to Kindle web tool handles files up to 200 MB, which covers the vast majority of PDFs. This is the simplest option for larger files.

1. Go to [amazon.com/sendtokindle](https://www.amazon.com/sendtokindle) and sign in.
2. Drag your PDF onto the page, or click to browse for the file.
3. Choose which Kindle devices or apps should receive it.
4. Click Send.

The free Send to Kindle desktop app for Mac and Windows does the same thing through a right-click menu, without needing a browser tab open.

According to [Amazon's help documentation](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=G5WYD9SAF7PGXRNA), supported formats include PDF, EPUB, DOC, DOCX, TXT, RTF, HTML, JPEG, and PNG.

## Method 3: Transfer via USB cable

No Wi-Fi? USB is the fallback. It works with files of any size and requires no internet connection at all.

1. Connect your Kindle to your computer with its USB cable.
2. On Windows, open File Explorer and look for your Kindle under "This PC." On a Mac, it shows up in Finder as a connected drive.
3. Open the **documents** folder on the Kindle drive.
4. Drag and drop your PDF files into that folder.
5. Eject the Kindle safely before unplugging.

The PDF appears in your Kindle library the next time you wake the device. This method is also useful when your Kindle's Wi-Fi isn't set up yet, or when you're transferring a large personal library all at once.

## Using the Kindle app on your phone or tablet

You don't need a physical Kindle to use any of this. The free Kindle app runs on iPhone, iPad, Android, Mac, Windows, and in a browser. Email a PDF to your Kindle address and it syncs to all your signed-in devices.

Sent documents show up in a "Docs" section of your library, separate from purchased books. If you sent a file and can't find it, check the Docs tab rather than the main library view.

## What to do when the PDF is too large

The 200 MB web uploader cap covers most files. But scanned textbooks and multi-chapter research papers can run larger. There are two practical options.

**Option 1: Compress the file.** If your PDF is image-heavy (scans, photo pages, or illustrated slides), compressing it often cuts the size substantially. Our in-browser [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) rasterizes each page to JPEG, which works well on those file types. One important caveat: this tool makes text-only PDFs larger, not smaller, because it converts crisp vector text into a photograph of that text. Only use it on PDFs that are already mostly images.

**Option 2: Send only the pages you need.** If you're reading a long academic PDF but only need one chapter, use the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) to extract those pages first, then send the smaller portion. A 50-page chapter from a 400-page book is much easier to transfer and to read on a Kindle screen.

## Why PDFs sometimes look awkward on Kindle

PDF is a [fixed-format standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF), meaning the exact layout, font sizes, and spacing are locked in. On a Kindle's smaller screen, text can appear tiny and require zooming and panning to read.

The "Convert" subject line asks Amazon to extract the underlying text and reflow it into Kindle's flexible format. This can make reading much more comfortable for straightforward text documents. It does nothing on scanned PDFs because there is no selectable text to extract; the whole page is an image.

If you find yourself reading a lot of PDFs on a small Kindle screen, consider EPUB as an alternative format. Many libraries and sites distribute the same books in both formats, and EPUB reflows automatically to any screen size.

For everything else, the three methods above get any PDF onto any Kindle device or app without spending anything.
