You can delete pages from a PDF on iPhone for free right now, using a method already on your device. No app download required. The quickest option for most people is the built-in Files app, which has had PDF page deletion since iOS 15.

Below are three approaches ranked by how many pages you need to remove and what you already have installed.

## Use the Files App (Built In, No Download)

Every iPhone running [iOS 15 or later](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101662) has PDF page deletion inside the Files app. It's not obvious, but it's there.

1. Open the **Files** app on your iPhone.
2. Find your PDF and tap to open it.
3. Tap the **thumbnail grid** icon at the top of the screen (four small squares arranged in a grid).
4. Long-press the page you want to remove. A context menu appears.
5. Tap **Delete Page**.
6. Repeat for any other pages, then tap **Done**.

The change saves automatically to the original file. There's no confirmation dialog and no undo, so check that you've got the right page before you tap.

The limitation here is that you can only remove one page per long-press. If you have five pages to cut, that's five separate actions. For anything more than two or three, the next method is faster.

## Remove Several Pages at Once with a Browser Tool

The [Delete Pages tool](/delete-pages) runs in Safari or Chrome on your iPhone. It's free, requires no account, and your file never leaves your device. The processing happens inside your browser tab, not on a server.

1. Open Safari (or Chrome) on your iPhone.
2. Go to the **Delete Pages** tool on this site.
3. Tap **Choose file** and pick your PDF from Files, iCloud, or wherever you store it.
4. Page thumbnails load in your browser. Tap each page you want to remove to mark it.
5. Tap **Delete selected pages** and save the result.

This works well for longer documents. A 20-page report that needs six pages removed takes roughly the same effort as removing one, because you select them all before hitting delete once.

Because nothing is uploaded, this method also works fine when you're on a slow connection or when the PDF contains sensitive information (contracts, financial records, medical documents) that you'd rather not send anywhere.

## Keep Specific Pages with the Print Trick

This one works differently. Instead of choosing which pages to delete, you choose which ones to keep. It's useful when you want to preserve a short section of a long document.

1. Open the PDF in **Files** and tap the **Share** icon (a box with an upward arrow).
2. Choose **Print**.
3. In the print preview, use a pinch-zoom gesture on the page thumbnails. This expands them into a full-page-by-page view.
4. Deselect the pages you want to remove by tapping the blue checkmark on each.
5. Tap the **Share** icon again (now inside the print dialog) and choose **Save to Files**.

This saves a new PDF with only the pages you kept. Your original file is unchanged.

## What About Dedicated iOS Apps?

Apps like [PDFGear](https://www.pdfgear.com/) offer page deletion alongside other features: annotation, form filling, signing. They're free to download from the App Store and work well. The trade-off is that they require an install and sometimes ask for an account to access all features.

If page deletion is the only thing you need, the Files app and the browser tool cover it without any of that overhead.

## A Note on What Stays Intact

When you delete a page, the content on every other page stays exactly as it was. Fonts, images, form fields, and formatting are untouched. One thing that won't update automatically: page numbers baked into the document design. If the PDF was laid out with "Page 3 of 12" as static text on each page, that text won't recalculate after deletion. You'll see a gap in the numbered sequence. That's a limitation of how the original PDF was built, not a problem with the deletion itself.

For most documents (reports, contracts, presentations, scanned pages) this isn't an issue. For documents where the page numbers matter, note it before sharing the result.

## After You Delete

Open the file in Files and scroll through to confirm every remaining page looks right. If you used the browser tool, the downloaded file is a standard [PDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) that opens in any reader.

If something looks off, the original is still where you left it (Files app changes are in place, but if you used the Print trick or browser tool, your source file was never modified). Start over from the original if you need to.

For anything beyond page deletion (merging the trimmed file with another document, compressing a large scan, or converting to another format), the other tools on this site handle those steps in your browser the same way, free and without sign-up.
