Flattening a PDF on Android takes under a minute and needs no app. Open Chrome, load the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf), pick your file, and download a locked copy. Every form field, annotation, and signature gets baked into the page permanently.

## What flattening does to a PDF

A PDF can carry two kinds of content. The base layer holds what you see: text, images, and the page layout. On top of that sit interactive layers: form fields, text annotations, comments, and digital signature widgets. These interactive elements are separate objects a reader can select, fill in, or clear.

Flattening collapses those layers into the base layer. The result looks identical to the original, but nothing is a separate editable element anymore. No one opening the file later can alter your signature, change your answers, or remove your highlights.

[PDF/A, the ISO archiving standard for long-term document preservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF/A), requires flattening because a document without interactive layers is guaranteed to look the same in any software, on any device, now or decades from now. That same guarantee is why courts, government agencies, and HR systems often require flat PDFs for submissions.

## How to flatten a PDF on Android using Chrome

You don't need to install anything. Chrome on Android handles browser-based PDF tools the same way a desktop browser does. Here's the process:

1. Open **Chrome** on your Android phone or tablet.
2. Go to the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf).
3. Tap **Choose file** and locate your PDF. If the file is in Google Drive, tap the hamburger menu in the file picker and select "Google Drive."
4. Wait a moment while the tool processes your file.
5. Tap **Download** to save the flattened copy to your Downloads folder.

If you want to open the result right away, tap the download notification that appears at the bottom of Chrome, or find the file in the Files or Downloads app.

One thing to do before you start: save a copy of the original somewhere safe. Flattening is permanent. The interactive fields are gone from the output, and there's no way to get them back from the flattened version.

## Why "Save as PDF" on Android is not the same as flattening

A common workaround is printing a PDF to itself: open the file in a viewer, tap the share or print icon, and choose "Save as PDF" from the printer menu. That produces a new file with no interactive fields, so it removes the form layer.

The problem is that Android's print-to-PDF path rasterizes the document, converting every page to an image. After that:

- Text is no longer selectable or searchable.
- Any text that was crisp vector type becomes slightly blurry at smaller sizes (it's now a JPEG of text, not real text).
- File sizes can jump significantly, because images take much more space than vector text.

A dedicated flatten tool targets only the interactive layer and leaves everything else exactly as it was. The text stays as text. Use the print workaround only if a browser-based tool isn't an option.

## Flatten before merging, or lose your fill data

This catches a lot of people by surprise. You fill out a PDF form on your Android device (a job application, a rental agreement, a medical intake form), then merge it with a cover letter. You open the merged PDF and the form fields are blank.

That happens because merging copies page content, not the [interactive AcroForm layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF#Interactive_forms). The fill values live in that layer, and they get dropped in the process. This is true for most merge tools, not just ours.

The fix takes one extra step:

1. Fill your form completely and check it looks right.
2. Open the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) and flatten the filled form.
3. Merge the flattened copy with your other documents using the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf).

Flattening at step 2 bakes your answers into the page content. They survive the merge with no issues.

## When flattening a PDF is the right move

A few common situations where flattening before you send or share is the right call:

- **Submitting a filled application.** HR portals, visa platforms, and scholarship systems often require flat PDFs to prevent editing after submission.
- **Sending a signed contract.** A signature baked into the page is harder to dispute than an interactive signature widget a recipient could theoretically clear and re-sign.
- **Archiving a completed form.** Flat PDFs are safer for long-term storage because they don't depend on the reader app supporting interactive features.
- **Sending to a print shop.** Commercial printers expect flat files. An unflatened form can print with blank fields because the shop's RIP software ignores interactive layers.
- **Uploading to a platform with a file-size limit.** If the form layer is adding extra weight, flattening trims it a little, then you can compress separately if needed.

## What about file size after flattening?

Flattening removes interactive field data, so many PDFs end up slightly smaller afterward. Simple documents may stay about the same. Neither change is dramatic, because flattening touches only the form layer, not images or embedded fonts.

If the file is still too large after flattening, run it through the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) as a second step. Keep in mind that compression works best on image-heavy files and can make a text-only document larger, so test it first if your PDF is mostly text.

- Merging with another document? Flatten the form before you merge.

- Keep the original. Flattening is one-way.
