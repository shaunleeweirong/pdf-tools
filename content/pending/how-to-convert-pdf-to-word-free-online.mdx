You can convert a PDF to Word for free using Microsoft Word, Google Docs, or a free online converter. Each method takes under a minute for a typical document. The right pick depends on what software you have and whether your PDF contains real text or scanned images.

## Method 1: Open the PDF directly in Microsoft Word

If you already have [Microsoft Word on your desktop](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/edit-pdf-content-in-word-b2d1d729-6b79-499a-bcdb-233379ca5e04), you don't need any extra tool. Word has been able to open PDFs as editable documents since Word 2013.

1. Open Microsoft Word.
2. Go to **File > Open** and browse to your PDF.
3. Click **Open**. Word shows a message saying it will convert the file.
4. Click **OK**. The document opens as a .docx you can edit and save.

This is the most convenient method if you already have Word installed. It handles simple, text-based PDFs well. Where it falls short is with scanned PDFs (they appear as images instead of text) and complex layouts like multi-column pages or documents with intricate tables. Those tend to come out jumbled.

## Method 2: Convert using Google Docs (completely free, no paid tier)

Google Docs can open a PDF and turn it into an editable document without any paid subscription. You need a Google account, but the conversion itself has no daily limits.

1. Go to [Google Drive](https://drive.google.com) and upload your PDF (click **New > File Upload**).
2. Once uploaded, right-click the file and choose **Open with > Google Docs**.
3. Google Docs opens the PDF as an editable document.
4. To save it as a Word file, go to **File > Download > Microsoft Word (.docx)**.

Google Docs does a reasonable job with text-heavy documents. Like Word, it can't read scanned PDFs, and multi-column layouts sometimes collapse into a single column. But for a standard letter, report, or contract, this method works well and costs nothing.

## Method 3: Use a free online converter

Online converters handle the work without requiring any installed software. Most run in any browser and don't need an account to get started.

**iLovePDF** and **Smallpdf** are two long-established options. Both offer free conversions and support scanned PDFs via optical character recognition (OCR), which reads the image of the text and turns it back into actual characters you can edit.

Steps for most online converters:

1. Open the converter (for example, ilovepdf.com/pdf_to_word).
2. Click **Select PDF file** or drag your file onto the page.
3. If the tool offers OCR and your PDF is scanned, enable that option.
4. Click **Convert to Word** (or the equivalent button).
5. Download the .docx file.

Conversion usually takes 10 to 30 seconds. If your PDF is over 50 MB, some free tools reject it or ask you to log in. If that happens, Google Docs is a good fallback since it has no hard upload size limit for Drive.

## Why formatting sometimes changes after conversion

Converting a PDF to Word is not as simple as it sounds. [PDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) was designed to preserve a fixed visual layout: every element sits at an exact position on the page, the same way it would look in print. Word treats content differently, as flowing text that wraps and reflows to fit the page width.

When a converter bridges the two formats, it has to reconstruct how those fixed positions should behave as flowing text. Simple paragraphs translate cleanly. Tables, side-by-side columns, text boxes, and images with text wrapped around them can end up in the wrong order or in the wrong place.

If your converted file looks messy, try a tool with stronger layout analysis (iLovePDF and Smallpdf both handle this better than basic converters). Budget a few minutes for cleanup after conversion on anything with a complex layout.

## When you should skip the conversion entirely

If you only need to fix a typo or update a number, converting to Word and back again creates more work than it solves. Every conversion risks formatting drift, and re-exporting to PDF at the end produces a re-rendered file that may differ subtly from your original.

A cleaner approach is to edit the PDF directly. Our [Edit PDF](/edit-pdf) tool lets you click existing text, change it, and save the result as a PDF, all in your browser. No account needed, and your file stays on your device.

If you only need certain pages rather than the whole document, [Split PDF](/split-pdf) lets you pull out a page range first, so you have a smaller file to work with before converting.

## What about scanned PDFs?

A scanned PDF is a photograph of a page. Microsoft Word and Google Docs cannot extract text from a photograph, so you get a document that shows images instead of editable text.

To get real, editable text out of a scan, you need [optical character recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_character_recognition) (OCR). iLovePDF and Smallpdf both include OCR on their PDF-to-Word tools. Adobe Acrobat Online also offers a free conversion with OCR (one file per day on the free plan).

OCR accuracy depends heavily on scan quality. A clean, high-contrast scan converts well. A blurry phone-camera photo of a printed page often produces garbled characters that need manual correction afterward. If the scan was done with a flatbed scanner at 300 DPI or higher, expect good results.

## Which method to use

| Situation | Best method |
|---|---|
| You have Microsoft Word installed | File > Open the PDF directly |
| No Word, but you have a Google account | Google Docs (free, no limits) |
| Your PDF is scanned and needs OCR | iLovePDF or Smallpdf |
| You only need to fix a few words | Edit PDF directly, skip conversion |
