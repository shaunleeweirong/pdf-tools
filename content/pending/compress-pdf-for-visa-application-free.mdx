To compress a PDF for a visa application, open the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) in your browser, drop your file in, pick a quality level, and download the result. No sign-up, no server upload, and for most scanned visa documents the file size drops by more than half.

That said, visa documents come in two very different types, and compression works completely differently on each. Getting this right saves you from making a file larger by mistake.

## Scanned documents vs. digital PDFs: which type do you have?

The most common visa documents fall into two groups, and knowing which you have changes everything.

**Scanned PDFs** are documents that exist as photographs of pages. Your passport copy, a bank statement you printed and scanned, an employment certificate on letterhead, a property deed, or any document that started as a physical paper and went through a scanner or a phone camera app is a scanned PDF. These files are image-heavy, and compression makes them smaller.

**Digital PDFs** are generated directly from software. A bank statement downloaded from your bank's website, a payslip exported from payroll software, an airline booking confirmation, or an employment letter created in a word processor and saved as PDF is a digital PDF. These files store text as compact vector data, not as photographs. Running them through an image compressor converts all that compact text into JPEG snapshots of it, which takes much more space. A 23.5 KB text document grew to 1.4 MB after compression in our testing. Never compress a digital PDF to meet a visa portal's limit.

If you're not sure which type you have, open the file and try selecting text with your cursor. If you can highlight individual words, it's a digital PDF. If clicking produces nothing, it's a scanned PDF.

## How to compress a scanned PDF for a visa application

For scanned documents, the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) runs directly in your browser, processes the file on your device, and returns the smaller version in a few seconds.

1. Open the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf).
2. Click or drag to add your PDF.
3. Choose a quality level. Start with **Medium**, which gives a good balance between file size and readability.
4. Click **Compress PDF** and download the result.
5. Open the downloaded file and zoom in to 200% on the most detail-heavy areas: passport photo, stamps, official seals, and any handwritten signatures. If text looks blocky or stamps are illegible, the level was too aggressive.

If Medium isn't enough to meet the portal's limit, go back to the original file and run it again at **High** (the smallest output). Start from the original each time. Running compression a second pass on the same file makes it larger, not smaller. An already-compressed image deck grew from 315.3 KB to 598.9 KB when put through a second Medium pass in our testing.

## What compression level to pick

**Medium** is the right starting point for most visa documents. A 6-page image PDF went from 913.4 KB to 315.3 KB at Medium in our testing, a 65.5% reduction. That kind of result is typical for a multi-page passport copy or a scanned bank statement.

**High** (smallest file) takes the same 913.4 KB file down to 175.6 KB, an 80.8% reduction. Use this when Medium output still exceeds the portal's limit, or when the portal demands something under 200 KB. After compressing at High, always verify the content is still readable. Photos and fine print hold up reasonably well, but fine-grained stamps or watermarks can degrade.

**Low** (best quality) takes files to 703.5 KB at the same starting size, so it rarely helps when a portal cap is the problem. Use it only when you want maximum quality and your file is already close to the limit.

## What to do with digital PDFs that are too large

If your file is a digital PDF (you can select text in it), compression will make it larger. A few other options:

**Go back to the source.** If you have access to the original application or word processor, re-export the PDF with reduced image quality or without embedded fonts. Many apps let you choose a "smallest file" option at export time. This is the most effective fix.

**Use a cloud link instead of a file upload.** If the portal allows a URL or you're emailing documents separately, a cloud share link from Google Drive or Dropbox sidesteps the size issue entirely.

**Split the document.** If a large digital PDF contains several sections, extract the pages you need and submit them as separate files. Use the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) to pull out individual page ranges without re-encoding the content.

**Ask the issuing institution for a smaller export.** Banks and government agencies often issue statements in multiple formats. A smaller export or a different file format sometimes resolves the problem without any additional compression.

## Merging multiple visa documents into one file

Some portals require all supporting documents combined into a single PDF. If that's the case for your application, use the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) to combine your files first, then check the total size.

Merging does not inflate the result. Three files totaling 971.9 KB produced a merged output of 970.5 KB in our testing. You won't make the problem worse by combining files first. Once you know the merged size, decide whether you need to compress at all, and if so, compress the merged version.

One thing to check after merging: if any of your source documents contained filled form fields (some online forms export as interactive PDFs), those fields are removed during the merge. The pages and their visible content are preserved, but the form layer is lost. If this matters for your application, flatten the forms before merging.

## Scanning tips to get smaller files from the start

The simplest way to avoid a compression step entirely is to scan at a lower resolution. Most visa portals ask only that documents be legible, not that they be printed at the highest possible quality.

Scan at [150 DPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dots_per_inch) rather than 300 DPI for text documents. The resulting file will be roughly a quarter the size, and text reads clearly on screen and in print at that resolution. For documents with fine detail (stamps, embossed seals), 200 DPI is a reasonable middle point.

If you're scanning with a phone, most document scanner apps (Apple Notes, Microsoft Lens, Adobe Scan) let you choose output quality. Pick a medium setting. The [PDF file format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) stores images at their scanned resolution, so the DPI you scan at is the DPI the visa officer sees.

## Before you submit: a quick checklist

- Open the compressed PDF and zoom in on stamps, barcodes, signatures, and small text.
- Check that the file is under the portal's stated limit with a small buffer to spare. Don't upload a file that's exactly at the limit.
- Confirm the document hasn't gained extra pages or white space from the compression process.
- If the portal asks for a specific format (PDF/A, for example), verify that the compressed file meets the spec. Standard compression tools produce regular [PDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) output, not PDF/A.
- Keep the original uncompressed file. If the portal rejects your upload, you'll want to start from the original rather than compress an already-compressed copy.
