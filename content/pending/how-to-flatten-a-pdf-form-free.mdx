## How to flatten a PDF form for free

Flattening a PDF form locks your filled-in answers permanently into the page. You can flatten a PDF form for free using the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf): drag in your filled form, click Flatten, and download the result. No account required, and nothing leaves your browser.

If you need to submit the form to a court, email it as an attachment, or combine it with other documents, flatten it first. The sections below explain exactly what flattening does and when you need it.

## What flattening does to a PDF form

A fillable [PDF form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF#AcroForms) stores two kinds of content separately: the static page layer (backgrounds, labels, borders) and the interactive layer (text boxes, checkboxes, radio buttons, and other annotations). Until you flatten the file, those two layers are distinct. Some PDF viewers and e-filing systems read only the static layer and skip the interactive one entirely, which is why an unflattened form can look perfectly complete on your screen but appear blank to whoever receives it.

Annotations follow the same pattern. Comments, highlights, and stamps added on top of a page sit in a separate overlay until the document is flattened.

Flattening merges both layers into a single static page. After flattening:

- The values you typed appear as ordinary text on the page.
- Checkboxes and radio buttons become fixed marks.
- No one can click into a field, change a value, or delete an entry.
- Annotations are baked into the page and cannot be removed.
- The document looks identical in every PDF viewer, on every device.

Think of it as photocopying a completed form. The copy shows exactly what you wrote, permanently fixed in place.

## When do you need to flatten?

**Before e-filing with a court.** Federal and state courts using [CM/ECF](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-records/electronic-filing-cmecf) require flattened PDFs. The filing system reads document text from the static content stream. Form field values live in a separate data layer, so an unflattened form can appear blank after upload even though it looks correct in your PDF viewer. Dozens of federal district courts publish explicit instructions requiring flattening before any document is docketed.

**Before merging with other documents.** This is the mistake that catches people most often. When you merge a filled form into a combined PDF, the form fields are stripped from the output. The blank field borders may survive, but the values you entered do not. The fix is to flatten the form first, check that the values appear as plain text on the page, then [merge the flattened file](/merge-pdf) with your cover letter or supporting documents.

**Before emailing or sharing.** Web browser PDF viewers and mobile apps don't always render interactive fields the same way. A carefully filled form can show up with empty boxes on the recipient's screen. A flattened version looks the same everywhere, regardless of what software the other person uses to open it.

**Before printing.** Most modern print dialogs handle filled fields correctly, but older PDF drivers can skip the interactive layer. Flattening before printing removes that risk entirely.

## How to flatten a PDF form (step by step)

1. Open the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) in your browser.
2. Drag your filled PDF onto the page, or click to select the file from your device.
3. Click **Flatten**.
4. Download the flattened PDF to your computer.

Processing happens entirely in your browser. Your file is not uploaded to any server.

## The free no-tool method: print to PDF

Every modern operating system includes a built-in PDF printer. You can use it to flatten any form without extra software:

1. Open your filled PDF in your browser or default PDF viewer.
2. Press **Ctrl+P** on Windows or **Cmd+P** on Mac to open the print dialog.
3. Choose **Save as PDF** (Chrome, Edge, Firefox), **Microsoft Print to PDF** (Windows), or **Save to PDF** (macOS) as the printer.
4. Pick a save location and click Print or Save.

The resulting file has no interactive fields. This method works on any device with nothing to install and no account to create.

The trade-off is that the "Save as PDF" approach re-renders the entire page, which strips bookmarks and hyperlinks and can shift the layout slightly in some documents. For a simple one-page form, the result is visually identical to a dedicated flatten tool. For a complex document with navigation links or embedded content, the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) is more precise.

## Flatten before you merge, not after

If your plan is to merge a filled form with other pages, the order matters. Running the merge first and hoping to preserve the field values afterwards does not work. The values disappear the moment the merge runs, because merging copies page content but not the interactive layer where field values are stored.

The correct sequence every time:

1. Fill in the form and save it.
2. Open the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) and flatten the filled form.
3. Open the flattened file and confirm the values appear as plain text on the page.
4. [Merge the flattened file](/merge-pdf) with your other documents.

This is the same advice given by courts that require combined filings. The form has to be flat before anything else joins it.

## Save a copy before you flatten

Flattening is permanent. The editable fields are gone from the output file and there is no undo. Before you run the tool, save a copy of the filled form with the fields still intact. Keep the original in case you need to correct an answer. Send the flattened version to whoever needs it.

That habit takes five seconds and has saved plenty of people from re-filling a long form from scratch because they flattened the only copy.
