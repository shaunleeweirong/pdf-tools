You can convert a PDF to an audio file for free online in under a minute: upload your PDF, choose a voice, and download the MP3. The exact tool to pick depends on whether your PDF contains real text or scanned images, and whether you need it to work without creating an account.

## Why people listen to PDFs instead of reading them

Listening is faster for commutes, workouts, and long reading lists. Text-to-speech (TTS) technology, which underpins every PDF-to-audio converter, reads out text at whatever speed you set, so a 40-page report can become a 25-minute listen at 1.5x speed.

People also use audio conversion for accessibility. For anyone with dyslexia or low vision, having a document read aloud removes friction. The [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/) treat audio alternatives as a core accommodation, and the same logic applies to personal documents.

## Text PDFs vs scanned PDFs: why it matters

Not all PDFs contain actual text. A scanned document is essentially a photograph stored inside a PDF container. A TTS converter reads characters; it cannot read a picture of characters.

If your PDF was created directly from a Word document, Google Docs, or a similar application, it almost certainly has real text. Any converter will work.

If your PDF is a scan, a photo of a document, or an older book that was digitized without OCR, you need a converter that runs [optical character recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_character_recognition) before converting. Speechify and Natural Reader both include OCR. If you run a basic converter on a scanned PDF, you'll get an audio file that says nothing, or skips pages entirely.

When in doubt, try selecting and copying a sentence in your PDF reader. If you can highlight text, it's a text PDF. If you can't, it's scanned.

## Free tools that convert PDF to audio online

Here are the most reliable free options, based on what's available without a paid plan:

**DigitalOfficePro** takes files up to 300 MB, converts to MP3, and requires no account. It's a clean, no-sign-up flow: upload, convert, download. Good for most everyday documents.

**NoteGPT PDF to Audio** lets you choose a voice and language before converting, then gives you a download link plus a QR code to scan and listen on your phone. Also free with no account.

**Zamzar** is a general-purpose file converter with PDF-to-MP3 support. Files under 100 MB convert free without signing in. The output is a standard MP3.

**Speechify** is the most feature-rich option and is widely cited as the benchmark for voice quality and language coverage. The free tier handles short documents. For full books or long reports, you'll hit the length cap quickly.

**Natural Reader** has a free web version with several natural-sounding voices and OCR support for scanned PDFs. The free plan limits the number of characters per day.

**Read Aloud** is a browser extension for Chrome and Edge that reads any open PDF aloud without any uploading at all. It does not produce a downloadable MP3, but if you only need to listen rather than save a file, it's the fastest option.

## How to convert a PDF to audio file, step by step

1. Open the tool you chose (DigitalOfficePro or NoteGPT work well for most people).
2. Click Upload and select your PDF from your computer or phone.
3. Choose a voice and, if your document isn't in English, select the correct language.
4. Click Convert (or Start, or similar).
5. Wait for the conversion to finish. A 20-page text PDF usually takes under 30 seconds; a scanned document with OCR takes longer.
6. Download the MP3 file to your device.
7. Transfer it to your phone, import it into a podcast app, or just play it in your browser.

For scanned PDFs, make sure you pick a tool with OCR in step 1, otherwise steps 4 to 6 will produce a silent or nearly silent audio file.

## What to do if your PDF is too large to upload

Most free tools cap at 50 to 300 MB. Text-based PDFs are almost always small (a typical 50-page report is under 1 MB), so hitting a limit usually means your PDF is image-heavy: lots of embedded photos, diagrams, or full-page scans.

If it's a scanned PDF and you want to reduce the file size, our [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) can shrink image-heavy files before you upload them to a TTS converter. The compressor works by rasterizing pages to JPEG, which cuts file size for documents that are already image-based.

One thing to be aware of: compressing a text-based PDF will make it dramatically larger, not smaller, because it converts the crisp vector text into a photograph of text. Only compress image-heavy or scanned files, not standard text documents.

If your document is too long rather than too large, split it using a PDF splitter and convert each part separately.

## Choosing a voice and language

Every tool has different voice libraries. If the output sounds robotic or mispronounces common words, try a different voice. Most services let you preview voices before converting, so spend a minute on this step if you're creating audio you'll listen to regularly.

For non-English PDFs, check the tool's language list before uploading. NoteGPT and Speechify both cover dozens of languages. Zamzar converts to audio but does not change the language of playback, so the text will be read with whatever language the TTS engine defaults to for that voice.

## Can you convert a PDF to audio offline?

Yes, but you'll need an app rather than a website. On iOS and macOS, the built-in Speak Screen and Speak Selection accessibility features in [Apple's accessibility settings](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/spoken-content-iph96b214f0/ios) can read any open PDF aloud without an internet connection. The output isn't saved as a file, but it works completely offline.

On Windows, Microsoft's built-in Narrator and the Edge browser's Read Aloud feature can read PDFs aloud offline. Neither produces a downloadable MP3.

For a downloadable offline audio file, you'd need a desktop application like Balabolka (Windows, free) or a script using a local TTS engine.

For most people, the free online tools listed above are the fastest path to a listenable MP3, and none of the recommended no-sign-up options store your file after the conversion is done.
