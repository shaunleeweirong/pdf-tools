Viewing two PDF files side by side is the fastest way to cross-reference documents, check revisions, or compare a contract against a template. You can do it on any computer for free, using tools you already have.

## The quickest method: snap two browser windows

Browsers like Chrome, Firefox, Edge, and Safari open PDFs natively. Once you have two PDFs open in separate windows, your operating system handles the layout.

1. Open the first PDF in your browser. If it opened as a tab, drag the tab out to make it its own window.
2. Open the second PDF in a new browser window (Ctrl+N on Windows, Cmd+N on Mac).
3. Snap the two windows side by side using the keyboard shortcut for your platform (see below).

No plugins, no uploads, no account.

## How to view PDFs side by side on Windows 10 or 11

Windows has a feature called Snap Assist that splits your screen into halves automatically.

1. Open both PDFs in separate browser or reader windows.
2. Press **Windows key + Left arrow** to snap the first window to the left half of the screen.
3. A thumbnail grid of your other open windows appears on the right. Click your second PDF window.
4. Both PDFs now fill the screen side by side. You can scroll each one independently.

For a wider view, grab the dividing line between the two windows and drag it to give each PDF more or less space. On a wide monitor, four-quadrant layouts are also possible. [Microsoft's Snap Assist documentation](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/snap-your-windows-885a9b1e-a983-a3b1-16cd-c531795e6241) covers every keyboard shortcut available.

### Moving a Chrome tab to its own window

If both PDFs are open as tabs in one Chrome window, right-click the second tab and choose **Move tab to new window**. Then snap as above.

## How to view PDFs side by side on Mac

macOS uses Split View to tile two apps across the screen at once.

1. Open the first PDF in Preview or in any browser.
2. Hold the **green fullscreen button** in the top-left corner of the window until a menu appears.
3. Choose **Tile Window to Left of Screen**. The window fills the left half of the screen.
4. Click your second PDF window from the thumbnails that appear on the right side.

Both windows are now in Split View. Scroll each one independently and resize the split by dragging the center divider. To exit, press Escape or click the green button at the top of either window.

[Apple's Split View guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-apps-in-split-view-mh14112/mac) has the full walkthrough for macOS Sonoma and later, including Stage Manager as an alternative for managing multiple documents.

## How to view PDFs side by side on a Chromebook

ChromeOS has its own Snap Windows feature built in.

1. Open the first PDF in Chrome and the second in a separate Chrome window (Alt+N).
2. Hold **Alt + [** to snap the first window to the left half of the screen.
3. Hold **Alt + ]** to snap the second to the right.

Alternatively, drag a window's title bar to the far left or right edge of the screen until a half-screen preview appears, then release. ChromeOS does the rest.

## Viewing PDFs side by side on a phone or tablet

Side-by-side viewing is harder on small screens, but possible on some devices.

On Android, open the first PDF in Chrome, tap the square Overview button, tap the window title, and choose **Split screen**. Then select Chrome again for the lower half and open your second PDF. This works on Android 9 and later.

On iPad, you can use Apple's multitasking to split the screen between two Safari windows, each with a PDF open. Drag one Safari window to the edge until Split View activates, then open the second PDF in the other half. On iPhone the screen is generally too small for side-by-side reading.

## When to use a comparison tool instead

Side-by-side viewing works well when you're cross-referencing two different documents or reading sections you already know. But when you need to find every change between two versions of the same document, scrolling through both simultaneously takes a long time.

The [Compare PDF tool](/compare-pdf) highlights visual differences between the first pages of two PDFs in your browser. Load both files and the tool marks changes in color so you can spot them in seconds. No sign-up, and files aren't sent anywhere off your device.

This is most useful for:
- Checking whether a contract's cover page changed between drafts
- Confirming that an updated form's first page matches the original layout
- Quickly spotting any alteration on a document's front page before you read the rest

For comparing entire multi-page documents page by page, the OS side-by-side method above is your free option.

## Combining documents after reviewing them

Once you've reviewed two PDFs side by side and want to merge them, the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) combines them into a single file. The output size stays close to the sum of the two inputs, so merging won't add significant bulk.

One thing to watch for: merging strips interactive form fields from the output. If either PDF has fillable fields, use the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) first to bake the fields into the page before merging.

If you want to reorder the pages from both documents before combining them, the [Organize PDF tool](/organize-pdf) lets you drag pages into any order visually before saving.

## Which viewer works best for side-by-side review?

Any browser that opens PDFs natively works. Chrome and Edge both include annotation toolbars in their PDF viewers, so you can highlight or add notes while reading side by side without switching to another app.

Edge's PDF toolbar is particularly full-featured for review tasks: you can draw, highlight, and add text comments directly on each document while both are visible.

[Firefox's built-in PDF viewer](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/view-pdf-files-firefox-or-choose-another-viewer) supports custom zoom levels, which is handy for matching the scale of both windows when documents have different page sizes.

The short answer is to use whatever browser you already have open. The snapping is handled by your operating system, not the PDF viewer, so any modern browser does the job equally well.
