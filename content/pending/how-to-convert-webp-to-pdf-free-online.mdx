Converting a WebP image to PDF is free and takes under a minute. The fastest method doesn't require any upload at all: open the WebP in any modern browser and print it to PDF.

WebP is a [modern image format developed by Google](https://developers.google.com/speed/webp) that most browsers now support natively. It's common in screenshots saved from Chrome, images downloaded from Google products, and photos exported by various apps. The problem is that many online forms and submission portals only accept PDFs, not images.

Here are three ways to get there, starting with the simplest.

## Method 1: Use the browser's built-in print-to-PDF (Windows, Mac, Linux)

This works on any operating system and doesn't upload your file anywhere.

1. Open the WebP file in Chrome, Firefox, or Edge. On most systems, double-clicking the file opens it in your default browser. If not, right-click the file and choose "Open with" followed by your browser.
2. Press **Ctrl+P** on Windows or Linux, or **Cmd+P** on Mac.
3. In the print dialog, look for the Destination (Chrome) or Printer (Firefox/Edge) setting.
4. Choose **Save as PDF** or **Microsoft Print to PDF**.
5. Click Save and pick where you want the file.

The result is a single-page PDF sized to the image's natural dimensions. This is the quickest option and works for one image at a time.

## Method 2: Use Mac Preview

If you're on a Mac, Preview handles this in two clicks without opening a browser.

1. Double-click the WebP file. It opens in Preview.
2. Go to **File > Export as PDF**.
3. Give it a name, choose a save location, and click Save.

Preview is built into every Mac, so nothing to install. It also preserves more of the original quality than a print dialog, since it's not scaling the image to fit a paper size.

## Method 3: Save as PNG, then use an online image-to-PDF tool

This path takes one extra step but gives you more control. It's especially useful when you want to combine several WebP images into a single PDF.

The catch with most online converters, including our [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf), is that they accept JPEG and PNG files but not WebP directly. Saving the WebP as PNG first takes seconds and doesn't reduce the image quality.

**How to save a WebP as PNG in your browser:**

1. Open the WebP image in Chrome, Firefox, or Edge.
2. Right-click the image and choose **Save image as**.
3. In the save dialog, change the filename extension from `.webp` to `.png`, or look for a format dropdown and select PNG.
4. Click Save.

In Chrome, when you right-click a WebP and save it, the browser often saves it as PNG automatically (the file name ends in `.png`). Check the file after saving.

**Then convert to PDF:**

1. Open the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf).
2. Drag the PNG file (or files) into the upload area.
3. Arrange the order if you have multiple images.
4. Download the PDF.

The tool runs in your browser and processes images without sending them to a server. If you have three or four WebP screenshots to merge into one document, this approach is more practical than the print method, which handles one image at a time.

## Converting WebP to PDF on a phone

On an iPhone or iPad, open the WebP in Safari, tap the **Share** button (the box with an arrow), and select **Print**. Once the print preview appears, pinch outward on the thumbnail with two fingers. This expands it into a full PDF view. From there, tap the Share button again and choose Save to Files (or any other destination you want).

On Android, open the WebP in Chrome, tap the three-dot menu in the top right, and choose **Print**. In the print dialog, change the Destination to **Save as PDF**, then tap the PDF button. The file saves to your Downloads folder.

Neither method requires installing an app.

## A note on file size

A WebP image converted to PDF will be roughly the same size as the original image, sometimes slightly larger, depending on the method. The [PDF/A standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF/A) aside, a standard PDF wraps image data in a container, so the image dimensions and compression determine the weight, not the conversion step itself.

If the resulting PDF is too large to email (Gmail and Outlook.com both cap messages at [25 MB](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6584)), and your image is a photo or scan, you can run it through the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) first. That works well on image-heavy PDFs. Avoid compressing PDFs that are mainly text, as rasterization will make them larger rather than smaller.
