You can convert a JPG to PDF on iPhone for free using tools that are already built into iOS, no download required. The fastest method takes three taps in the Files app and works on any iPhone running [iOS 15](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102662) or later.

Here are three ways to get it done.

## Method 1: Files app (fastest, fully built-in)

The Files app has a Quick Actions menu that converts images to PDF with a single tap. This is the cleanest built-in method and handles multiple images at once.

1. Open the **Files** app and go to the folder where your JPG is saved. If it's in your Camera Roll, first tap **Share** in the Photos app and choose **Save to Files** to move it there.
2. Tap **Select** at the top right, then tap each photo you want to include.
3. Tap the **three-dot menu** (labeled "More") at the bottom right.
4. Choose **Create PDF**.

That's it. iOS creates a PDF in the same folder, with one image per page, named after the first file you selected. If you picked multiple images, they all go into one PDF in the order you selected them.

This method is completely offline. Nothing leaves your device.

## Method 2: Print preview trick (works on older iOS)

If you're on iOS 11 to 14, or if you just prefer to stay in the Photos app, there's a print-to-PDF workaround that's been around since before Files got the Quick Actions update.

1. Open the **Photos** app and select the photo.
2. Tap the **Share** button (the square with an arrow pointing up).
3. Scroll down and tap **Print**.
4. On the print preview screen, place two fingers on the thumbnail and **pinch outward** (the same zoom gesture you'd use on a webpage). The image opens as a PDF preview.
5. Tap **Share** again in the top right corner, then choose **Save to Files** to save the PDF.

This method only works for one photo at a time. For multiple photos, you'd need to repeat the process and then [merge the PDFs](/merge-pdf) afterward.

## Method 3: Browser tool (no install, no sign-up, works for multiple images)

If you want to combine several photos into one PDF without going through the Files app, a browser-based converter is the simplest path. Open Safari on your iPhone and visit the [JPG to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf).

1. Tap **Choose files** and select one or more images from your Camera Roll or Files.
2. Reorder the images if needed by dragging the thumbnails.
3. Tap **Convert** and download the PDF.

The tool accepts JPG and PNG files and puts each image on its own page. It runs directly in your browser tab, so your photos don't get uploaded to any server.

This approach is especially useful when you have five or ten photos to combine and don't want to manage them one at a time in the Files app.

## Which method should you use?

It depends on where you're starting from:

- **One or two photos already in Files:** use Method 1 (Quick Actions).
- **On an older iPhone or already in the Photos app:** use Method 2 (Print trick).
- **Multiple photos to combine, or you prefer a browser flow:** use Method 3 (browser tool).

All three methods are free and produce a standard [PDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) that opens on any device.

## What if I want multiple images on the same PDF page?

The methods above put one image per page, which is the most common format for sharing and submitting documents. If you want a layout with two or four photos per page (like a contact sheet), that's a different step: convert the images to PDF first, then use an N-up layout tool if your use case requires it.

For most purposes, one image per page is exactly what's needed for things like scanning receipts, sending reference photos, or submitting identification documents.

## How to share or email the PDF after converting

Once the PDF is in your Files app, tap it to open it, then tap the **Share** button. From there you can:

- Attach it to an email in the Mail app
- Share it to WhatsApp, Slack, or any other app
- Save it to iCloud Drive to access it on other devices
- AirDrop it to a nearby Mac or iPad

If the PDF is too large to email (most mail providers cap attachments at 20 to 25 MB), you can [compress it](/compress-pdf) first. Keep in mind that compression works best on image-heavy files, and the compressor rasterizes pages, so the PDF from a photo would be a good candidate.
