Combining receipt photos into one PDF is straightforward with the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf): upload all your photos at once and download a single, multi-page PDF you can attach to an expense report or email.

## Why expense systems want a single PDF

Finance teams and platforms like [SAP Concur](https://www.concur.com/) and [Expensify](https://www.expensify.com/) typically accept one attachment per expense claim. If you snapped separate photos of each receipt, you need to merge them into one document before you can submit. A single PDF also makes audits easier: the reviewer sees every receipt in sequence, in one file, without chasing down a folder of attachments.

## How to combine receipt photos into one PDF

### Step-by-step on desktop or mobile browser

1. Open the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) in your browser.
2. Click the upload area and select all your receipt photos. You can pick multiple files at once.
3. Check the preview to confirm each receipt appears as a separate page.
4. Click **Convert** and download the finished PDF.

Each JPG or PNG file becomes one page. Ten receipt photos produce a ten-page PDF.

### On an iPhone without any app

Your iPhone's built-in Notes app can handle this. Open Notes, create a new note, then tap the camera icon and choose **Scan Documents**. Aim the camera at each receipt in turn, letting it capture automatically. When you're done, tap **Save**. The receipts are saved as a multi-page PDF inside the note, which you can share via email or save to Files.

Alternatively, open the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) in Safari on your iPhone, tap the upload area, and select photos from your camera roll. No app download needed.

### On Android

Google Drive's built-in scanner works without any extra download. In the Drive app, tap the **+** button and choose **Scan**. Photograph each receipt, tap the **+** icon to add the next page, then tap **Save** when you're done. The pages save as one PDF in your Drive.

You can also use the browser-based [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) in Chrome on Android: tap the upload area and choose photos from your gallery.

## What image format should your photos be in?

The Images to PDF tool accepts JPG and PNG. Most Android phones and many iPhone settings save photos as JPG already.

If your iPhone saves HEIC images (a compressed Apple format), you need to export them as JPG first. The quickest fix is to go to **Settings > Camera > Formats** and select **Most Compatible**. Future photos will save as JPG. For photos you've already taken, share them to your Mac via AirDrop and macOS will convert them automatically, or use the **Files** app to export as JPEG.

According to [Apple's developer documentation on image formats](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturesession), HEIC images can require conversion steps before external apps can read them, which is why JPG is the safer choice for cross-platform tools.

## What if the combined PDF is too large?

Phone photos can be large. Ten receipt images might add up to 5 to 10 MB, which could be over your expense platform's limit.

Because receipts are images, the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) can usually shrink the file. The tool rasterizes each page and re-encodes it as JPEG. That hurts text-only PDFs (they get much bigger), but it helps image-heavy PDFs. Choose **Low (best quality)** to keep receipt text legible. **Medium** compresses more, but finer details like itemized amounts may get harder to read.

One thing to avoid: running the compress tool twice. A second pass at the same level makes the file larger, not smaller, because re-encoding an already-compressed JPEG adds data. If Low is not enough, start from the original and try Medium instead.

## Combining photo receipts with existing PDF receipts

Sometimes you have a mix: a paper receipt you photographed, a PDF emailed from an online store, and a bank statement page. You can handle this in two steps.

1. Convert your photo receipts to a PDF using the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf).
2. Use the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) to combine that new PDF with your existing PDF files into one document.

Merging does not inflate the file size: the merged output comes in at or under the combined size of the individual files, so you won't accidentally push yourself over an attachment limit just by merging.

## Keeping receipts in the right order

For predictable page ordering, name your image files with a leading number before you select them, for example `01-taxi.jpg`, `02-hotel.jpg`, `03-dinner.jpg`. Most operating systems sort files alphabetically by default, so numbered names keep them in the sequence you want.

If you're on a phone, you can also select photos one at a time in the order you need, rather than selecting all at once.

## A note on what the PDF looks like

Each receipt occupies one full page in the PDF. The image is not resized or cropped, so the receipt is as legible as your original photo. If the photo is blurry or poorly lit, the PDF will reflect that: the tool converts what you give it without enhancement.

For receipts that need to be clearly readable for an audit or reimbursement, take the photo in good light and as flat as possible. A slightly angled shot is readable, but a crumpled receipt shot from the side may not be.
