You can present a PDF as a slideshow for free using nothing more than your browser. Chrome, Edge, and Firefox all have a built-in full-screen or present mode that makes your PDF behave like a slide deck, with arrow-key navigation and no distractions.

## The fastest method: use your browser's present mode

Open your PDF in [Google Chrome](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/6213030) or Microsoft Edge by dragging the file into a browser tab. Then:

1. Click the three-dot menu (⋮) in the top-right corner of the PDF viewer.
2. Choose **Present** (Chrome) or **Full screen** (Edge).
3. Use the right arrow key or Page Down to move forward, and left arrow or Page Up to go back.
4. Press Escape to exit.

No account needed, nothing to install, nothing uploaded. The PDF stays on your device the whole time.

Firefox works too. Press F11 to fill the screen with the browser window, and your PDF fills the view. Arrow keys still advance pages. On Mac, Cmd+Ctrl+F triggers full screen in most browsers.

## How to present a PDF on Zoom, Teams, or Google Meet

Open the PDF in your browser before the call starts. When you're ready to present:

1. Start screen sharing in your meeting app.
2. Choose to share your full screen or the browser window.
3. Click into the browser tab with your PDF.
4. Go full-screen with F11 (Windows) or Cmd+Ctrl+F (Mac).
5. Advance slides with the arrow keys while you speak.

Sharing your full screen usually gives the cleanest result, because full-screen mode hides the browser toolbar entirely. If you share only the browser window, the toolbar may still show to attendees.

A Bluetooth presenter remote sends Page Up and Page Down signals that work in any browser-based PDF view. Logitech and Kensington both make reliable ones. It keeps your hands free and lets you advance slides from anywhere in the room.

## Prepare the PDF before presenting

Blank pages, wrong page order, or a mix of portrait and landscape pages can trip you up mid-presentation. A few minutes of prep makes a real difference.

**Reorder or remove pages.** Use the [Organize PDF](/organize-pdf) tool to drag slides into the right sequence and delete pages you won't use. It runs in the browser with no installation.

**Extract just the slides you need.** If you're presenting one chapter of a long report, [Split PDF](/split-pdf) lets you pull out a specific page range and save it as a new file. Smaller files load faster and are easier to navigate.

**Fix rotation problems.** If some pages are sideways, use [Rotate PDF](/rotate-pdf) to correct them before you start. Rotating only adjusts the page orientation flag in the file, so it doesn't re-render anything or change your file size.

## Convert PDF to Google Slides when you need speaker notes

Browser full-screen mode is fine for reading through slides, but it doesn't give you speaker notes or a presentation timer. If you need those, converting to [Google Slides](https://workspace.google.com/products/slides/) takes about two minutes:

1. Upload the PDF to Google Drive.
2. Right-click it and choose **Open with > Google Slides**.
3. Google converts each PDF page into a slide image.

Check every slide after the conversion. Simple layouts transfer well; dense text pages or complex graphics may not look exactly right. Once it's a Google Slides file, you get a proper presenter view with notes, a timer, and the ability to jump to any slide.

For PowerPoint, Adobe's free web version at acrobat.adobe.com allows a handful of PDF-to-PPTX conversions per month without an account.

## Convert PDF pages to images for non-PDF tools

Some tools don't accept PDFs at all. Canva, most email editors, and some content management systems expect image files. The [PDF to JPG](/pdf-to-jpg) tool exports every page as a separate JPG. You can drag those images into any slideshow builder, or post individual slides to social media.

This is also useful when a client asks for slide images rather than the full PDF file.

## When to compress before presenting

Very large PDFs can cause a noticeable delay between slides, because each page has to load before it displays. This is most common with scan-heavy files or PDFs assembled from high-resolution photos.

If your presentation PDF is slow to flip between pages, use the [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) tool to reduce its size. One thing to know first: compression works by rasterizing pages to JPEG, so it helps most with image-heavy and photo-heavy PDFs. A text-only PDF won't get smaller from compression and may actually get larger. For those, the better move is to split out only the pages you need rather than compressing the whole file.

## How to set up a PDF slideshow: step-by-step

Here's a complete workflow from raw PDF to presentation-ready slideshow:

1. **Open the PDF** in Chrome, Edge, or Firefox.
2. **Check page order** and remove anything you won't present using [Organize PDF](/organize-pdf).
3. **Fix any rotated pages** with [Rotate PDF](/rotate-pdf) so everything faces the right way.
4. **Reduce file size** if the file is slow to load, using [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) (image-heavy files only).
5. **Save the cleaned-up file** to your desktop.
6. **Open the final file** in your browser.
7. **Go full-screen** (F11 on Windows, Cmd+Ctrl+F on Mac).
8. **Share your screen** in your meeting app, or project the browser window.
9. **Use arrow keys** to advance slides.

## Quick tips for a smooth PDF presentation

**Test before the call.** Open the PDF in the browser you'll use, go full-screen, and confirm the arrow keys work. Do this before attendees join.

**Hide your desktop.** If you share your full screen, everyone sees everything on it. Close tabs you don't want visible and clear your desktop.

**Bring a backup.** If you're presenting at a venue, email yourself the PDF or keep it in cloud storage. Local files disappear when you close the laptop; email doesn't.

**Download the file.** If your PDF opens from a URL rather than a local file, a slow connection can cause rendering delays between pages. Download the file to your machine before the meeting.

**Use keyboard shortcuts.** Beyond arrow keys, the End key jumps to the last page and Home jumps to the first. Handy if you need to jump to a summary slide quickly.
