Printing a PDF as a booklet is free on any computer. The fastest method is [Adobe Acrobat Reader](https://helpx.adobe.com/acrobat/kb/print-booklets-acrobat-reader.html), which is free and has a dedicated booklet option built in. On Mac, the Preview app handles it without any extra software. If you'd rather not install anything, a few online tools do the job with no account required.

Before you print, two things matter: your PDF's page count must be a multiple of 4, and you need a printer that handles both sides (or two print passes manually). The rest of this guide walks through each method step by step, plus the two fixes that solve most booklet printing problems.

## Booklet printing vs. 2 pages per sheet: what's the difference?

These two things look similar but are not the same, and mixing them up is the most common source of frustration.

**Two-pages-per-sheet** puts pages in reading order: pages 1 and 2 sit side by side on the first sheet, pages 3 and 4 on the next. You can read the output without folding anything. Our [Pages Per Sheet tool](/n-up-pdf) does exactly this and is useful for handouts, study notes, or anything you want compact but readable flat.

**Booklet printing** uses a technique called imposition to rearrange pages so that folding and stapling the sheets produces a document that reads in the correct sequence. For an 8-page booklet, the outer sheet has pages 8 and 1 on one side, pages 2 and 7 on the other. The inner sheet has pages 6 and 3 on one side, pages 4 and 5 on the other. After folding, everything falls in order from page 1 through 8.

You never need to rearrange pages yourself. Let the software handle imposition. Manual reordering almost always produces the wrong result.

## Rule: your page count must be a multiple of 4

Each folded sheet holds exactly 4 booklet pages (front and back, 2 per side). A 12-page booklet uses 3 sheets. A 10-page PDF can't be divided evenly, so booklet software pads the document with blank pages to reach 12.

If you'd rather control where the blank pages land, [merge](/merge-pdf) a blank-page PDF at the end of your document before printing.

## Method 1: Adobe Acrobat Reader (free, Windows and Mac)

Acrobat Reader is the most reliable free option for booklet printing. It's available for Windows and Mac with no cost.

1. Open your PDF in Acrobat Reader.
2. Press **Ctrl+P** (Windows) or **Cmd+P** (Mac).
3. Under "Page Sizing & Handling," click **Booklet**.
4. Set **Booklet subset** to "Both sides" for an auto-duplex printer. Without auto-duplex, choose "Front side only," print, reload the paper, then choose "Back side only."
5. Set **Binding** to Left for left-to-right languages, or Right for Arabic or Hebrew text.
6. Click **Print**.

**Most common mistake:** For portrait-orientation booklets, your printer must flip pages on the short edge. If the backs of your sheets print upside down, open your printer's own properties dialog (not Acrobat's) and switch from "Flip on long edge" to "Flip on short edge." This one setting fixes the upside-down problem on virtually every printer.

## Method 2: Mac Preview (free, no download needed)

Preview, the PDF viewer built into macOS, handles simple booklets without any extra software.

1. Open the PDF in Preview.
2. Go to **File > Print** (Cmd+P).
3. Click **Show Details** at the bottom of the print dialog.
4. Change the middle dropdown (which shows "Preview" by default) to **Layout**.
5. Set **Pages per Sheet** to 2 and **Layout Direction** to the icon showing two pages facing each other.
6. Enable **Two-Sided** and set **Binding** to **Short-Edge**.
7. Click **Print**.

Preview's approach works for short documents. For anything longer than about 20 pages, Acrobat Reader gives you more control over the imposition and binding settings.

## Method 3: free online tools (no install)

If you don't want to install anything at all, a few online tools convert your PDF into a print-ready booklet file that you download and print normally from any PDF viewer.

**BookletFree.com** is the simplest: drop your PDF in, pick your paper size, and download the rearranged file. No account needed.

**Online2PDF.com** lets you set the layout and output size and is free without registration.

One practical note: these tools upload your file to a server for processing. For a recipe booklet or event program that's fine. For contracts, medical records, or anything sensitive, use Acrobat Reader or Preview instead, since both process everything locally on your device.

## How to print on a printer without auto-duplex

You don't need a fancy duplex printer to print a booklet. Here's the manual approach:

1. In your booklet print settings, choose **"Front side only"** (or "Odd pages" depending on the tool).
2. Print. Take the printed stack out of the output tray, keeping the pages in order.
3. Flip the stack. The exact flip direction depends on your printer model: most desktop inkjets want you to flip along the short edge (top-to-bottom), then put the stack back in the tray face down.
4. Go back to print settings, choose **"Back side only"**, and print again.

If a test print comes out wrong, try flipping the paper the other way and reprinting just the second pass.

## Troubleshooting the two most common problems

**Pages are in the wrong order after folding.** This almost always means you used a reading-order N-up tool instead of a booklet imposition tool. The content is fine; the page sequence wasn't rearranged. Start again with Method 1 or 2, and let the software handle the ordering.

**The back side of each sheet is upside down.** Your duplex binding direction is wrong. In your printer driver's properties (not the PDF app), switch from "Flip on long edge" to "Flip on short edge." This is the standard fix for portrait booklets on Windows and Mac printers.

## Printing large PDFs as booklets

A saddle-stitched booklet (fold and staple along the spine) works well up to about 48 to 64 pages. Beyond that, the paper stack gets thick and won't lie flat when folded. For a 100-page document, split it into multiple 16-page or 32-page sections first, then print and bind each section separately. Our [Pages Per Sheet tool](/n-up-pdf) can help you prepare compact handout versions of individual sections if you need a preview before committing to printing.

The right paper size is one step up from your finished booklet. An A5 booklet prints on A4 paper; a half-letter booklet (5.5 by 8.5 inches) prints on standard letter paper (8.5 by 11 inches). Most home printers max out at A4 or letter, which makes A5 and half-letter the practical choices for home printing.
