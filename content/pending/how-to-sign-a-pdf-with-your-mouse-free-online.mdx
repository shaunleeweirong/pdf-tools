Signing a PDF with your mouse takes about thirty seconds: open the file, draw your signature in the browser, and download the result. No software to install, no account to create, and your file stays on your device throughout.

## How do I draw a signature on a PDF in my browser?

Open the [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) and upload your file. A drawing canvas loads over the first page. Hold your mouse button and draw your signature across the canvas, the same way you'd sign on paper. When you lift the button, the signature is fixed in place. Hit Clear if the first attempt looks shaky and try again. Slower, deliberate strokes usually produce cleaner results than trying to dash off your name at speed. When you're happy, click Download to save the signed PDF.

The tool is free, needs no sign-up, and your PDF never leaves your browser.

## Is a mouse-drawn signature legally valid?

In most everyday situations, yes. The US [ESIGN Act](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/electronic-signatures-global-national-commerce-act) (2000) gives electronic signatures the same legal standing as handwritten ones for the vast majority of commercial and personal transactions. A mouse-drawn signature counts because it captures your intent to sign.

A few document categories sit outside this rule. Wills, certain real-estate deeds, and court filings in some jurisdictions still require a physical signature. Some regulated industries, such as pharmaceutical companies submitting data under [FDA 21 CFR Part 11](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/part-11-electronic-records-electronic-signatures-scope-and-application), require a certificate-based digital signature rather than a drawn one. For anything in those categories, confirm what your counterparty accepts before sending a mouse-drawn version.

For ordinary contracts, lease agreements, consent forms, and internal approvals, a mouse-drawn signature is fine.

## What is the difference between a mouse-drawn signature and a digital signature?

These two terms get mixed up constantly, but they work in different ways.

A **mouse-drawn signature** (an electronic signature) is an image of your handwriting overlaid on the document. It looks like a signature and satisfies most legal and practical requirements. It does not contain any cryptographic information, so it cannot detect whether someone edits the document after you sign.

A **digital signature** uses a certificate issued by a trusted authority to tie your verified identity to the document. The certificate detects any changes made to the PDF after signing, and the reader's software shows a warning if the file has been altered. This level of assurance is needed in a handful of regulated or high-stakes situations, but most people never encounter those requirements.

If you're signing a freelance contract, a rental application, or a permission slip, a drawn signature is all you need.

## Can I use a trackpad or touch screen instead of a mouse?

Yes. The drawing canvas works with any pointing device: a laptop trackpad, a stylus, a finger on a phone screen, or a graphics tablet. Many people find a large trackpad gives them more control than a mouse because they can rest their hand and draw with their fingertip.

On a Mac, the built-in Preview app also lets you create a trackpad signature. [Apple's support guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/preview/fill-out-and-sign-pdf-forms-prvw35725/mac) walks through the steps. For a browser-based option that works on Windows, Mac, Chromebook, or Linux without downloading anything, the Sign PDF tool covers all platforms.

## What if I need my signature on a page other than the first?

The Sign PDF tool places your signature on the first page of the PDF. If you need it on page 5 of a 10-page contract, here is a clean way to handle it:

1. Go to [Split PDF](/split-pdf) and extract just the page that needs your signature.
2. Open [Sign PDF](/sign-pdf) and sign that single-page file.
3. Go to [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) and combine the pages back in the right order.

All three tools run in your browser and are free to use.

Alternatively, if the PDF already has an interactive signature field placed on the correct page, the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) may place your signature there directly.

## Should I flatten the PDF after signing?

Flattening converts your drawn signature from a separate overlay into a permanent part of the page. Once flattened, the signature cannot be moved, resized, or removed by whoever receives the file.

Two situations where flattening matters:

- **Before merging.** Merging PDFs with our [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) strips interactive form fields from the output. Flattening the signed file first locks the signature image into the page so it survives the merge.
- **Before sending to someone who might edit the file.** If the recipient opens the PDF in an editor, a non-flattened signature might appear as a separate editable object. Flattening removes that possibility.

Use the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) after signing if either scenario applies to you.
