Your resume PDF is too large to upload, and you need to fix it now. Here's how to compress a resume PDF for free in your browser, with no sign-up and no upload to anyone's server.

But first: not all resumes benefit from compression. The method you use matters a lot, and the wrong approach can make a text resume much larger. Read through this before running your file through any tool.

## What's making your resume PDF large?

A plain two-page text resume is usually 100 KB to 400 KB without any effort. If yours is larger, the culprit is almost always one of these:

- **An embedded photo.** A headshot or profile photo in the header, common in European and many Asian CV formats, can add 1 to 3 MB on its own.
- **A graphic design template.** Resume builders and Canva templates embed background graphics, decorative fonts, and color fills that bloat the file.
- **Scanned pages.** If you scanned a paper resume or included scanned certificates, each page is stored as an image rather than text.
- **Embedded font subsets.** Some design apps embed the entire font file rather than just the characters you used, which adds overhead that isn't visible in the layout.

If your resume is plain text with no photos or graphics, it's probably already small enough. The question in that case is whether you're exporting it correctly.

## Try re-exporting from the source first

Before running any compression tool, try this: open your original Word file, Google Doc, or Canva design and export it fresh as a PDF. A direct PDF export from [Microsoft Word](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/save-a-document-as-a-pdf-8a53d09b-0b95-4b91-b97c-82e05dca06ed) or Google Docs compresses images automatically and often cuts file size by 50% or more, compared to printing to PDF or using a browser's "Save as PDF" option.

This is usually the fastest fix for resumes created in document editors. Try it before anything else. It takes 10 seconds and doesn't require any third-party tool.

For resumes, compression helps when:

- Your resume includes a headshot or embedded photos
- You're using a graphic design template with heavy background graphics
- The resume was originally a scan, not a typed document

If your resume fits one of those cases, Medium compression is a good choice. Open the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf), drop your file in, select Medium, and download the result.

## When compression will hurt

Do not use a compression tool on a plain text resume. Because the tool rasterizes every page, it converts vector text into a JPEG image. In our own testing, a 20-page text document went from 23.5 KB to 1,400.8 KB at Medium compression, roughly 60 times larger. A two-page text resume would see the same kind of jump.

There's a second problem with this. Many [applicant tracking systems (ATS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applicant_tracking_system) parse the text layer of a PDF to extract job titles, skills, and dates. If your resume becomes a rasterized image instead, those systems may not be able to read it correctly, and your application could be missed entirely.

For a text resume that's somehow too large, re-export from the source document. That almost always solves it without any compression at all.

## File size limits by job portal

Most job boards accept resumes up to 2 to 5 MB, but specific limits vary:

| Platform | Upload limit |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn Easy Apply | 2 MB |
| Workday | 5 MB (typical, varies by posting) |
| Greenhouse | Up to 100 MB (configured per posting) |
| CareerBuilder | ~1 MB |
| USAJOBS | [5 MB per document](https://help.usajobs.gov/how-to/account/documents/resume) |

These numbers can change, so if you hit a limit on a specific platform, check that platform's help center directly for the current figure.

A good general target: under 500 KB for a text resume, under 2 MB for a design-heavy one with photos or graphics. Most modern hiring portals handle files in that range without issue.
