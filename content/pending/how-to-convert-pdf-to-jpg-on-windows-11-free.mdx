## How to convert a PDF to JPG on Windows 11 for free

Converting a PDF to JPG on Windows 11 is free and takes less than a minute. [The PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) runs directly in Edge, Chrome, or Firefox and turns each page of your PDF into a separate JPG image, with no software to install and no sign-up required.

Here's the whole process:

1. Open your browser on Windows 11 and go to the [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg).
2. Click **Select PDF** and choose the file you want to convert, or drag it onto the page.
3. The tool processes each page in your browser and produces one JPG per page.
4. Download the JPG images when the conversion is done.

Because everything happens inside your browser tab, no file is ever sent to an external server. Windows Defender and your antivirus software see nothing unusual, and your document stays on your device throughout.

## Does Windows 11 have a built-in PDF to JPG converter?

No, Windows 11 doesn't include a dedicated PDF-to-JPG tool. Microsoft Edge is the default PDF reader on Windows 11 and does a good job of displaying PDFs, but it has no export-to-image option. The Photos app can view certain PDFs too, but again, no image export.

If you only need to capture one or two pages, the Snipping Tool is a reasonable workaround:

1. Open your PDF in Microsoft Edge.
2. Press **Windows + Shift + S** to open the Snipping Tool overlay.
3. Draw a rectangle around the page you want and release.
4. Windows copies the screenshot to your clipboard and shows a notification. Click it to save the image as a PNG or JPG.

This works fine for grabbing a single page. For a document with ten or twenty pages, screenshotting each one individually gets old fast. A dedicated converter handles the whole document in one step.

## How to convert a multi-page PDF to JPG on Windows 11

When a PDF has several pages, you want a tool that processes the entire document at once instead of making you handle each page yourself.

The [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) does exactly that: upload your PDF and it converts every page to a separate JPG file in a single pass. You end up with one image per page, numbered in order, ready to download.

If you'd rather have something installed locally for offline use, [GIMP](https://www.gimp.org/) is a free, open-source image editor that can open PDF files and export each page as a JPG. GIMP is more complex than a browser tool, but it works without an internet connection once it's installed.

## How to convert PDF to JPG offline on Windows 11 with GIMP

GIMP is free and has no usage limits. Here's how to use it for a PDF-to-JPG conversion:

1. Download and install [GIMP from gimp.org](https://www.gimp.org/downloads/).
2. Open GIMP, then go to **File > Open** and select your PDF.
3. A dialog asks which pages to import and at what resolution. Set the resolution to at least 150 DPI for readable output; 300 DPI is better for print-quality images.
4. GIMP opens each page as a separate layer or image window.
5. Go to **File > Export As**, name the file with a `.jpg` extension, and confirm the JPEG settings.
6. Repeat the export for each page if you imported multiple pages at once.

GIMP is worth the setup if you convert PDFs to JPG regularly or if you need to do it without an internet connection. For occasional conversions, the browser-based tool is faster.

## How to keep quality high when converting PDF to JPG

[JPG uses lossy compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG), which means some detail is discarded when the image is saved. Here's how to minimize the impact:

- **Always start from the original PDF.** If an earlier conversion looked blurry or blocky, don't try to improve it from the JPG. Go back to the source PDF and convert again. Each round of JPEG encoding compounds quality loss.
- **Use the highest quality setting available.** Higher quality means a larger file, but the images stay sharp. If file size matters, compress only after you're sure the conversion looks right.
- **Set a higher DPI if the tool gives you the option.** 72 DPI is fine for on-screen viewing; 150 DPI or higher is better for printing or embedding in documents.

Documents that are mostly text (invoices, contracts, legal forms) tend to look blocky as JPGs because [JPEG compression handles hard edges poorly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG#Sample_photographs). If text sharpness matters more than compatibility, PNG is a better format than JPG for those documents. The browser tool at [PDF to JPG](/pdf-to-jpg) outputs JPGs, so if you need PNGs you'd use a different tool for that specific case.

## What to do after converting PDF pages to JPG on Windows 11

Once you have your pages as JPG files, a few common next steps:

**Embed in Word or PowerPoint.** Insert the JPG via **Insert > Pictures** in any Office app on Windows 11. This is useful when you need to include a page from a scanned document in a presentation or report.

**Share a single page by email.** Sending one page of a PDF as a JPG attachment is often cleaner than sending the whole PDF when the recipient only needs to see that one page.

**Post to social media or a website.** Most platforms accept JPG uploads directly. Converting a PDF slide or info page to JPG lets you share it on LinkedIn, Instagram, or Twitter/X without a third-party viewer.

**Edit in Paint or Photos.** Windows 11 includes Paint and the Photos editor. Both can crop, annotate, resize, or adjust color on a JPG once you have it as an image file.

**Rebuild as a PDF later.** If you ever want to combine JPG images back into a PDF, the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) handles that in one step. Drop in your JPGs, arrange the order, and download a new PDF.
