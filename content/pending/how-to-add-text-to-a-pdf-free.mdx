You can add text to a PDF free in your browser, no Adobe Acrobat needed. Open the [Edit PDF tool](/edit-pdf), drop in your file, click anywhere on the page, and type. No sign-up, no watermark, no server upload.

That's the short version. The longer version is worth reading because "adding text to a PDF" covers three pretty different tasks, and picking the right tool for your specific situation saves time.

## Why most PDFs look uneditable

A [PDF stores content as a fixed layout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF), not as a flowing document you can click into. When you open a PDF in a browser tab or in Preview on a Mac, you're using a PDF viewer. Viewers show the content correctly, but they don't expose any controls for adding or changing text.

To add text you need a PDF editor. The distinction matters because people often think their file is broken when they can't type into it. It's not. They just need a different tool.

## Three different things people mean by "add text"

Before picking a method, it helps to know which of these you're actually doing:

1. **Type into existing form fields.** The PDF has blank boxes or underlines where text is expected. Clicking them opens a cursor.
2. **Place a text box on any spot.** The PDF has no fields, but you need to write something on the page: your name, a date, a note, a missing piece of information.
3. **Edit existing text.** You want to change words already printed on the page.

Methods 1 and 2 work well with free browser tools. Method 3 is trickier and depends on whether the PDF contains actual text data or a scanned image of text.

## How to add a text box to any PDF

This approach works on any PDF, with or without form fields.

1. Open the [Edit PDF tool](/edit-pdf).
2. Drag your file onto the page, or click to browse and select it. The file loads and stays in your browser.
3. Select the **Add Text** option in the toolbar.
4. Click on the part of the page where you want to type. A text box appears.
5. Type your content. You can drag the box to reposition it or resize it.
6. When you're done, click **Download** to save the file to your device.

The whole process runs in your browser. The file is not uploaded to any server.

## How to fill in a PDF form

If your PDF already has form fields (text boxes, checkboxes, signature lines), use the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) rather than placing free-floating text boxes on top of them.

1. Open the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form).
2. Upload your form.
3. The tool detects form fields automatically. Click a field to type into it.
4. Tab through the remaining fields or click each one.
5. Download the completed form.

Keeping your text inside the official fields matters when the recipient's system reads field values programmatically, which is common with job applications, government forms, and legal contracts. A text box floating over a field looks right to a human but is invisible to automated processing.

## Scanned PDFs: what you can and can't do

A scanned PDF is a photograph of a physical page. The text you see is part of the image, not real characters stored as data. You can still add text boxes on top using the Edit PDF tool, and they'll appear in the correct positions when you save. What you can't do is change the photographed text itself without OCR software.

[OCR (Optical Character Recognition)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_character_recognition) converts the image of text into actual characters the computer can read and edit. That's a separate process, and most free browser tools don't include it.

For the most common scenarios, such as adding your name and date to a scanned form, placing a text box on top of the image is perfectly fine. The saved PDF will contain both the original scanned image and your new typed text.

## Should you flatten after adding text?

Flattening turns your text boxes (and any other annotations) into permanent parts of the page content. They can no longer be selected, moved, or deleted.

Whether you want that depends on the situation:

- **Flatten before sharing**: the recipient's PDF viewer can't accidentally move your text or strip annotations. The document looks the same to everyone.
- **Skip flattening**: you (or a colleague) can still reopen the file and adjust the text later.

Use the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) when you're done editing and ready to share a final version.

## Does it work on Windows, Mac, and mobile?

The browser-based Edit PDF tool works on any device with a modern browser, with no install required.

- **Windows**: Chrome, Edge, or Firefox. Open the tool, drop the file, add text, download.
- **Mac**: Safari, Chrome, or Firefox. Identical steps.
- **iPhone or Android**: Open the tool in your mobile browser. Tap where you want to add a text box. Download the result.

Because the tool runs in the browser, your operating system doesn't matter. The file stays on your device throughout.

## What this tool handles well, and what it doesn't

The Edit PDF tool is built for adding text boxes and annotations, not for heavily restructuring an existing document. If you need to reflow paragraphs, change fonts across a multi-page report, or rewrite large sections, converting to Word and back will give you better results.

For quick additions such as a name, a date, a missing field, a short annotation, or a few lines of text on any page, the browser tool gets the job done without the overhead of installing software or creating an account.
