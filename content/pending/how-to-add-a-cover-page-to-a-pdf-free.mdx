Adding a cover page to a PDF for free takes three steps: create your cover, convert it to PDF if it's an image, then merge it in front of your existing document. No Adobe Acrobat required, nothing to install.

## The three-step workflow

You need two PDFs: your cover page and your main document. Here's how to get both and combine them.

### Step 1: Get or create your cover page

If you already have a cover page saved as a PDF, you're ready to go.

If you have a JPG or PNG image, use the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) to turn it into a single-page PDF. Upload the image, choose a page size that matches your main document (A4 or US Letter), and download the result.

If you want a simple title page with your document name, author, and date, create it in any word processor. Google Docs, Microsoft Word, Apple Pages, and LibreOffice all work. Design the page, then export or print it to PDF using the built-in PDF export option on your device.

### Step 2: Open the Merge PDF tool

Go to the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool and upload your files. Add the cover page PDF first, then add your main document.

### Step 3: Set the order and download

The tool lists files in the order they'll appear in the final PDF. Confirm the cover is at the top, click Merge, and download the combined file. Your cover becomes page one, followed by every page of the original.

This works in any modern browser, including Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Edge. No account needed.

## What happens to your existing content?

Nothing in the original pages changes. Your text, images, and layout carry through exactly as they were. The cover is placed at the front and the rest follows.

There's one thing to know if your document has fillable form fields: merging strips [interactive PDF form fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF#Interactive_elements) from the output. The page content survives, but the fields themselves do not.

If your main PDF has filled-in form fields you want to preserve, flatten the document first. The [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) bakes the field values into the page as regular content so they can't be lost during a merge. After flattening, merge as usual. The same applies if your cover page is itself a filled form.

## Does the file size go up a lot?

Only by the size of the cover page. In measured testing, combining PDFs totaling 971.9 KB produced an output of 970.5 KB, just under the combined input, because shared PDF structure is written once rather than duplicated. Adding a cover page won't push you over an email size limit in any meaningful way beyond what the cover file itself adds.

## How to design a cover page worth using

A cover page that reads well usually has:

- A clear title in a large font
- A subtitle or short description (optional but useful for reports)
- The author name and date
- A logo or background image if relevant

Canva has free templates sized for A4 and US Letter. Google Slides and Microsoft PowerPoint work too. Design your cover, export it as PDF or a high-resolution JPG, and you're set. If you export as JPG, run it through the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) before merging.

For a plain title page with no graphics, a word processor is usually the fastest option. Type the title, center it vertically, export to PDF, merge. It takes under two minutes.

## Why you don't need Adobe Acrobat

[Adobe Acrobat](https://www.adobe.com/acrobat.html) has a cover-page generator with built-in design templates, but it's a paid subscription tool. The free approach here achieves the same end result: a PDF with your cover as the first page.

The trade-off is that with the free approach you design the cover in a separate app. That's one extra step, but it gives you more control over the design and doesn't lock you into a template library.

## Does this work on a phone?

Yes. Both the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) and [Images to PDF](/jpg-to-pdf) tools run in mobile browsers without any app installation. On an iPhone, you can create a cover in Pages, share it as a PDF to your browser, then merge. On Android, the same workflow works in Chrome.

Your files are processed entirely within your browser and are never sent to a server. That matters for anything sensitive, such as a contract, a medical form, or a client report.

## Replacing an existing cover page

If your PDF already has a cover page you want to swap out, the process has one extra step. Use the [Delete Pages tool](/delete-pages) to remove the existing first page, download the updated PDF, then merge your new cover in front of it using [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf). The rest of the document stays exactly as it was.

This workflow also works if you want to update a cover page with a new date or a revised title. Remove the old one, create the new one, merge them back together.
