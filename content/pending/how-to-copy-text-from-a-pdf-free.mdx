You can copy text from a PDF for free right now, without installing anything. Open the file in Chrome, Firefox, Edge, or Safari, click and drag to select the text you want, then press Ctrl+C on Windows or Command+C on Mac. Paste with Ctrl+V.

That covers most PDFs. The cases below are for when that doesn't work.

## Why can't I select text in my PDF?

Three things cause this, and diagnosing the right one saves you time:

**The PDF is a scan.** Someone printed a document and fed it through a scanner. The result is a photograph of text, not real text characters. Nothing to select, because there's no text layer in the file.

**Copy restrictions are set.** The author applied permissions that block selection and copying, even though the text is present in the file. Your viewer displays the words normally, but the selection tool does nothing.

**Wrong viewer mode.** Many PDF readers open in a scroll/hand mode. The cursor looks like a grabbing hand and drags the page instead of selecting text. Switch to the text selection cursor (usually a "T" or cursor icon in the toolbar) and try again.

To tell which you're dealing with: try clicking on a word. If a text cursor appears but won't let you drag, restrictions are likely the issue. If the cursor never changes from a hand even after you switch modes, you're looking at a scanned image.

## How to copy text from a standard (digital) PDF

If the PDF was created digitally (from Word, Google Docs, a PDF printer, etc.), text selection usually just works in a browser. Here are the basic moves:

- **A sentence or paragraph:** click at the start, hold Shift, click at the end.
- **A single word:** double-click it.
- **All text on the page:** click in the document area, then Ctrl+A / Command+A.

Copy with Ctrl+C or Command+C, then paste anywhere. If your browser's PDF viewer is giving you trouble, try a different one. Chrome and Firefox both have solid built-in viewers that handle most files.

## How to copy text from a scanned PDF for free

A scanned PDF has no text layer. You need [OCR (optical character recognition)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_character_recognition) to create one. Here are three free ways:

**Google Drive.** Upload the PDF to [Google Drive](https://drive.google.com), right-click the file, and choose "Open with Google Docs." Google processes the scan and opens a new document with the text extracted. Copy from there. This is free and works in any browser with no sign-up beyond a Google account.

**Google Lens.** Use the [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) to convert your PDF pages into image files. Then open each image in [Google Lens](https://lens.google.com/) (available on Android, iOS, and via the Google app on desktop). Tap or click "Text" to extract and copy the recognized text. Best for a handful of pages.

**Online OCR tools.** Several free web tools accept a scanned PDF directly and return a text layer. iLovePDF's OCR feature and Adobe Acrobat's free web tier both work without installing software, though they require an upload.

OCR accuracy depends on scan quality. A clean, high-contrast scan at 300 dpi or higher gives near-perfect results. A blurry, skewed, or low-contrast page will miss words and add errors. Always read through the output before using it.

## How to copy text from a restricted PDF for free

If the PDF has copy restrictions, the text exists in the file but your viewer won't let you touch it. If you have the owner password or written permission to remove the restrictions, use the [Unlock PDF](/unlock-pdf) tool. It removes the password and restrictions in your browser, and the file never leaves your device. After that, open the result and copy normally.

If you don't have the password, there's no free way to remove the restrictions, and this guide doesn't cover bypassing them without authorization. Restrictions exist for a reason, and removing them without permission isn't something to pursue.

## How to copy text from a PDF on a phone

**iPhone.** Open the PDF in Files, Mail, or Safari. Tap and hold a word until the selection handles appear, then drag to cover what you need. Tap Copy. If tapping doesn't give you selection handles, the PDF is a scan. Share it to Google Drive and use the Google Docs method above.

**Android.** Open the PDF in Chrome or Files. Long-press a word, drag the handles to extend the selection, tap Copy. Same rule applies: if you can't select, the file is image-based and needs OCR.

## How to copy all text from a PDF at once

If you want everything in the file rather than a section, this works for digital PDFs:

1. Open the file in your browser.
2. Click anywhere inside the document area.
3. Press Ctrl+A (Windows/Linux) or Command+A (Mac) to select all.
4. Press Ctrl+C or Command+C to copy.
5. Paste into a plain-text editor or document.

Heads up: pasting directly into Word or Google Docs can import a mess of extra line breaks and formatting. Pasting into Notepad (Windows) or TextEdit in plain-text mode (Mac) first strips the formatting to clean text. Then copy from there into your destination.

For PDFs with complex layouts (two-column articles, tables, forms), copying all at once usually scrambles the reading order. Copying section by section and cleaning up in a text editor takes more time but gives a cleaner result.

If the Ctrl+A shortcut doesn't respond, try right-clicking inside the document area and looking for a "Select all" option, or switch to [Adobe Acrobat Reader](https://get.adobe.com/reader/) (free) which handles this reliably across file types.
