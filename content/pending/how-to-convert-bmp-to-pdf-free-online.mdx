Converting a BMP file to PDF is straightforward, but it takes one extra step most guides skip: you need to convert the BMP to a JPG or PNG first, then create the PDF from that image. Most online PDF tools, including browser-based ones, don't accept raw BMP files directly. Here's every method that works.

## What is a BMP file?

[BMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP_file_format) stands for Bitmap, an image format Microsoft introduced alongside the original Windows graphics system. BMP stores pixel data with no compression at all, which means every pixel gets its own entry in the file regardless of whether adjacent pixels are identical.

That uncompressed design makes BMP files large. A 1920x1080 screenshot saved as BMP is typically 5 to 6 MB. The same image as a JPG is around 200 to 400 KB. As a PNG it might be 1 to 2 MB. This size difference is why most document systems, email providers, and online portals refuse BMP uploads or hit size limits quickly.

Converting to PDF solves both problems: it produces a compact, portable file that any device can open.

## Method 1: Windows Paint (fastest on Windows)

Windows Paint handles BMP natively and can re-save to JPG or PNG in one step.

1. Right-click your BMP file and choose **Open with > Paint**.
2. Go to **File > Save as** and choose **JPEG picture** or **PNG picture**.
3. Save the file and note the location.
4. Open the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) in your browser.
5. Click to upload the JPG or PNG file.
6. Download the finished PDF.

If you have several BMP files, convert them all to JPG in Paint first, then upload the batch to the Images to PDF tool. You can drag to reorder the images before generating the single PDF.

## Method 2: Mac Preview (built into macOS)

Preview opens BMP files without any extra apps.

1. Double-click the BMP to open it in Preview.
2. Go to **File > Export**.
3. Set the format to **JPEG** or **PNG** and click **Save**.
4. Open the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) in your browser.
5. Upload the exported image and download the PDF.

There is a faster shortcut too. In Preview, go to **File > Print**, click the **PDF** dropdown in the bottom-left corner, and choose **Save as PDF**. This skips the image export entirely and gives you a PDF in one step, though the file size will be larger than the JPG path.

## Method 3: Chrome or Edge (any operating system)

Modern browsers can open BMP files directly and print them to PDF without any plugins or sign-ups. This works on Windows, Mac, Chromebook, and Linux.

1. Drag the BMP file onto a new Chrome or Edge tab. The image appears in the browser window.
2. Press **Ctrl+P** (Windows/Linux) or **Cmd+P** (Mac) to open the print dialog.
3. Set the destination to **Save as PDF**.
4. Click **Save** and choose a folder.

The result is a lossless PDF. It will be larger than a JPG-compressed version, but the image quality is identical to the original BMP.

## Method 4: Upload a JPG or PNG directly

If the image that ended up as a BMP was originally photographed or scanned, check whether a JPG or PNG version already exists on your device. Many cameras and phones save in both formats. If a JPG version is available, skip the conversion step entirely. Open the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf), upload the JPG, and download the PDF.

The tool runs in your browser. No account required and no file leaves your device to a third-party server for processing.

For sharing by email or uploading to a form, the JPG path produces the smallest PDF. For archiving or printing where pixel accuracy matters, use PNG as the intermediate format or print directly to PDF from your browser.

## Why convert BMP to PDF at all?

BMP is a fine format for editing in image software, but it's a poor format for sending or storing documents. Most upload fields on job sites, government portals, and university systems expect PDF or JPG. PDF in particular is the standard for multi-page documents and is defined by [ISO 32000](https://pdfa.org/resource/pdf-specification-index/), meaning any device with a PDF reader, including phones, tablets, and printers, can open it reliably.

PDF also lets you combine several BMP images into one file. If you have a set of scanned pages saved as BMP, convert them all to JPG and upload them together to the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf). You get a single, ordered document instead of a folder of separate image files.

One thing worth keeping in mind: if the BMP is a scanned document (a form, a letter, an invoice), the resulting PDF will be an image-only file. Text inside the scan won't be selectable or searchable. For that, you'd need an OCR tool to make the text extractable. But for most purposes, sharing a scan as a PDF is perfectly fine.
