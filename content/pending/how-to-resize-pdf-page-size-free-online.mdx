You can resize a PDF page size in your browser for free, but the right method depends on what you're actually trying to do. If you want to trim white borders and make pages physically smaller, the [Crop PDF tool](/crop-pdf) handles that without uploading anything. If you need to convert between paper standards like A4 and US Letter, the free print-to-PDF trick in Chrome or Edge takes less than a minute.

## What "resize PDF page size" actually means

People use "resize" to mean two distinct things, and picking the wrong method wastes time.

**Cropping margins** trims the visible area of each page. The canvas gets smaller by removing white space around the edges. Nothing inside the document is redrawn. If your document has wide white borders you want to remove, or a scanned page has a thick black frame from the scanner lid, this is what you need.

**Changing the paper standard** (A4, US Letter, Legal, A5) adjusts the canvas to match a target size. If your content was laid out for one paper size and needs to print on another, this is the correct operation. Content may scale to fill the new canvas, or get repositioned, depending on the tool.

There's a third thing people sometimes mean by "resize": making the file smaller in bytes. That's [compression](/compress-pdf), a completely separate operation. Compression reduces storage size. Page-size changes alter dimensions. They don't overlap.

## How to trim PDF margins free

The [Crop PDF tool](/crop-pdf) trims margins off every page in your browser. Files never leave your device.

1. Open the [Crop PDF tool](/crop-pdf).
2. Drop your PDF onto the page.
3. Set how much to trim from each edge (top, bottom, left, right).
4. Download the result.

Every page gets the same trim applied, so it works best when your whole document has consistent borders. The content inside doesn't change, and text stays crisp because nothing is re-rendered.

One thing to know: cropping in PDF hides the trimmed area rather than permanently deleting it. The hidden content lives in the file but falls outside the visible page box. For reading, printing, and sharing this is irrelevant. But if you hand the file to a sophisticated tool that reads all PDF boxes, the trimmed content could reappear. For everyday use, this distinction doesn't matter.

## How to convert PDF paper size from A4 to Letter (or vice versa)

[A4 and US Letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_216) are close but not the same. A4 measures 210 by 297 mm, while US Letter is 8.5 by 11 inches (about 216 by 279 mm). A4 is taller and narrower. A document formatted for A4 will show small white strips at the sides when printed on Letter paper, unless you scale it to fit.

The easiest free method uses the browser's built-in print-to-PDF feature. No software download, no sign-up, works on Windows, Mac, and Chromebook:

1. Open the PDF in Chrome, Edge, or Firefox.
2. Press Ctrl+P (or Cmd+P on Mac).
3. Set the printer to "Save as PDF" or "Microsoft Print to PDF".
4. Click "More settings" and choose your target Paper size (A4 or Letter).
5. Under Scale, choose "Fit to page" so content fills the new canvas without clipping.
6. Click Save.

The output is a new PDF at the correct page size. This approach re-renders each page, which means you get a fresh canvas at the right dimensions.

One trade-off worth knowing: print-to-PDF re-renders pages as images, which means the searchable text layer from the original is lost. If you need to copy text out of the resized document, look for a dedicated PDF page resizer that scales the vector content directly. For printing, presentations, and sharing, the browser method is perfectly fine.

## Will content get cut off when I resize a PDF?

It depends on the method.

**Cropping** can hide content if you trim too aggressively. If text or images sit close to the edge, they fall outside the new visible area. Most crop tools let you preview the result before downloading. Check the edges of a representative page before committing.

**Paper-size conversion via print** won't cut off content as long as you choose "Fit to page" in the print dialog. The content scales up or down to fill the new canvas proportionally. If you choose "Actual size" instead, anything that extends past the new paper edge gets clipped, so always use Fit to page for paper-size swaps.

## How to make all pages the same size

PDFs can contain pages of different dimensions, which is common when combining files from different sources. A cover letter might be US Letter while an attachment scanned in Europe is A4.

The fastest free fix: open the PDF in Chrome, print to PDF with "Fit to page" and your target paper size. Every page gets the same canvas in one pass.

If you're starting with separate documents at different sizes, combine them first with [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf), then run the print-to-PDF step to standardize the pages. The merge output keeps the original page sizes intact, so the standardization step should come second.

## When to use compression instead

If the goal is fitting a PDF into an email limit or reducing a file for upload, you don't need to change the page size at all. The [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) reduces file size without touching page dimensions.

Compression works best on PDFs that are mostly images or scanned pages. For text-only documents, it actually increases file size because the tool rasterizes every page to JPEG, turning compact text vectors into image data. If your PDF is a contract, invoice, or text report, try splitting it or zipping it before compressing.

## Browser tools vs. desktop software

The crop and print-to-PDF methods work on every major operating system, cost nothing, and take seconds for single files. They cover the vast majority of resize jobs.

For more advanced needs like resizing individual pages to different sizes within a single document, or processing hundreds of files in a batch, desktop tools give more control. But for one-off page-size fixes, the free browser approach is the fastest path to the result.
