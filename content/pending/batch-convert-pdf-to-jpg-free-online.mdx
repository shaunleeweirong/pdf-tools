## What "batch" means here

"Batch convert" covers two different jobs people want to do:

**All pages of one PDF.** This is the main use case, and the one the tool handles in a single pass. A 10-page proposal becomes 10 images. A 50-page report becomes 50. You get a numbered set that maps directly to the original pages.

**Multiple separate PDF files.** If you have several different PDFs and want JPGs from each, the tool handles one file at a time. Run the first PDF through, download the images, then repeat for the next. There is no queue limit, no account gate, and no slowdown after a certain number of conversions. For occasional use, this is fast enough; for processing hundreds of files at once, a desktop tool or command-line script would be more efficient.

## Free vs paid batch converters: what is actually different

Most tools that advertise "batch PDF to JPG" have a free tier with restrictions. Common catches:

- **Page caps.** Free tier converts up to N pages, then asks you to upgrade.
- **Watermarks.** The output has the tool's logo stamped on every image.
- **Account required.** You have to register before you can download anything.
- **Server upload.** Your file leaves your device and sits on their infrastructure.

The [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) has none of those. All pages convert, no watermarks appear, and your file stays local. The only limitation is processing one PDF at a time.

## Page-to-image vs extracting embedded images

These are two different operations that people sometimes confuse.

**Converting pages to JPG** renders the entire visual appearance of each page as a flat [JPEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG) image. Text, graphics, backgrounds, and annotations all get baked into the image. The output is a pixel-for-pixel representation of what the page looks like on screen. This is what the PDF to JPG tool does.

**Extracting embedded images** retrieves only the image objects stored inside the [PDF file structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF). A slide deck saved as PDF might contain 20 photos embedded at full resolution. The Extract tool returns those 20 photos. But it does not return slide backgrounds, text, or any content that was not already an image object in the file.

Which one you want depends on your goal. If you want to share or display the pages as they look in the PDF, convert pages to JPG. If you want to recover the original photos or graphics that were embedded in the source document, use the [Extract Images tool](/extract-images) instead.

## Are your files private when you convert online?

Most online converters receive your file, process it on their servers, and send the result back. The file exists on their infrastructure during that time. If you are converting a contract, a medical record, a tax document, or any other sensitive PDF, that is worth thinking about.

This tool processes everything in your browser, using your device's own resources. Your file is never transmitted anywhere. Close the tab and it is gone.

## When converting PDF pages to JPG makes sense

A few situations where image output is the right call:

**Posting pages on social media.** Most platforms display JPGs inline. PDFs usually show up as a download link, if they show up at all.

**Embedding a document page in a presentation.** PowerPoint and Google Slides take JPGs directly. Dropping in a PDF is more complicated and behaves inconsistently across systems.

**Sending a page as an inline image in an email.** A JPG shows up in the email body. A PDF requires the recipient to open an attachment.

**Sharing a single page from a report.** If you want one chart or one slide as a standalone file, a JPG is easier to pass around than the full PDF.

**Working with scanned documents.** If the PDF is already a scan of paper, each page is effectively a photograph. Converting to JPG changes the container but not the content.

## When to keep the PDF

Converting to JPG makes each page a photograph of itself. The text is no longer selectable or searchable. Hyperlinks stop working. Form fields and bookmarks disappear. File size can increase when a single compact PDF becomes a collection of images.

Keep the PDF when anyone needs to read, search, or copy content from the document, or when it needs to look consistent across different printers and screens. [PDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) was designed for exactly that job, which is why it remains the default for contracts, reports, and official records.

Convert to JPG when the visual appearance of the page is what you want to share, not the document itself.
