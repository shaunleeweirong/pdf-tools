The best free PDF scanner apps that don't require an account are already on your phone. On iPhone, the Notes app has a built-in document scanner. On Android, Google Drive does the same. Neither requires signing up for anything new. If you want a dedicated app, Genius Scan and Notebloc both scan without asking for an email address.

That's the short answer. The rest of this covers why so many scanner apps require accounts, what each option can and cannot do, and a faster route if you already have phone photos of your document.

## Which popular scanner apps actually require an account?

It helps to know what you're avoiding before you install anything.

**Adobe Scan** asks for an Adobe ID before the camera even opens. You can create a free Adobe account, but you cannot take one scan without logging in. Files are saved to Adobe's cloud automatically, which is useful for sync but means your document leaves your device.

**Microsoft Lens** requires a Microsoft account for most save and export options. If your phone is already signed into a Microsoft account, this feels invisible. If it isn't, you hit a login screen before you can export anything useful.

Both apps are genuinely capable. The account requirement isn't a catch. It's how they sync your scans across devices and apply OCR in the cloud. But if you need a one-off scan and don't want to hand over an email address, there are better options.

**CamScanner** used to be a popular no-account choice, but it has moved toward requiring a login for cloud sync and now adds watermarks on some exports in the free tier. It's no longer the easy recommendation it once was.

## Your phone already has a scanner built in

Before installing anything, check what's already there.

**iPhone** has a document scanner inside the Notes app. Open Notes, tap the pencil icon to start a new note, then tap the camera icon and choose "Scan Documents." The app auto-detects document edges and corrects the perspective. Tap "Save" when you're done, then tap the scan and use the share icon to export as a PDF. No extra apps, no accounts, and no cost.

**Android** has the same capability inside Google Drive. Tap the blue "+" button in the lower-right corner and choose "Scan." You get automatic edge detection, a cropping adjustment, and a PDF saved directly to your Drive. Since you're almost certainly already signed into a Google account on your Android phone, this doesn't require creating anything new.

Both work well for flat documents under reasonable lighting. For multi-page documents, you can keep adding pages before saving.

## The best third-party scanner apps without sign-up

If you want more control than the built-in options give you, a few apps handle this well:

**Genius Scan** (iPhone and Android) is consistently the most-recommended no-account option across user forums. The free version handles scanning, auto-cropping, and PDF export without watermarks. The paid upgrade adds cloud sync and OCR, but the core scan-to-PDF flow is free and login-free.

**Notebloc** (Android) is a solid pick if you prefer a document-focused layout. It's free with no watermarks and no sign-in required, though the app does show ads. It also includes [OCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_character_recognition) for about 18 languages in the free tier, which is more than most free apps offer.

**NAPS2** is worth knowing about if you're scanning from a flatbed or sheet-fed scanner on a desktop computer. It's [open-source, free, and requires no account](https://www.naps2.com/). It supports OCR, multi-page documents, and several export formats. Not a phone app, but the best desktop option if you want to avoid installing driver-heavy manufacturer software.

## Already have photos? You don't need a scanner app

If you took photos of each page with your phone's default camera app, you already have what you need. You can combine those images into a PDF in your browser with the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf). Drop in your JPGs or PNGs, put them in the right order, and download the result. No account, no install, and your files never leave your device.

This is often faster than using a dedicated scanner app, particularly when you already photographed the document or received the images in a text message or email.

The difference between a scanner app scan and a regular phone photo for PDF purposes is mainly perspective correction. Scanner apps detect document edges and warp the image so the result looks like a flat, straight scan rather than a slightly angled photo. For most use cases (forms, receipts, letters), the difference is minor. For something where precision matters (legal documents, official records), a scanner app's auto-correction is worth having.

## What about OCR and text recognition?

OCR turns a scanned image into selectable, searchable text. Most free no-sign-up apps either skip OCR entirely or put it behind a paid upgrade.

If you need searchable text, the honest options are Microsoft Lens (requires a Microsoft account but OCR is excellent and free) or Adobe Scan (same trade-off). Google Drive's scanner also runs OCR when you open the resulting PDF inside Google Docs.

If you only need a PDF image of the document without searchable text, the no-sign-up apps are fine. Most everyday scanning tasks (a receipt, a form, a signed letter) don't need OCR.

## Getting scanned PDFs under size limits

Phone cameras produce large image files, and a multi-page scan can easily reach several megabytes. If you need to email the result or upload to a form with a file size cap, the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) can reduce the size in your browser. It works best on PDFs that are mostly scans or photos. It's not suitable for text-only PDFs, which can get bigger rather than smaller after compression, because the tool rasterizes pages to JPEG format.

One pass at a reasonable compression level is usually enough. Running the same file through again at the same level makes it larger rather than smaller. If one pass doesn't get you where you need to be, try restarting from the original file at a higher compression setting.

## Choosing what's right for your situation

Start with what's already on your phone. The built-in scanners in iOS Notes and Google Drive handle most everyday scanning tasks without installing anything or creating a new account. For more control without a sign-up, Genius Scan covers the common cases on both iPhone and Android.

If you're starting from photos rather than a live scan, skip the scanner apps and use a browser-based [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) to turn them into a PDF directly.
