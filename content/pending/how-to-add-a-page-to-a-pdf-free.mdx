You can add a page to a PDF for free without installing any software. The fastest route is the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf): upload your existing file and the page you want to add, arrange them in order, and download. No account, no watermark, and it runs in your browser on any device.

The exact steps depend on what you're adding, so here's how each scenario works.

## How to add pages from another PDF

If the page you want to insert already exists in a second PDF, merging is the right approach.

1. Open the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf).
2. Upload your original document.
3. Click the add-file button to upload the second PDF.
4. Drag the thumbnails into the order you need. You can drop the new file anywhere in the sequence, including in the middle.
5. Click Merge and download.

The output is nearly identical in size to the combined inputs. When we measured our own tool, three PDFs totalling 971.9 KB merged into a 970.5 KB file. Combining documents won't push you over an email attachment limit unless you were already right at the edge.

## How to add a photo or scan as a new page

If the page you want to add is a JPEG or PNG (a scan, a photo, a handwritten note, a signed page), convert it to a single-page PDF first, then merge.

1. Open the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf).
2. Upload your image. JPG and PNG both work.
3. Download the one-page PDF.
4. Go to the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf), upload your original and the image PDF, arrange them in order, and download.

This two-step process handles a lot of real-world situations: attaching a handwritten note to a typed report, adding a signature page to a contract, or including a receipt in an expense document.

## How to insert a page in the middle of a PDF

When you load both PDFs into the Merge tool, you can drag the page thumbnails into any sequence before clicking Merge. The new content can land anywhere, not just at the start or end.

If your original document is long and you need to insert content at a precise point, a slightly longer path works well: use the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) to divide the original at the insertion point, add your new page, then merge the three pieces (first half, new page, second half) in order. It takes an extra step, but it keeps the page order clean.

## How to add a blank page to a PDF

There's no blank-page button in the Merge tool, but a quick workaround gets the job done.

1. Open the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf).
2. Upload a small white or solid-colour image. A screenshot of a blank document, or any single-colour image from your phone's camera roll, works fine.
3. Download the resulting one-page PDF.
4. Merge it into your document at whatever position you need.

A blank page is useful as a section divider, a spacer for double-sided printing, or a placeholder you'll fill in later.

## What happens to form fields when you merge?

[PDF form fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF#Interactive_elements) are stored in a separate structure inside the file (called the AcroForm). When you merge PDFs, that structure doesn't carry over. Page content, text, images, and layout all survive intact, but fillable fields don't make it through.

This matters most when you've filled in a form and want to attach it to another document. If you merge the filled form directly, the values disappear with the fields.

The fix is to flatten the form first. Use the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) before you merge. Flattening converts the field values into regular page content (static text and graphics), so the answers are preserved in the merged output. It adds one step, but it's the reliable path.

## What if I need to add content to an existing page, not a new page?

Merging adds complete pages. If you need to place a text box, an image, or a signature on an existing page rather than inserting a whole new one, a different tool fits better.

- To add text or labels: [Edit PDF tool](/edit-pdf)
- To place a drawn signature: [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf)
- To stamp a watermark across all pages: [Watermark PDF tool](/watermark-pdf)
- To number pages after you've assembled the document: [Add Page Numbers tool](/add-page-numbers)

## Does merging change the page order if I get it wrong?

Yes, but there's no penalty for re-doing it. The Merge tool combines files in the order you set, and if the result isn't right you can run it again from the original files. Merging doesn't degrade quality or change the PDF's content in any way beyond the page structure, so starting over costs nothing.
