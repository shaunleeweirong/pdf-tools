The fastest way to annotate a PDF free online is to open it in Chrome or Microsoft Edge. Both browsers let you highlight text, add sticky notes, and draw on the page with no account and nothing to install.

This guide covers each type of annotation, what to do when the tools are greyed out, and how to make sure your markup stays put when you share the file.

## What annotating a PDF actually means

"Annotating" is an umbrella term for several different kinds of markup you can add on top of a PDF without changing the original text underneath:

- **Highlights**: coloring a span of text to mark it for review
- **Sticky notes and comments**: floating notes that attach to a spot on the page
- **Text boxes**: typed text placed at a precise location on the page
- **Freehand drawings**: pen strokes, circles, arrows, shapes

Most free tools handle highlighting well. Where they differ is text boxes and drawings. Keep that in mind when you choose your tool below.

## How to highlight and comment in Chrome or Edge

Chrome and Edge include PDF annotation built into the browser. No extensions, no sign-up.

**In Chrome:**

1. Drag your PDF onto a new Chrome tab, or press Ctrl+O (Cmd+O on Mac) and open it.
2. Select text by clicking and dragging. A small toolbar appears with a Highlight option.
3. Click Highlight. Right-click the highlight to change color or add a note.
4. To save: press Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on Mac), choose "Save as PDF" as the destination, and click Save.

The exported file has your highlights baked in as permanent content.

**In Microsoft Edge:**

Edge has a fuller annotation panel. Click the pen icon in the PDF toolbar to enter markup mode. You get multiple highlight colors, a sticky note tool, and a freehand drawing pen. [Microsoft's PDF annotation guide for Edge](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/annotate-pdfs-in-microsoft-edge-f1b83e77-e0e8-4a30-8b7c-3c7c8c87b4f8) covers each tool.

Press Ctrl+S to save with annotations embedded, or print to PDF for a flattened copy.

Both browsers work on Windows, Mac, and Chromebook. Files never leave your device.

## How to add text boxes and typed labels to a PDF

Highlights float above the page, which is fine for reading notes. But sometimes you need a typed correction, a label on a diagram, or a comment sitting next to a specific paragraph.

The [Edit PDF tool](/edit-pdf) handles this in your browser. Drop in your PDF, click anywhere on the page, and type. Drag the box to position it exactly where you want it.

Steps:

1. Open the [Edit PDF tool](/edit-pdf) and drop your file in.
2. Select the text tool and click on the page where you want to add text.
3. Type your annotation and adjust the size if needed.
4. Click Download to get the annotated file.

Your file is processed locally, so it does not upload to any server.

## How to draw on a PDF free

Freehand drawing is good for circling something, sketching an arrow pointing at a specific element, or marking up a diagram.

Edge handles this natively: open markup mode and pick the pen tool. Set color and line thickness, then draw directly on the page. Save with Ctrl+S.

On Mac, Apple Preview includes a full annotation toolbar with a drawing pen, shapes, and arrows. Open your PDF in Preview, click the markup toolbar button (the pencil icon), and choose the tool you need.

Drawings from both tools save with the PDF. They're part of the annotation layer, which means someone else could remove them in another editor. Read the next section to lock them in place.

## How to make annotations permanent before sharing

Annotations, highlights, and text boxes are stored as a separate layer inside the PDF. That's useful while you're reviewing, because you can go back and adjust them. But before sharing a marked-up document, you probably want those annotations locked down so the recipient sees exactly what you intended.

The [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) converts every annotation, drawing, form field, and text box into static page content. The markup looks identical but can't be moved, edited, or deleted. The [ISO 32000 PDF specification](https://pdfa.org/resource/pdf-specification-index/) defines annotations as a distinct data structure from page content, which is why flattening is a separate step rather than happening automatically on save.

Steps to flatten after annotating:

1. Finish your annotations and save the PDF to your device.
2. Open the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) and drop the file in.
3. Download the result.

Your markup is now part of the page, not a removable layer on top.

## What to do when you cannot annotate a PDF

Two situations cause annotation tools to stop working.

**The PDF has restriction permissions.** Some documents are set by their authors to block markup, even if you can open and read them. This is a layer of [PDF permission security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF#Security_and_signatures) separate from an open password. When tools are greyed out but you can select text, this is usually why.

The simplest workaround: open the file in Chrome, press Ctrl+P, and save it as a new PDF. The exported copy does not carry the original permission flags, so annotation tools work normally on it.

**The PDF is a scanned document.** Scanned PDFs are photos of pages. There are no text characters for a highlighter to attach to. You can draw on a scan or add text boxes over it, but proper text highlighting requires OCR (optical character recognition) to convert the image to real text first.

[Google Drive offers free OCR](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/176692): upload the scanned PDF to Drive, right-click it, and choose "Open with Google Docs." Drive processes the scan and creates a document with editable text. You can then re-export it to PDF and annotate it like any other file.

## Which method to use

Most situations come down to this:

- **Quick highlight or note on any device**: open the PDF in Chrome or Edge. Done.
- **Typed text at a specific location on the page**: use the [Edit PDF tool](/edit-pdf).
- **Sending a marked-up file to someone**: flatten it with the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) first so the annotations can't be removed.
- **Greyed-out tools**: print to a new PDF in Chrome to strip restriction flags.
- **Scanned document**: run OCR in Google Drive before annotating.

The browser-native options cover most needs without any account or upload. Add the Edit PDF and Flatten tools for the cases where you need more control over placement or permanence.
