ChatGPT can read and analyze a PDF by letting you attach the file directly in the chat window, and the free plan supports this. You attach the PDF, ask your question, and the model reads the content before answering. No subscription is required to start.

If you'd rather not create an account, there's a simpler path: copy the text from the PDF and paste it straight into any AI chat box. That skips file limits entirely.

## How to upload a PDF to ChatGPT

1. Go to [chat.openai.com](https://chat.openai.com) and sign in with a free account.
2. Start a new conversation.
3. Click the paperclip icon in the message box.
4. Choose "Upload from computer" and select your PDF. You can also link from Google Drive or OneDrive.
5. Type your question or instruction in the same message, then send.

ChatGPT reads the uploaded file and answers based on its contents. You can keep asking follow-up questions in the same thread without re-uploading.

## What ChatGPT can do with your PDF

Once the file is uploaded, you can ask the model to do quite a lot:

- Summarize the document in a few paragraphs or a bulleted list
- Answer questions about specific sections, figures, or claims
- Extract structured data like names, dates, and dollar amounts
- Rewrite a section in plain language or at a different reading level
- Translate passages into another language
- Compare two PDFs if you upload both in the same conversation

Specific prompts get better results than vague ones. "List the three main risks in section 4 as bullet points" will outperform "what does this say?"

## File size limits and daily caps

[ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT) accepts files up to 512 MB each. Text documents, including PDFs, also face a separate ceiling of roughly 2 million tokens. That works out to about 1,500 pages for a typical document, so most PDFs stay well under both limits.

The more pressing constraint on the free plan is the daily upload quota: you can upload 3 files per day. Once your GPT-4o access for the day is used up, document analysis may not be available until the quota resets. ChatGPT Plus subscribers get up to 80 uploads every three hours, and as of early 2026 OpenAI raised the per-message cap to 20 files on web.

## What to do when a PDF is too large

When ChatGPT rejects your file or the upload stalls, the fix depends on what the PDF contains.

**Image-heavy PDFs** include scanned documents, photo portfolios, and presentations full of full-bleed graphics. These are large because every page is a raster image. Running the file through the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) before uploading can shrink it significantly. In testing on a 6-page image deck, High compression brought a 913.4 KB file down to 175.6 KB, a reduction of more than 80%.

**Text-based PDFs** (contracts, reports, invoices, filled forms) should not be compressed before uploading. The compressor works by rasterizing each page to JPEG, which means a text document gets bigger rather than smaller. In testing, a 23.5 KB text report grew to 1.4 MB after compression. Use the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) instead: divide the document into chunks of 20 to 30 pages and upload the parts in order. Mention in your first message that the document is arriving in parts and ask ChatGPT to wait for all sections before summarizing.

A second pass of compression also makes files bigger rather than smaller, so don't run the same file through the compressor twice. If the first pass wasn't enough, start over from the original at a higher compression level.

## What ChatGPT can't do well with PDFs

[According to Tom's Guide's testing of ChatGPT with PDFs](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/how-to-use-chatgpt-to-analyze-pdfs-for-free), heavily formatted documents sometimes produce garbled output. Tables, multi-column layouts, and footnotes can confuse the model's reading order. Scanned PDFs where the text has been digitized at low resolution may also produce errors, since the model is reading a photograph of text rather than actual characters.

For scanned PDFs where accuracy really matters, extracting the text with an OCR tool first and then pasting it tends to produce cleaner results than a direct upload.

## The paste method (no account needed)

If your PDF contains selectable text, you don't need to upload anything. Open the PDF in a browser, press Ctrl+A (or Cmd+A on Mac) to select all, copy, and paste into any AI chat box. This works whether or not you have an account, uses none of your daily upload quota, and handles documents of any length.

The limitation is that layout information gets lost. Tables and multi-column text often paste out of order. For clean prose (a research paper, a legal agreement, a report), the paste method is faster than uploading and just as accurate.

You can also use this approach with AI tools other than ChatGPT. If you're working with a sensitive document, a tool that runs in your browser without sending the file to a server is worth considering.

## Picking the right approach

Direct upload is the easiest path for most PDFs. Paste-the-text works for any document with selectable content and has no limits. When a file is too large, the right fix is splitting for text PDFs and compressing for image-heavy ones. Knowing which fix matches your file type saves you from making the file larger by accident.
