Filling out a PDF form on Android is free and takes just a few minutes, whether the form has clickable fields or is a flat scan. This guide shows you how to fill out a PDF form on Android without downloading an app, what to do when the fields won't respond, and how to add a signature when you're done.

## The fastest way: your browser, no download

Open Chrome on your Android phone and go to the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form). Tap the upload button, choose your PDF from your files or Google Drive, and tap into any field to start typing. When all the fields are filled, tap Download to save the completed PDF to your phone.

No app install, no account, no sign-in. The whole process happens in your browser.

## How to fill an interactive PDF form on Android

An interactive [PDF form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF#Interactive_elements) has real form fields baked into the file: text boxes you can type in, checkboxes you can tap, and dropdowns that show a list of choices. Most official forms (government applications, rental agreements, insurance documents) are interactive.

Here's how to fill one using the Fill PDF Form tool:

1. Open the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) in Chrome on your Android device.
2. Tap the upload area and select your PDF file.
3. Tap any text field and type your answer using the keyboard that appears.
4. Tap checkboxes to check or uncheck them.
5. Work through every page until the form is complete.
6. Tap Download to save the finished file.

If your form has a lot of small fields, pinch to zoom in on each section so it's easier to tap accurately on a smaller screen.

## What to do when you can't type in the fields

Some PDFs look like forms but have no interactive fields at all. These are called flat forms or scanned forms. They're images of a paper form, photographed or scanned and saved as a PDF. Nothing happens when you tap the blank lines because there are no fields, only pixels arranged to look like a form.

You'll run into flat forms with older government documents, scanned lease agreements, and any form that was originally printed on paper and then converted to PDF by scanning it back in.

The fix is to add a text layer on top of the image. A PDF editor with a text overlay feature lets you tap anywhere on the page, type your answer, and position the text to align with the printed line or box underneath. It's not quite as clean as filling an interactive form, but it gets the job done and produces a readable, shareable PDF.

If you open a PDF in our [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) and the fields don't respond when you tap them, try the text-add mode to place text manually over the form areas.

## Using Google Drive's built-in form filler

If your PDF is already stored in Google Drive, Android has a shortcut. Open Drive, tap your PDF, and look for "Fill out form" at the bottom of the screen. [Google Drive's PDF form filling](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/9463834) feature lets you type into interactive fields without leaving the Drive app.

This is convenient for files already in your Drive. The main limitation: it works on interactive forms only. If the PDF is a flat scan, Drive won't have interactive fields to offer you. In that case, download the file and open it in a dedicated PDF form filler instead.

## How to sign after filling out the form

Most forms want a signature at the end. Once you've finished filling in the fields, use the [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) to add your signature:

1. Download the completed PDF from the Fill PDF Form tool.
2. Open the Sign PDF tool in Chrome.
3. Upload the filled PDF.
4. Draw your signature with your finger on the signature pad.
5. Drag the signature to the right spot on the page.
6. Download the signed file.

You can do both steps back to back. Fill the form, download it, then immediately upload it to the Sign PDF tool without saving it anywhere else first.

## Why PDF forms sometimes won't let you type on Android

This is one of the most common frustrations with PDF forms on phones. A few things cause it:

**The PDF has no interactive fields.** It was scanned or created from a paper form. No amount of tapping will open a text field. Use text overlay instead.

**Your viewer is rendering it as an image.** Email clients, file managers, and messaging apps often open PDFs as flat previews rather than interactive documents. Open the file in a proper PDF tool instead of tapping it directly from Gmail or WhatsApp.

**The file is locked or restricted.** Some PDFs are set to prevent editing, which includes filling out forms. If the PDF has a password or permission restrictions, you'll need to remove those restrictions before you can fill it in.

**The form uses XFA rather than AcroForm fields.** XFA is an older form format that most modern browsers and apps don't support well. If the PDF was made in Adobe LiveCycle, it may use XFA and fail to render form fields in a standard PDF viewer. Adobe Acrobat Reader for Android handles XFA forms; most other tools don't.

## Choosing the right method

Here's a quick way to match your situation to the right approach:

- **Interactive fields, file on your phone:** Fill PDF Form tool in Chrome
- **Interactive fields, file in Google Drive:** Drive's built-in "Fill out form" option
- **Flat or scanned form:** Use a text overlay tool to place text on top
- **Need a signature after filling:** Sign PDF tool after you finish the form

If you're not sure whether your form is interactive, open it in the Fill PDF Form tool and tap a field. If the keyboard appears, you're set. If nothing happens, the form is flat and you need the text overlay approach.

## A quick note on privacy

PDF forms often carry sensitive information: your address, date of birth, tax numbers, or medical details. Before uploading a form like that to any online tool, check how the tool handles your file. Tools that process PDFs in your browser keep the file on your device. Tools that upload to a server put your data in transit. Look for a privacy or security notice on the tool's page before you upload anything you wouldn't want others to see.
