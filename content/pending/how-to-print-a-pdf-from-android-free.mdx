Printing a PDF from an Android phone is free and requires no third-party app for most setups. Android has had a built-in print framework since version 4.4, and most modern Wi-Fi printers work with it automatically. Here's how to do it.

## Use Android's Built-In Print Menu

Android's print system uses the [Internet Printing Protocol (IPP)](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8011), an open standard that lets your phone talk directly to most network printers. If your phone and printer are on the same Wi-Fi network, Android will usually find the printer without any app.

**Steps:**

1. Open the PDF in any viewer, such as Google Files, your phone's browser, or Adobe Acrobat Reader.
2. Tap the three-dot menu or the share button.
3. Tap **Print**.
4. In the print dialog, tap the printer name at the top to see available printers.
5. Pick your printer, adjust copies, page range, color, and paper size.
6. Tap the print icon or the button in the top corner to send the job.

If your printer doesn't show up, confirm both devices are on the same network. Some older printers don't broadcast IPP and won't appear here. In that case, use your printer brand's app (see the next section).

## Use Your Printer Brand's App

Manufacturer apps handle printers that Android can't discover on its own, including older models and those with proprietary protocols. They're all free on the Google Play Store.

| Printer brand | Free app |
|---|---|
| HP | HP Smart |
| Canon | Canon PRINT Inkjet/SELPHY |
| Epson | Epson iPrint |
| Brother | Brother iPrint&Scan |
| Samsung | Samsung Print Service Plugin |

Install the app for your brand, connect it to your printer once during setup, and then print from any app on your phone by choosing **Print** and selecting your printer. These apps also let you check ink levels, scan, and manage print jobs.

## Print via Wi-Fi Direct (No Router Needed)

Wi-Fi Direct creates a direct connection between your phone and printer without going through your home network. It's useful when you don't have a shared Wi-Fi network, such as printing at a copy shop, an office you're visiting, or anywhere you can access the printer physically but not the local network.

1. On the printer, go to its settings menu and enable Wi-Fi Direct. The printer will show a network name (usually starting with "DIRECT-") and a passphrase.
2. On your Android phone, go to **Settings > Wi-Fi**, find that printer network in the list, and connect using the passphrase.
3. Open your PDF and print using the normal print menu. Android routes the job over the direct connection.

Your phone's regular internet access will pause while it's connected to the printer via Wi-Fi Direct, since Android uses the same Wi-Fi radio for both. Reconnect to your usual network once you're done printing.

## What to Do When the PDF Won't Print

**Printer not appearing.** Double-check that both devices are on the same network. Try turning the printer off and on. On some Android versions, clearing the cache of the "Print Spooler" system app (Settings > Apps > Print Spooler > Storage > Clear cache) unsticks jobs that got lost.

**PDF is too large to email for printing.** If you need to email the file to someone else to print and it exceeds the attachment limit, check what the PDF contains. Scanned pages and image-heavy documents compress well: the free [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) handles this in your browser, with no account required. Text-only files like contracts or forms won't benefit from this type of compression (rasterizing text into images actually makes files larger), so split out just the pages you need instead.

**Pages printing in the wrong orientation.** Open the print dialog and tap **Orientation** to switch between portrait and landscape.

**Only need a few pages.** Type a page range in the print dialog (for example, 2-5 or 1,3,7) rather than printing the whole file.

## Print Two Pages Per Sheet

If the document is long and you want to save paper, you can fit two PDF pages onto a single printed sheet.

Some Android printer drivers include a "Pages per sheet" or "N-up" setting directly in the print dialog. Look for it under **More options** or the layout settings. If your printer driver doesn't offer it, you can prepare the file first: the [Pages Per Sheet tool](/n-up-pdf) combines 2 or 4 pages per sheet in your browser without a sign-up, then you print the resulting PDF as a normal single-page-per-sheet job.

## Google Cloud Print Is Gone

Many older tutorials recommend Google Cloud Print. That service was shut down on January 1, 2021, and it no longer works. Android's native IPP printing and the manufacturer apps above replace everything it used to do.

## Third-Party PDF Apps

Apps like [Adobe Acrobat Reader for Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adobe.reader) (free) include their own print option that calls the same Android print system. The advantage of a dedicated reader is better rendering before the file goes to the printer, which helps with PDFs that use uncommon fonts or complex vector graphics. The steps are the same: open the PDF, find the print option in the menu, and pick your printer.

For most everyday PDFs (a receipt, a form, a boarding pass) you don't need anything beyond what Android already includes.
