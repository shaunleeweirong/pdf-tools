Compressing a PDF for tax filing is the right move when your document is scanned, and the wrong move when it isn't. A compression tool rasterizes every page to a JPEG image, which shrinks photo-heavy files but makes text-only files much larger. Picking the wrong approach for your tax document can turn a 500 KB W-2 into a multi-megabyte file that's harder to upload, not easier.

Here's how to tell what you're dealing with and what to do about it.

## What file size limits do tax portals enforce?

The [IRS Document Upload Tool](https://www.irs.gov/payments/irs-document-upload-tool) allows up to **15 MB per file**, with up to 40 files per submission and a 120-page cap per PDF. Most individual taxpayers won't hit those limits with a standard return. The situations that push past them are:

- Supporting documentation for an audit response (receipts, bank records, medical bills)
- Form 8949 attachments listing large numbers of stock transactions
- Multi-year records requested during a correspondence audit

Tax software portals set their own, often stricter limits. TaxAct caps PDF attachments around 5 MB. State tax portals are sometimes as tight as 1 to 2 MB. Check your specific portal's help page before you do anything else, so you know what target size you're actually working toward.

## When compression works: scanned receipts and supporting documents

If your PDF contains scanned pages, compression is the right tool. Scanned receipts, bank statements, and paper records are stored as images inside the PDF container. A compression tool shrinks them by re-encoding those images at a lower quality level, and the results are substantial.

In our own testing, a 913.4 KB image deck came down to 315.3 KB at Medium compression (a 65.5% reduction) and 175.6 KB at High (an 80.8% reduction). A thick folder of scanned tax receipts behaves the same way. What was a 25 MB bundle of scans can compress to 5 to 8 MB, well within IRS limits.

**How to compress a scanned tax PDF:**

1. Go to the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf). No sign-up needed. Your file never leaves your browser.
2. Upload your scanned receipt packet or supporting document.
3. Select **Medium** quality. This typically cuts file size by 60% or more while keeping text sharp enough to read.
4. Download the result. Check the file size.
5. If it's still above your portal's limit, go back to the **original file** and compress at **High** quality. Do not run the Medium output through the tool a second time. A second pass at the same level adds size rather than removing it. Our testing showed a Medium-compressed file growing from 315.3 KB to 598.9 KB when compressed again. Always start from the original.
6. Upload the final compressed PDF to your tax portal.

## When compression backfires: digital returns and forms

A W-2, a 1099-MISC, or a PDF tax return generated by software is almost entirely vector text. The pages are not images. When you run a text PDF through a compression tool, it converts those vector characters into a JPEG photograph of the text. The result is a larger file with blurry text.

In our own testing, a 20-page text document went from 23.5 KB to 1,400.8 KB at Medium compression, roughly 60 times larger. That file would not help you get under a portal's size limit.

The fix for a large text-based tax PDF is not compression. The options that actually work:

- **Split the document.** Use our [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) to extract only the pages the portal needs. If you have a 40-page combined return and only need to attach Schedules C and D, extract those 8 pages and submit just that portion. Splitting is lossless and takes seconds.
- **Delete unused pages.** If the PDF has cover sheets, blank pages, or sections the portal doesn't require, use Delete Pages to remove them before uploading.
- **Submit in multiple files.** Most portals, including the IRS's, accept multiple uploads in the same submission. If Form 8949 attachment runs to 100 pages of stock trades, split it into three 35-page files.

## How to tell if your PDF is scanned or digital

Open the PDF and try to click and drag to select text. If individual words highlight, the document is digital. If your cursor becomes a crosshair and nothing selects, the pages are scanned images.

A secondary check: compare page count to file size. A digital 20-page tax return typically runs under 500 KB. A 20-page scanned document often exceeds 15 MB. If the file is large relative to how many pages it has, it's almost certainly scanned.

## Combining multiple tax documents into one PDF

If you want to send several receipts or attachments as a single file, the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) combines them without adding any meaningful overhead. Our testing showed that merging files produces output that is marginally smaller than the sum of its parts.

One important note: merging strips interactive form fields. If you have a PDF form with filled-in fields, flatten it with the Flatten PDF tool before merging, so the entered values are preserved as visible page content rather than being lost.

## What if the scanned PDF is still too large after compression?

A few additional approaches when High compression still isn't enough:

- **Scan in grayscale or black and white at 150 to 200 DPI.** Color scans are roughly three times the size of grayscale scans for the same document. For government submissions, color adds nothing. If you still have the paper, re-scan it in grayscale. [The IRS does not require color copies of supporting documents](https://www.irs.gov/payments/irs-document-upload-tool).
- **Split the scan into smaller parts.** Even after compression, a 100-page receipt bundle may be too large for one upload. Split it into four 25-page files and upload each separately.
- **Remove pages you don't need.** Receipts with duplicate copies or blank reverses can be trimmed. A 30-page expense report might compress down to 20 pages of unique content once blanks are removed.

## Quick decision guide
