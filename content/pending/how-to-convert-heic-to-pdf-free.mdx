If your iPhone photos arrive as .heic files that Windows PCs, email clients, or upload forms won't accept, converting heic to pdf solves the problem immediately. PDFs open on every device, the image quality is kept, and you can combine multiple photos into one document. Here are four ways to do it for free, with no account required.

## What is a HEIC file and why does it cause problems?

HEIC stands for High Efficiency Image Container. It's the photo format Apple introduced in iOS 11 using [HEVC (H.265) compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Efficiency_Video_Coding), the same codec used for 4K video streaming. HEIC images are roughly 40 to 50 percent smaller than equivalent JPEGs at the same visual quality, which is why Apple switched to it.

The downside is compatibility. HEIC is an [Apple-specific implementation of the HEIF standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Efficiency_Image_File_Format), and many Windows machines, Android devices, websites, and form upload fields don't support it. Converting to PDF or JPG before sharing avoids the problem entirely.

## How to convert HEIC to PDF on a Mac

Mac's built-in Preview app can open HEIC files directly and export them as PDF. No extra software, no sign-up.

1. Find the HEIC file in Finder.
2. Double-click to open it in Preview (or right-click and choose Open With > Preview).
3. Go to **File > Export as PDF**.
4. Choose a folder, name the file, and click **Save**.

The PDF preserves the full resolution of the original image.

For a batch of photos, you can open all of them in Preview at once: select multiple HEIC files in Finder, right-click, and choose Open With > Preview. Then go to **File > Print**, and in the bottom-left corner of the print dialog click the dropdown and select **Save as PDF**. All images become pages in a single PDF.

## How to convert HEIC to PDF on iPhone

The Files app on iOS 13 and later can create a PDF from HEIC photos without any extra app.

1. Open the **Files** app and locate the HEIC photo.
2. Long-press the file until the context menu appears.
3. Tap **Quick Look** to open a preview.
4. Tap the **Share** button (the square with an upward arrow) and choose **Create PDF**.
5. The PDF opens in Quick Look. Tap **Done**, then **Save File**, and pick where to save it.

To convert several HEIC photos into a single PDF: tap **Select** in the Files app, pick all the images you want, tap the Share button, and then **Create PDF**. The images become pages in the order you selected them.

Note that the Photos app doesn't have a direct "Create PDF" option. The Files app is the right place for this. If your HEIC photos are in your Camera Roll rather than Files, you can share them to Files first via the share sheet.

## How to convert HEIC to PDF on Windows

Windows 11 can open HEIC files natively on most up-to-date systems. If you get an error, install the [HEVC Video Extensions](https://apps.microsoft.com/detail/9nmzlz57r3t7) from the Microsoft Store (it's free on most devices).

Once Windows can open the file:

1. Open the HEIC photo in the **Photos** app.
2. Click the **Print** icon or press Ctrl+P.
3. In the Printer dropdown, select **Microsoft Print to PDF**.
4. Click **Print**, choose where to save the file, and click **Save**.

You'll get a PDF with the photo on a single page. For a multi-page PDF with several images, open all of them in Photos, select them all, and print to PDF from there. Alternatively, bring the images into Paint, which also prints to PDF and lets you adjust page size and orientation before saving.

## How to convert HEIC to JPG and then to PDF

Some workflows need a JPG as the starting point rather than HEIC. Getting there varies by device:

**On Mac:** Open the HEIC file in Preview, go to **File > Export**, and choose **JPEG** from the Format dropdown. Adjust the quality slider and click Save. You can do this for a batch by selecting all images in Preview's sidebar before exporting.

**On iPhone:** AirDrop the HEIC photos to your Mac and export them as JPEG from Preview. Alternatively, change your camera settings before the next shoot (more on that below) so future photos save as JPEG from the start.

Once you have JPG files, the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) turns them into a PDF for free, with no account needed. You can add multiple images and arrange them in whatever order you want before downloading the result.

## Stop your iPhone from saving HEIC in the first place

If sharing photos frequently is part of your workflow, the simplest fix is changing your camera settings so your iPhone captures JPEG instead of HEIC from now on.

1. Open **Settings** on your iPhone.
2. Tap **Camera**, then **Formats**.
3. Choose **Most Compatible**.

New photos will be saved as JPEG. Your existing HEIC photos stay as they are, but you won't need to convert going forward.

One thing to know: JPEG files are about twice the size of equivalent HEIC files, so your storage use will increase. If that matters, you can keep HEIC for everyday photos and only switch to Most Compatible when you know you'll need to share the images widely. You can toggle between the two settings any time.

## Which method should you use?

- **Quick, single image on Mac:** Preview's Export as PDF is the fastest option.
- **On iPhone with no laptop nearby:** Use the Files app Quick Look method.
- **On Windows:** Print to PDF through the Photos app.
- **Multiple images into one PDF on any device:** Convert to JPG first, then use the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) to combine them in any order.
- **Long-term fix:** Switch your iPhone camera to Most Compatible so new photos are always JPEG.

Any of these takes under a minute once you know where to look.
