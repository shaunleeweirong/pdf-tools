You can sign a PDF on your iPad for free right now, without printing a single page or downloading anything. Every iPad already has a built-in Markup tool that lets you draw your signature directly on a PDF and place it wherever the signature line is. Here's how to use it, plus a browser-based option for when Markup doesn't fit the job.

## The quickest option: Apple Markup in the Files app

Markup comes with every iPad running iPadOS. You don't need to install anything. Open your PDF in the Files app, add your signature, and you're done in under a minute.

1. Open the **Files** app and find your PDF.
2. Tap the PDF to open it in the viewer.
3. Tap the **Markup** icon (a pencil tip in a circle) in the top-right corner.
4. Tap the **+** button at the bottom right, then choose **Signature**.
5. Draw your signature with your finger or Apple Pencil, then tap **Done**.
6. Your signature appears on the page as a movable element. Drag it to the right spot and use the corner handles to resize.
7. Tap **Done** again to save.

That's the whole process. The signed PDF overwrites the file in place (or you can share a copy straight from the Done menu). iPadOS stores the signature you draw, so next time you tap Signature your saved ones appear as thumbnails. You only need to draw it once.

## Signing a PDF that arrived in an email

If someone sent you a PDF to sign and return, you don't need to save it to Files first.

1. Open **Mail** and tap the PDF attachment preview.
2. Tap the **Markup** icon in the top-right corner.
3. Tap **+**, then **Signature**, and choose your saved signature or draw a new one.
4. Drag it to the correct spot, then tap **Done**.
5. Tap the **Share** icon and choose **Reply** to send the signed file back.

Mail uses the same Markup engine as Files, so the steps are identical once you're inside the document.

## Does Apple Pencil make a difference?

For iPad users who have an Apple Pencil, signing feels noticeably different from using a finger. The Pencil is pressure-sensitive, so a firm stroke produces a thicker line and a light one produces a thin line, much like signing with a real pen. The result usually looks cleaner, especially on smaller signature boxes.

Finger input works fine for most documents. The lines are slightly thicker and less precise, but for contracts and everyday paperwork that's not an issue.

One useful iPad setting: in Markup, go to the three-dot menu and look for **"Only draw with Apple Pencil"**. Turning this on means your finger scrolls the document while the Pencil draws. Without it, your palm can accidentally leave stray marks when you rest it on the screen.

## What if the Markup icon is greyed out or missing?

A few things can cause this.

**The PDF opened in a third-party app.** If you have a PDF reader installed, iPadOS may send the file there instead of Files. Fix it: go back to the Files app, long-press the PDF, and choose **Markup** from the action menu. This bypasses the other app entirely.

**The file came in via Mail and Markup is unavailable.** Tap and hold the attachment in Mail, choose **Save to Files**, then open it from there.

**The PDF has editing restrictions.** If the original creator locked the file against changes, Markup treats that as a restriction on annotations too. You'd need to remove those restrictions first. The [Unlock PDF tool](/unlock-pdf) can clear the password if you know it.

## Signing a PDF in Safari on iPad (no app at all)

If you want a fully browser-based option, the [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) runs in Safari. Open it on your iPad, upload your PDF from the Files app, draw your signature on the screen, and download the result. Nothing is uploaded to a server; the whole process runs in your browser.

One thing to keep in mind: the tool places the signature on the first page of the document. If the signature line is on page three of a contract, Apple Markup is more practical because you can scroll to any page before inserting.

## What to do if the PDF has fillable form fields

Lots of documents you need to sign also have form fields: name, address, date, and so on. There's a difference between tapping into a form field and drawing a signature on top of the page.

Markup can add typed text anywhere using **+**, then **Text**, but it lays the text on top of the page rather than filling an actual form field. For a PDF with real interactive fields, the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) detects and fills them properly. After completing the form, save it and then open it in Markup to add your signature.

If you plan to merge a filled form with another document later, flatten it first. Merging without flattening strips the field values from the PDF (the structure holding the form data doesn't survive the merge). The [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) converts fields to ordinary page content before you combine anything.

## How to share the signed PDF

Once you tap Done in Markup, the signed file is back in Files. From there:

- Tap **Share** (the box with an arrow) to send it by Mail, Messages, AirDrop, or any other app.
- Choose **Save to Files** to put a copy in a specific folder or iCloud Drive.
- Or just leave it where it is. The original PDF in Files now contains your signature.

## Is the signature legally valid?

For the vast majority of everyday documents, yes. [Electronic signatures are recognized under the U.S. ESIGN Act and UETA](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/electronic-signatures-global-national-commerce-act), which give a drawn digital signature the same standing as ink for contracts, letters of intent, and similar agreements. The [eIDAS regulation](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eidas-regulation) sets out equivalent standards in the EU.

A few exceptions exist: wills, adoptions, certain real-estate closings, and court filings often require a witnessed signature or a higher level of authentication. If you're signing something formal and you're not sure, ask the receiving party what standard they need before you send.

For contracts, NDAs, freelance agreements, permission slips, and the documents most people handle day to day, your finger-drawn or Pencil-drawn signature in Markup is perfectly sufficient and widely accepted.
