Removing annotations from a PDF lets you share a clean copy of a document without the highlights, sticky notes, and comments added during review. You can do it free, without Adobe Acrobat, straight in your browser.

## What counts as a PDF annotation?

Annotations are anything added on top of the original document content. The [PDF specification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF#Annotations) defines more than a dozen types: highlights, underlines, strikethroughs, sticky note comments, freehand ink drawings, shape markup (rectangles and circles), stamps, and text boxes placed over a page.

What they all share is that they sit in a separate data layer from the body text and images. That separation is what makes it possible to strip them without touching the document itself.

## Why would you want to remove them?

A few common situations come up:

- You're sending a reviewed draft to a client and want a clean final version with no internal comments visible.
- A PDF arrived pre-marked and you want a fresh, unmarked read.
- You're submitting a document to a portal that strips annotations anyway, and you'd rather control the output before upload.
- A colleague's sticky notes are covering part of the page and you need to see what's underneath.
- You want to share a file without revealing which sections were flagged during internal review.

## How to remove all PDF annotations at once (free, no Acrobat)

Browser-based annotation removers are the fastest option. No download, no account.

1. Open a browser-based PDF annotation remover.
2. Upload your PDF. The tool reads the annotation layer separately from the page content.
3. Choose to remove all annotations, or select specific types if the tool supports it.
4. Download the cleaned PDF.

The whole process takes under a minute for most files. Because annotations live in their own layer, the tool strips them without touching the text or images underneath.

A note on privacy: if your document contains sensitive content, look for a tool that processes files locally in the browser rather than uploading them to a server. Some tools advertise this explicitly ("files never leave your browser").

## How to remove specific annotation types

Sometimes you want selective control. You might want to keep a colleague's comments but remove the highlights, or delete sticky notes on one section and leave the rest. Many tools let you pick which types to strip: highlights and underlines only, comments only, ink drawings only, and so on.

## Does removing annotations change the document content?

No. Annotations are stored separately from the body text, images, and page layout. When you delete them, the underlying document is unchanged. The page looks exactly as it did when the document was first created, before anyone added markup.

There's one exception worth knowing about: if annotations were **flattened** into the document at some point, they're no longer a separate layer. They've been rendered into the page content itself. In that case, an annotation remover won't find anything to strip, because there's no annotation layer left. The marks would need to be edited out using a PDF editor instead.

## The difference between removing and flattening

These two operations are often confused because people use the words interchangeably, but the outcome is different.

**Removing annotations** deletes them from the document. The page shows no trace of where they were. This is what you want when you need a clean, unmarked copy.

**Flattening annotations** bakes them into the page as static graphics. They still look the same on screen and in print, but they're no longer interactive or editable. Nobody can delete them, move them, or read a hidden comment text.

Our [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) handles the second case. Use it when you want to lock annotations permanently into the page, for example before sharing a document where you want readers to see your highlights but not be able to remove them. Use a dedicated remover when you want the annotations gone entirely.

## Removing annotations on mobile

Any browser-based tool works on iPhone, Android, or a tablet. Open your phone's browser, go to the tool, tap to upload the PDF from your files app, and download the clean result when it's done. You don't need a separate app.

If you're sharing a document from your phone and want to strip markup before sending, this is the quickest path. The process is the same as on a desktop.

## What about password-protected PDFs?

Most annotation removers require an open (unencrypted) file. If your PDF is password protected, you'll need to remove the password before you can strip the annotations. Once the password is removed, you can run the annotation remover as normal.

PDF annotations, including highlights, comments, sticky notes, drawings, and stamps, are stored in a separate layer and can be stripped without affecting the document itself. Browser-based removers handle this in one step, free, with no account needed. If you only want to delete certain items, use a PDF editor to click and remove them individually.
