The best free PDF app for iPhone isn't an app at all. Browser-based PDF tools run in Safari with no download and no sign-up, handling almost every task most people actually need.

If you're looking for the fastest way to handle a PDF on your iPhone, skip the App Store entirely. Open Safari, go to the tool you need, process your file, and download the result. Nothing to install, nothing to pay for, no email address required.

## What iPhone already gives you

Apple's built-in tools handle reading and basic markup well. Open a PDF in the Files app, tap the share icon or the pencil, and [Markup](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111819) launches. With Markup you can:

- Annotate and draw on pages
- Add a text box or a signature (drawn with your finger or Apple Pencil)
- Fill in basic form fields

That's genuinely useful for light annotation and signing. What Markup can't do: merge files, compress a large PDF, convert images to PDF, split a document, or extract specific pages. For any of those, you need a different tool.

## Why browser tools beat app downloads

Every PDF app you download asks for storage space, sometimes an account, often a subscription after a free trial. Browser-based tools sidestep all of that. Here's why they're usually the better choice for occasional tasks:

**No install.** Open Safari, go to the tool, start working. You're done in under a minute.

**No account.** No email, no password, no trial that turns into a charge.

**Files stay on your device.** Tools that process files in the browser using on-device JavaScript never upload your file to a server. Your PDF doesn't travel anywhere.

**Works across devices.** The same URL works on your iPhone, iPad, and laptop. No syncing required.

## What you can do for free on iPhone, right now

**Compress a PDF.** If a scanned document or image-heavy PDF is too large to attach to an email, the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) reduces the file size in Safari. One thing worth knowing upfront: this compressor works by rasterizing each page to JPEG, which shrinks scans and photo-based PDFs well but makes text-only documents larger. If your PDF is a contract or a form with mostly typed text, compression will backfire. Split the file or ask the recipient to accept a shared link instead.

**Merge multiple PDFs.** The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) combines several PDFs into one document. Add your files in the order you want, tap merge, and download. Merging doesn't increase the combined file size beyond what the parts already add up to, so it won't push you over an email size limit. One caveat: if any of your files has interactive form fields that you've filled in, flatten them before merging. Merging strips interactive fields, so values filled into a form may be lost if you skip that step.

**Split a PDF.** Only need certain pages from a longer document? The [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) pulls out specific page ranges as a new file. Good for sharing one chapter of a report or one page of a contract without the rest.

**Convert images to PDF.** The [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) takes JPG and PNG files from your Camera Roll or Files and builds a PDF, one image per page. This is the fastest way to turn a stack of iPhone photos into a single shareable document.

**Convert a PDF to images.** The [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) turns each page into a JPEG file. Useful when you need to paste a page into a presentation, post it on social media, or share it somewhere that doesn't accept PDFs.

**Sign a document.** The [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) lets you draw a signature with your finger and place it wherever the document needs it. No subscription, no watermark.

**Extract specific pages.** The [Extract Pages tool](/extract-pages) saves just the pages you choose as a separate PDF. Handy when someone sends a 40-page report and you need to forward just three pages.

**Rotate sideways pages.** A scanned page that came out rotated is a quick fix with the [Rotate PDF tool](/rotate-pdf). It sets the page orientation flag without re-rendering anything, so the file size stays the same.

## How to use any of these tools on iPhone

1. Open Safari.
2. Go to the tool you need (for example, [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf)).
3. Tap the upload area. A file picker opens letting you choose from Files, Photos, or iCloud Drive.
4. Add your file or files, set any options (page order, compression level).
5. Tap the action button and wait a few seconds.
6. The finished file downloads to your Downloads folder in Files.
7. From there, share it to Mail, Messages, WhatsApp, or any other app with the standard Share sheet.

For most tasks, the whole process takes under 90 seconds.

## When to actually install a PDF app

A dedicated app earns its place if you work with PDFs every day and need features like OCR for scanned text, deep annotation libraries, offline batch processing, or sync across multiple devices through a cloud account. PDF Expert and Adobe Acrobat are the two most-recommended options for heavy professional use.

For everything else, browser tools are faster to reach and cost nothing.
