## How to convert a PDF to Word without losing formatting

Converting a PDF to Word without losing formatting is possible in most cases, though how well it works depends heavily on what the PDF contains and which method you pick. For most people, the fastest free option is opening the PDF directly in Microsoft Word.

This guide covers the best free methods, explains why formatting slips in the first place, and tells you when it's smarter to skip conversion altogether.

## Why formatting gets lost when converting

Understanding the cause makes the fix much easier to pick.

A [PDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) stores each piece of text at an exact position on the page, a bit like stickers placed on a canvas. There's no concept of paragraphs, flowing columns, or line wrapping: just coordinates, glyphs, and sizes. A single sentence might be stored as a dozen separate fragments across the page.

Word documents work the other way around. Text flows, wraps, and reflows based on margins and font size. When a converter bridges the two, it has to reconstruct paragraph structure from raw position data. For a simple letter or a resume, it usually gets this right. For a multi-column newsletter, a financial report with merged table cells, or anything that was designed rather than typed, the converter is making educated guesses, and those guesses show.

Two extra factors make conversion harder:

**Scanned PDFs.** If the PDF is a photo of a paper document, there is no text in the file at all. Converters need optical character recognition (OCR) to extract anything, and OCR accuracy varies with scan quality, skew angle, and font style.

**Unusual fonts.** If the converter doesn't have access to the original font, it substitutes something close. Spacing shifts, and columns that depended on precise character widths go off-kilter.

## Method 1: Open the PDF directly in Microsoft Word

This is the most accurate free option for the majority of office documents.

1. Open Microsoft Word (2013 or any later version, including Microsoft 365).
2. Click **File > Open** and select your PDF file.
3. Word will show a message explaining that it will convert the file. Click **OK**.
4. Review the result and fix stray line breaks or misplaced images as needed.

Tables, headings, and running body text usually survive this process very well. Multi-column text and images wrapped tightly around paragraphs need some cleanup. The conversion is entirely local to your device, so nothing leaves your computer.

## Method 2: Google Docs (browser-based, no software required)

If you're on a Chromebook, a shared computer, or just prefer to stay in the browser, Google Docs handles PDF conversion for free.

1. Open Google Drive and upload the PDF.
2. Right-click the uploaded file and choose **Open with > Google Docs**.
3. Google Docs converts the file and opens a new editable document.
4. Download it as a Word file via **File > Download > Microsoft Word (.docx)** if needed.

Google Docs applies OCR automatically for scanned PDFs, which is a big plus. The formatting fidelity is a step below Word's importer for complex layouts, but for most letters, contracts, and reports created in a word processor originally, the result is clean enough.

## Method 3: Free online converters

Online converters are useful when you don't have Word or Google Drive, or when a particularly tricky PDF needs a different approach.

A few well-regarded free options:

- **ILovePDF** handles scanned PDFs with OCR and doesn't require an account for individual files.
- **Smallpdf** offers drag-and-drop conversion with one free session per hour.
- **LibreOffice Writer** is a free, open-source alternative to Word that opens PDFs natively. [LibreOffice](https://www.libreoffice.org/) runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux, and its importer often handles complex tables and multi-column text better than purely online tools.

For large PDFs that hit an upload size limit, compress the file first. Our [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) can significantly shrink image-heavy PDFs before you send them through a converter.

## What to do when the converted file still looks wrong

If the output is badly broken, work through these steps before giving up:

**Turn on formatting marks in Word.** Click the pilcrow button (¶) in the Home tab. Hidden line breaks and extra paragraph marks are the most common cause of odd spacing, and you can't fix them without seeing them.

**Rebuild tables manually.** Broken tables are usually faster to recreate from scratch using the correctly extracted text than to fix by hand. Select the table, delete it, insert a new one, and paste in the text.

**Try a different method.** Results vary noticeably from one converter to the next. If Word's importer makes a mess of a particular file, try LibreOffice or an online tool, and vice versa.

**Handle mixed documents separately.** If your PDF has some typed pages and some scanned pages, you can use the [Extract Images tool](/extract-images) to pull the scanned pages out as images before running the conversion. That gives you clean typed text in Word and separate image files you can insert back in.

## When to skip conversion and edit the PDF directly

Not every editing job requires converting to Word. If you need to change a date, update a phone number, or fix a typo, converting the whole file just to make one small change creates a lot of unnecessary work.

A free online [PDF editor](/edit-pdf) lets you click on existing text and edit it in place, add new text boxes, move things around, or swap out images. The rest of the document stays exactly as it is. No reformatting, no wondering whether the paragraph broke in the right place.

This approach works well for targeted changes. For a document that someone needs to write and rewrite repeatedly, converting to Word makes more sense.

## Choosing the right method

| Situation | Best free method |
|---|---|
| Simple text document, have Word | File > Open in Microsoft Word |
| No Word installed, need a browser option | Google Docs |
| Scanned PDF, need OCR | Google Docs or ILovePDF |
| Complex tables or multi-column layout | LibreOffice Writer |
| Only need to change a few words or numbers | Edit the PDF directly |

For most people, opening the PDF in Word or Google Docs covers it. The output won't be pixel-perfect for anything that was professionally designed, but for documents created in a word processor originally, the conversion is usually very close to the original.

If formatting still slips after trying these methods, the most practical fix is often to stop aiming for a perfect automated conversion and spend ten minutes cleaning up the Word file by hand. Automated tools are surprisingly good, but they're not designers. Some things are faster to fix yourself.
