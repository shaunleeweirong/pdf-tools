Merging PDFs on Android is free and takes under a minute in Chrome. Open the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool in your browser, pick your files, and download the combined result. No app to install, no account required, and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

## Does Android have a built-in PDF merger?

Unlike iPhone, which gained a [Create PDF option in the Files app](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105080) with iOS 15, stock Android doesn't ship with a native PDF combining tool. Google Drive, Samsung My Files, and Chrome's built-in PDF viewer can all open and display PDFs, but none of them merge multiple files into one document.

That leaves three practical routes on Android:

- A browser-based tool in Chrome (no install, works on any Android phone or tablet)
- A third-party app downloaded from the Play Store
- Desktop software if you have a computer nearby

The browser route is the simplest. It works on any Android device, requires no account, and your files stay on your device the whole time.

## How to merge PDFs in Chrome on Android

1. Open **Chrome** on your Android phone or tablet and go to the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool.
2. Tap **Select files**. Android opens the system file picker, where you can browse Downloads, Google Drive, Samsung My Files, or any other location your device knows about.
3. Tap the PDFs you want to combine. The tool lists them in the order you selected them.
4. If the order is wrong, drag the files to rearrange before you continue.
5. Tap **Merge PDF** and wait while the tool processes in your browser.
6. Tap **Download** to save the combined file. It lands in your Downloads folder.

On a phone, the touch targets are large enough to be usable. On a tablet you get more screen to see file names and reorder the list before merging.

## Picking files from Google Drive, Samsung My Files, or email

When you tap Select files, Android's storage picker shows every location your phone has access to. Most Android users see something like:

- **Downloads** (files saved to local storage)
- **Google Drive** (if the Drive app is installed and you're signed in)
- **Samsung My Files** (on Galaxy phones)
- Other cloud apps like Dropbox or OneDrive, if they're installed and registered as storage providers

You can pick PDFs directly from Drive without downloading them to your phone first. If Google Drive doesn't appear in the picker, make sure the Drive app is installed and that you've signed in at least once.

For PDFs that arrived as email attachments, save them to your local storage first. In Gmail, long-press the attachment and tap **Save to Drive** or **Download**, then find the file in Downloads or Drive when the picker opens.

## Does merging increase the file size?

No. Merging doesn't inflate the output in any meaningful way. We ran our merge tool on three [PDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) files totalling 971.9 KB: the combined result came out at 970.5 KB, a fraction smaller than the sum of the inputs. That small reduction happens because shared document structure is written once in the output rather than once per source file.

This matters if you're merging before emailing or uploading to a portal. Combining files will not push you over a size limit beyond whatever the parts already add up to.

## What happens to filled-in PDF forms?

Merging removes interactive form fields from the output. If you've filled in a PDF form on your Android phone and want to include it in a combined document, the field values may not survive the merge step as editable content.

To protect the values, use the [Flatten PDF](/flatten-pdf) tool before merging. Flattening converts the interactive fields into regular page content, so the text you typed is part of the page itself. Once flattened, the form fields can't be edited again, but the values are permanently visible and will come through the merge intact.

## What to do if the merged file is still too big

If the combined PDF is too large for an email attachment or an upload portal, the fix depends on what the document contains.

For a PDF made of scanned pages or photos, the [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) tool can cut the size substantially. Image-heavy files often shrink by two thirds or more at Medium compression. Compress the merged file once, at the level that fits, and check the output size before uploading.

For a PDF made of typed text, don't use the compressor. In-browser compression rasterizes pages to images, and a text page stored as an image is much larger than text stored as font data. A text PDF that's too large is usually large because it's long, or because it has an embedded image like a high-resolution logo. For that, use the [Split PDF](/split-pdf) tool to send only the pages the recipient actually needs, rather than the whole document.

## Tips for merging on Android

**Select all files at once** rather than doing multiple rounds. Merging an already-merged PDF through the tool again is harmless but adds an extra step with no benefit.

**Rename files before you start** if the order matters. Prefixes like `01-`, `02-`, `03-` let you tap files in sequence from the picker without hunting through a jumbled list.

**Use a tablet for large batches.** Selecting and reordering many PDFs on a small phone screen is workable but fiddly. On a tablet the file picker and the tool's reorder interface give you much more room.

**Check the output size before sending.** Download the merged file, open your Files app or Downloads folder, and long-press the file to see its size. A quick size check saves the frustration of an attachment bounce or a failed portal upload.
