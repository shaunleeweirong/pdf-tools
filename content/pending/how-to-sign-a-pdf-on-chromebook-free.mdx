You can sign a PDF on a Chromebook for free without printing a single page. ChromeOS ships with a built-in signature tool right inside the PDF viewer, and any browser-based signing tool works just as well since Chromebooks run Chrome natively.

Here's how to do it, and what the difference is between the two methods.

## Does Chromebook have a built-in PDF signing feature?

Yes, and most people don't know it's there. When you open a PDF in ChromeOS's built-in viewer, the toolbar at the top includes a signature icon. Clicking it lets you draw your signature using a mouse, trackpad, finger (on touch-screen Chromebooks), or stylus. Once drawn, you drag the signature to the correct spot on the page and save the document.

ChromeOS also remembers your signatures between sessions. If you sign one document and later open another, you'll see your saved signature available to reuse without redrawing.

This feature has been part of [ChromeOS since 2022](https://support.google.com/chromebook/answer/12453461), when Google added annotation, form-fill, and signature tools to the platform's PDF viewer.

## How to sign a PDF on Chromebook, step by step

This uses the built-in method, which requires nothing extra to install.

1. **Open the PDF.** Click the file in your Files app or Downloads folder. It opens in the Chrome viewer automatically.
2. **Find the signature button.** Look at the top toolbar for the signature icon (it looks like a pen nib or a stylized signature line). If you don't see it, click the pencil icon to enter annotation mode first, then it appears in the sub-toolbar.
3. **Draw your signature.** A drawing box appears. Use your mouse or trackpad to draw your name. If your Chromebook has a touchscreen or stylus, those work better for a natural result. You can clear and redraw as many times as you need.
4. **Place the signature.** Click "Place signature" and drag the signature box to the correct line on the document. You can resize it by dragging the corners.
5. **Save the file.** Press Ctrl+S or use the download button. The signed PDF saves to your Downloads folder.

That's the whole process. It takes about a minute once you know where the signature button is.

## Using a browser-based tool for more control

The built-in method works well for basic cases, but the positioning controls are limited. If you need to place a signature precisely anywhere on a multi-page document, or you want a cleaner result with more control over sizing, a browser tool fills that gap.

The [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) runs entirely in your browser. Open it, upload your PDF, draw your signature in the panel, position it where you need it, and download the result. Your file is processed locally, never sent to a server, so documents that contain confidential information stay on your device.

The process on a Chromebook is the same as on any other computer:

1. Open the [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) in Chrome.
2. Upload your PDF using the file picker.
3. Draw your signature in the signature panel with your mouse, trackpad, or touchscreen.
4. Drag and resize the signature on the page until it sits exactly where you need it.
5. Download the signed PDF.

Because Chromebooks run Chrome as their native browser, every browser-based PDF tool works exactly as it does on Windows or Mac. No Android apps or Chrome extensions required.

## How to fill form fields and sign on Chromebook

Many documents have both typed fields (name, date, address) and a signature line. ChromeOS's built-in viewer handles interactive PDF form fields directly. For PDFs that don't have interactive fields but have blank lines to fill in, the annotation tools let you add text boxes.

If you want to handle both filling and signing together, open the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) to complete typed fields first, then use the [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) to add your drawn signature. Doing it in that order keeps things clean.

One practical note: if you're sending a filled form somewhere important, flatten it with the [Flatten PDF](/flatten-pdf) tool before you send it. Flattening bakes the field values and signature into the page as static content, so nothing can be accidentally cleared or edited by whoever receives the file. This matters especially for forms where filled values might not survive being opened in a different PDF viewer.

## Is a Chromebook PDF signature legally valid?

That depends on what the document requires.

The built-in ChromeOS tool and most browser-based signing tools add a visual drawing to the PDF page. For a lot of everyday situations, like signing a permission slip, accepting a contractor quote, or returning a signed HR form, this is perfectly sufficient. The document carries your handwriting and a timestamp.

For contracts governed by regulations like the [US Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (ESIGN)](https://www.ftc.gov/tips-advice/business-center/guidance/complying-esign-act) or the [EU eIDAS regulation](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eidas-regulation), you may need a signature with an identity trail and an audit log. Those require a dedicated e-signature platform. If you're not sure, ask the other party before you sign whether they require something more formal.

For the vast majority of documents most people sign on a Chromebook, a drawn signature is exactly what's needed.

## What about DocuSign and similar services on Chromebook?

DocuSign, Adobe Sign, and similar platforms all work through the browser, so they run without any issues on a Chromebook. If someone sends you a DocuSign link, open it in Chrome and follow the same steps you would on any other computer.

The main reason people look for free alternatives is that those services charge a subscription for sending documents. For signing documents that others send you, they're usually free. For signing your own documents before emailing them or uploading them to a portal, a browser tool costs nothing.

## Saving and sending a signed PDF from Chromebook

Once you've downloaded the signed PDF, it lands in your Downloads folder. From there you can:

- Open the Files app and move it to Google Drive for cloud access.
- Share it directly from the Files app to Gmail as an attachment.
- Upload it to a school portal, government form, or job application site.

If you signed via the built-in viewer and pressed Ctrl+S, the file saves in place with the original filename. If you used a browser tool and clicked Download, rename the file before you send it so the recipient can tell which version it is, especially if you went through a few drafts.

Signing PDFs on a Chromebook is genuinely painless once you know the two routes. The built-in tool covers most everyday signing, and a browser-based signing tool fills in when you need more precision.
