Compressing a PDF on Android is faster than most people think, and you don't need to install anything. Open Chrome, go to the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf), pick your file, and download a smaller version in under a minute. Your file stays in your browser the whole time.

## Why most PDF compressor apps are not worth installing

Search the Play Store for "PDF compressor" and you'll find dozens of apps. Most of them have two things in common: they upload your document to a remote server, and they show ads or require a paid upgrade for anything useful.

A browser-based tool skips all of that. The processing happens inside Chrome's JavaScript engine on your phone. The PDF never leaves your device, there's no account to create, and you pay nothing.

## What kind of PDF can actually be compressed?

This is the part most guides leave out, and it matters.

The compressor works by converting every page to a [JPEG image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG). That helps a lot when the page is already a photograph or a scanned document. It hurts when the page is text.

In testing, a six-page image deck went from 913.4 KB down to 315.3 KB at Medium quality. The same compressor took a 20-page text report from 23.5 KB to 1.4 MB. Text stored as vector characters is tiny; a photograph of the same text is not.

**Compress these:** Scanned pages, photo-heavy presentations, PDFs made from phone camera shots.

**Don't compress these:** Invoices, contracts, forms, Word documents exported to PDF, anything that's mostly text.

If your text PDF is too large for email, the culprit is usually embedded fonts, a high-resolution logo, or revision history baked into the file. Try merging without the cover page, or zip the file before attaching it.

## How to compress a PDF on Android using Chrome

You need Chrome or any modern browser on Android. The tool works on Chrome, Firefox, Samsung Internet, and Edge.

1. Open your browser and go to the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf).
2. Tap the upload area or the file picker button. Your phone's file browser opens.
3. Find your PDF. It may be in Downloads, Google Drive, or another app's folder.
4. Pick a quality level: Low keeps the best quality with a modest size reduction, Medium is the default for everyday sharing, High makes the smallest file but reduces image sharpness.
5. Tap Compress PDF and wait a few seconds.
6. Tap Download to save the smaller file back to your phone.

From there you can attach it to Gmail, WhatsApp, or any other app.

## How to attach the compressed file to Gmail on Android

[Gmail's attachment limit is 25 MB](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6584). Because email uses Base64 encoding to carry binary files, a PDF of around 18 MB is the practical ceiling before Gmail will reject it and offer to send a Drive link instead.

Once your PDF is compressed and saved to Downloads:

1. Open Gmail and start composing a message.
2. Tap the paperclip icon.
3. Choose Attach file.
4. Go to Downloads and pick the compressed PDF.
5. Send.

If the file is still too large after compression, it may be a text-heavy PDF that actually grew during compression (see above). In that case, try sending it as a Google Drive link instead: in Gmail, tap the paperclip and choose Insert from Drive.

## Don't compress a PDF twice

A common instinct is to run a second pass if the first one didn't shrink the file enough. It doesn't work.

When the compressor runs a second time, it re-renders the already-rasterized pages at a larger internal scale before re-encoding them as JPEG. In testing, a 315 KB file that had already been through Medium compression came out at 599 KB on a second Medium pass.

If Medium wasn't enough, go back to your original file and run it at High instead. Never chain passes.

## Choosing between Low, Medium, and High compression

| Level | Best for | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Images you want to print or zoom into closely | Modest size reduction, high quality |
| Medium | Everyday sharing via email or messaging | Good balance, this is the default |
| High | Attachments where size matters most | Smallest file, some visible softness in images |

The right level depends on what you're doing with the file. A PDF you're sending to a hiring manager looks better at Low or Medium. A PDF you're uploading to a form that has a strict size cap warrants High.

## Using the tool from your phone's home screen

Android lets you add any website to your home screen as a shortcut. Open the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) in Chrome, tap the three-dot menu, and tap "Add to Home screen." After that, one tap gets you to the tool directly, and it feels close enough to an app that you won't miss the Play Store version.

## What to do if your PDF won't open in the file picker

Some Android versions or file manager apps hide PDFs unless you change the filter. In the file picker, look for a dropdown or menu that says "All files" and switch to it. If the PDF is saved inside a messaging app's folder (WhatsApp, Telegram), use that app's "Share" function and choose the compressor from the share sheet, or save the file to Downloads first.
