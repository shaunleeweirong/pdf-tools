You don't need Adobe Reader to open a PDF. Every modern browser opens PDFs without any plug-in, and every operating system ships with at least one free PDF viewer. Here's how to open PDFs on any device, plus what to do when you need to edit, compress, or sign them.

## Why most people still think they need Adobe

The assumption that PDFs require Adobe Reader traces back to the early 2000s, when Adobe was the only way to read the [PDF format it had invented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF). Since 2008, browsers and operating systems have included their own PDF engines, and today Adobe's software is rarely necessary for day-to-day reading.

Adobe Acrobat Reader (the free version) also loads slowly, installs background update services, and regularly prompts you to upgrade to the paid Pro product. For reading a PDF, you're better off without it.

## Open a PDF in your browser (Windows, Mac, Linux, Chromebook)

This works on any desktop with Chrome, Firefox, Edge, or Safari installed.

1. Open your browser.
2. Drag the PDF file from your file manager and drop it onto any browser tab.
3. The file opens in a new tab. Use Ctrl+F (Cmd+F on Mac) to search, the scroll wheel to move through pages, and Ctrl+P (Cmd+P) to print.

You can also right-click the PDF file in Windows Explorer or macOS Finder, choose **Open with**, and select your browser.

Chrome lets you control how PDFs open: go to **Settings > Privacy and security > Site settings > Additional content settings > PDF documents** and choose whether PDFs open in Chrome or download automatically. [Chrome's PDF support page](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/6213030) covers these settings in detail.

## Open PDFs on Mac without Adobe

macOS includes **Preview**, which opens PDFs without any additional software. Double-click any PDF in Finder, and Preview opens it.

Preview does more than just display pages. You can:
- Annotate with highlights, notes, and shapes.
- Fill in basic form fields.
- Rotate or delete individual pages.
- Sign the document using a trackpad or camera signature.
- Merge two PDFs by dragging pages from the sidebar.

[Apple's Preview guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/preview/welcome/mac) covers the full feature set. For anything beyond what Preview handles, the browser-based tools below pick up where it leaves off.

## Open PDFs on Windows without Adobe

Windows 10 and Windows 11 both come with Microsoft Edge, which is set as the default PDF viewer from the start. Double-click any PDF and Edge opens it. The Edge viewer handles searching, printing, highlights, and basic form filling.

If you'd prefer Chrome as your default PDF viewer, right-click a PDF file, choose **Open with > Choose another app**, select Chrome, and check **Always use this app to open .pdf files**.

## Open PDFs on iPhone and iPad without Adobe

Safari on iOS opens PDFs inline when you tap a link. You can also open them from the Files app: tap any PDF, and iOS displays it in the built-in viewer.

For PDFs you receive in Mail, tap the attachment and it previews in-app. From there, tap the share icon to send it to another app, annotate it in Markup, or save it to Files.

## Open PDFs on Android without Adobe

Chrome for Android opens PDFs automatically when you tap a link. Downloaded PDFs open through the Files app; tapping one will prompt you to choose an app. Google's Files app, Samsung's My Files, and many other pre-installed file managers open PDFs directly.

## What if you need to do more than read?

A viewer is fine for reading, but if you need to edit, shrink, combine, or convert a PDF, free browser-based tools handle those tasks without installing anything.

**Edit text or add annotations.** The [Edit PDF tool](/edit-pdf) lets you click into existing text to change it, or add new text boxes and shapes anywhere on the page. It runs in your browser, no account needed.

**Reduce the file size.** If a PDF is too large to attach to an email, try the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf). It works by rasterizing pages to JPEG, so it gives the best results on scanned documents and image-heavy decks. A text-only PDF will actually get larger after compression, not smaller, so for contracts and invoices the better move is to split out pages you don't need or ask the sender for a smaller version.

**Combine files.** The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) joins two or more PDFs into one. It does not inflate the output: the merged file comes out within a few bytes of the combined size of its inputs, so you won't push a file over an email limit by merging.

**Extract pages as images.** If a recipient's system won't accept a PDF, [PDF to JPG](/pdf-to-jpg) converts each page to a JPG image that almost anything will open.

All of these tools run in your browser, with files processed on your device or returned immediately, no cloud account or download required.

## How to stop Adobe Reader from opening your PDFs

If Adobe Reader has claimed the default association and double-clicking a PDF still opens it, you can change this in a few steps.

**Windows 10 / 11:**
1. Right-click any PDF file.
2. Choose **Open with > Choose another app**.
3. Select Edge, Chrome, Firefox, or another viewer from the list.
4. Check **Always use this app to open .pdf files** and click OK.

**Mac:**
1. Right-click (or Ctrl-click) a PDF and choose **Get Info** (or press Cmd+I).
2. Under the **Open with** section, open the dropdown and pick Preview or your browser.
3. Click **Change All** and confirm. All PDFs will now open in that app.

Once you've done this, Adobe Reader stays installed (you can still launch it manually if you ever need it) but it won't automatically take over new files.
