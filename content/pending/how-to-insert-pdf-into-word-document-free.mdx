Inserting a PDF into a Word document is free, and Word already has what you need. The trick is picking the right method for what you actually want to do with the content. This guide walks through three approaches in plain terms.

## Method 1: Embed the PDF as an Object (Best for Attaching the File)

Word can embed your entire PDF as a clickable icon. Readers open the icon to view the PDF in their default PDF viewer, but the content doesn't flow into the document's pages. It's essentially an attachment that travels inside the .docx file.

Microsoft documents this under [Insert an object in Word](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/insert-an-object-in-your-word-document-8fc1ea53-0e01-4603-a4cf-98c49b6ea3f5).

**How to do it:**

1. Click in your Word document where you want the icon to appear.
2. Go to the **Insert** tab and click **Object** (in the Text group on the ribbon).
3. Select the **Create from File** tab.
4. Click **Browse**, find your PDF, and click **Insert**.
5. Check **Display as icon** if you'd rather show a small icon than a large static preview of the first page.
6. Click **OK**.

That's it. The PDF is now inside the .docx file. Readers double-click the icon to open it.

**What works well:** contracts, appendices, and reference documents you want attached but not visible inline.

**What doesn't work:** if you need the PDF's pages to appear as content inside your Word document, this method won't do that. It shows either a single-page preview or a small icon.

**One thing to know:** if you update the original PDF, the embedded copy inside Word won't update. You'd need to delete and re-insert it.

## Method 2: Insert PDF Pages as Images (Best for Visual Accuracy)

If you want the PDF's pages to actually appear inside the Word document as readable content, converting to images first gives the cleanest result. This is especially useful for PDFs with diagrams, charts, or any design where the visual layout matters.

**Step 1: Convert PDF pages to images**

Open the [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) and upload your PDF. Each page becomes a separate JPG file. Download the images when the conversion is done. The tool runs in your browser and doesn't send your file to any server.

If you only need a few pages from a longer PDF, use the [Extract Pages tool](/extract-pages) first to pull out just those pages before converting.

**Step 2: Insert the images into Word**

1. Click where you want the first page to appear.
2. Go to **Insert > Pictures > This Device** (or **Insert > Pictures** on older versions).
3. Select the JPG and click **Insert**.
4. Repeat for each page you want to include.

After inserting, set the image wrapping to **In Line with Text** (click the small icon to the image's right after selecting it) so the pages sit in the document's flow instead of floating over the text.

**Why not just take a screenshot instead?** Screenshots capture whatever your screen resolution happens to be at that moment, usually 96 to 120 DPI on a standard monitor. That's fine for reading on screen but can look soft when printed. A PDF-to-image conversion renders from the file's own vector or raster data, which produces sharper output independent of your screen resolution.

## Method 3: Insert PDF Text as Editable Content

Word has a feature that tries to pull a PDF's text directly into the document as editable paragraphs. It's a bit hidden.

1. Click where you want the content to appear.
2. Go to **Insert** and click the small **dropdown arrow** next to Object.
3. Choose **Text from File**.
4. Select your PDF and click **Insert**.

Word converts the PDF and places the content into the document.

**Where this works:** PDFs made entirely of plain text with a single column and no complex formatting. A simple report or a plain-text contract often comes through reasonably well.

**Where it fails:** multi-column layouts, tables, headers and footers, forms, and PDFs with a lot of images or custom fonts. In these cases the text often comes through in the wrong order, with odd line breaks, or with the images missing entirely. The more visually complex the PDF, the worse the result.

If you need clean editable text from a complex PDF, a browser-based PDF-to-Word converter will generally do better than Word's built-in feature.

## Choosing the Right Method

| What you want | Best method |
|---|---|
| Attach the PDF so readers can open it | Embed as object (Method 1) |
| Show PDF pages inline in the document | Convert to images, then insert (Method 2) |
| Get editable text from a simple PDF | Insert > Text from File (Method 3) |

Most of the time, Method 1 covers the "I want this PDF included with my document" use case, and Method 2 covers "I want people to see the pages without opening a separate file."

## What About File Size?

Embedding adds the PDF's full size to your Word file. Images do too, though Word compresses them slightly when you save the document.

If the resulting .docx is too large to send by email, it's worth reducing the PDF size first. The [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) works well on image-heavy PDFs, particularly scans and photo-based documents. It rasterizes each page to JPEG, which cuts file size significantly for those types. However, if the PDF is mostly text, compressing it before embedding won't help and may make it bigger. For a 20-page text report, the same compressor grew the file from 23.5 KB to 1.4 MB because converting text to raster images takes more space than storing the text as vector data.

For text-heavy PDFs you want to trim down before embedding, try extracting just the pages you need with the [Extract Pages tool](/extract-pages) to drop pages that aren't necessary.

## Can You Insert a PDF into a Table Cell in Word?

Yes, with some limits. You can insert an image (from Method 2) into a table cell: click inside the cell, then go to **Insert > Pictures**. Resize it to fit the cell by dragging the image handles, or widen the column first.

You can't embed a PDF object (Method 1) inside a table cell. Word doesn't allow that combination. If that's what you need, place the object outside the table or convert to an image instead.

## Summary

Three free approaches: embed as a clickable object for attaching the file, convert pages to images for visual accuracy, or use Text from File to pull in editable content from simple PDFs. The [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) handles the image conversion in-browser with no account needed, and the result is sharper than a screenshot.

The [PDF format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) stores content as a portable document, not as a Word-editable file, so none of these methods preserve formatting perfectly. Knowing which trade-off matters for your situation makes it easier to pick the right one and avoid redoing the work.
