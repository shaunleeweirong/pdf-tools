Scanning a document to PDF is free and takes under a minute on any phone or computer. No paid app and no sign-up required. The right method depends on what device you have, but every major platform now has a built-in option that gets the job done.

Here is how to do it on each platform, plus a browser-based method that works on anything.

## How to scan to PDF on iPhone

Your iPhone has a document scanner built right in. No extra app needed. Open **Notes**, create or open a note, and tap the camera icon above the keyboard. Choose **Scan Documents**. Hold your phone above the page and it will scan automatically when it detects the document edges, or you can tap the shutter yourself.

To scan multiple pages into a single PDF, keep tapping after each page without pressing Save. When you're done with all pages, tap **Save** and the whole document becomes one PDF inside the note.

To export the file, tap the share icon and choose how you want to send it. If you want the PDF saved directly to a folder rather than in a note, the **Files app** has the same scanner: open Files, go to your folder, tap the three-dot menu, and choose **Scan Documents**.

Both options are completely free and need no account. The scanner has been part of iOS since version 11.

## How to scan to PDF on Android

On Android, the scanner is built into the Google Drive app, which comes pre-installed on most phones. [Google's documentation covers it in detail](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/3145835):

1. Open **Google Drive**.
2. Tap the blue **+** button at the bottom right, then tap **Scan**.
3. Aim at your document. The app auto-detects the edges and corrects perspective.
4. Tap the shutter. Tap **+** to add more pages.
5. When finished, tap the checkmark, name the file, and save.

The PDF saves to your Drive and is immediately accessible from any device. If you don't see a Scan option, check for a camera icon directly on the main screen instead of inside the **+** menu, as Google has moved it around in recent app updates.

If you don't have Google Drive or prefer not to use it, Microsoft Lens is a well-regarded free alternative that saves directly to PDF.

## How to scan to PDF on Mac

**With a physical scanner connected:** Open **Image Capture** from your Applications folder. Select your scanner from the left sidebar, set the Format to PDF, and click Scan. The file saves to whatever folder you choose.

**Without a physical scanner:** If you have an iPhone on the same Wi-Fi network and signed in to the same Apple ID, macOS supports [Continuity Camera](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111829) from Ventura onwards. In Finder, right-click inside a folder, choose **Import from iPhone**, then **Scan Document**. Your iPhone wakes up into scanning mode, you scan the page, and the result appears on your Mac automatically. No cable needed.

## How to scan to PDF on Windows

Windows 10 and 11 include two free tools for physical scanners:

**Windows Scan app:** The simpler option. If it isn't already installed, it's free in the Microsoft Store. Open it, choose your scanner from the dropdown, set File type to PDF, and click Scan.

**Windows Fax and Scan:** Also built in. Press Start, search for "Windows Fax and Scan", click **New Scan**, choose your settings, and save.

If you don't have a physical scanner at all, the fastest option is to photograph your document with your phone and convert the images using the browser method below.

## How to turn phone photos into a PDF

No scanner and want to skip downloading apps? Take a photo of each page with your phone's regular camera and convert in your browser:

1. Lay the document flat on a solid surface.
2. Photograph each page. Use good overhead lighting and hold your phone directly above the page.
3. Open the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) in any browser.
4. Drop in your photos (JPG or PNG). Drag to reorder if the pages are out of sequence.
5. Click **Convert** and download the PDF.

The conversion runs entirely in your browser. Your files are never uploaded to a server, so nothing leaves your device. No account, no file size warnings, no watermarks.

This works on phone or desktop and is useful any time you already have photos of a document and just need them bundled into a PDF.

## How to scan multiple pages into one PDF

Each platform handles multi-page scans a little differently:

| Platform | How to add more pages |
|---|---|
| iPhone Notes or Files | Tap the shutter for each page, then tap **Save** at the end |
| Android Google Drive | Tap **+** after each page |
| Mac Image Capture | Use the flatbed or feeder and scan all pages before saving |
| Mac Continuity Camera | Tap **Keep Scan** and then **Add Page** for each additional page |
| Photos and browser tool | Drop all image files at once before converting |

If you already have separate single-page PDF files and need to merge them, the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) combines them in your browser without any re-uploading or quality loss.

## Tips for a cleaner scan

The scanning app matters less than the conditions you scan in:

- **Overhead light, not side light.** A desk lamp at an angle creates shadows across the page. Ceiling lights work well. Natural light from a window is good too, as long as the sun isn't hitting the page directly.
- **Flat surface.** Curled corners, a spine crease, or a book held open will distort the scan. Press the page flat before you aim the camera.
- **Phone directly above the page.** Shooting at an angle stretches the text and makes it harder to read even after the app corrects for it.
- **Check before you save.** Zoom in to confirm all four corners are included and the text looks sharp. Re-scanning takes five seconds; fixing a blurry page later takes much longer.
- **Scanned PDFs tend to be larger than text PDFs.** Each page is stored as an image rather than as vector text, so the file size adds up. If you need to reduce it, the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) is designed for image-heavy files, which is exactly what a scanned document is.

One common issue: many scanner apps apply light compression by default, which works fine for most documents. If you're scanning something with fine print or small details and it looks soft, dig into the app settings and set quality to the highest option before scanning again.
