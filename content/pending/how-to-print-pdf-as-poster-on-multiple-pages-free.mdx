To print a PDF as a poster on multiple pages free, open it in Adobe Acrobat Reader, press **Ctrl+P** (Windows) or **Cmd+P** (Mac), and choose **Poster** under Page Sizing and Handling. Acrobat tiles the page across as many sheets as needed and prints trim marks so the pieces line up. If Poster is greyed out in your version, free browser tools handle it just as well.

## What is poster printing (tiling)?

Tiling takes one PDF page and breaks it into a grid of smaller pages, each sized for a standard printer. You print each section, then tape or glue them together to get a full-size poster, floor plan, classroom sign, or banner.

Most home and office printers max out at A4 or US Letter (8.5 x 11 in). Tiling lets you use that printer to produce output much larger than a single sheet, like a four-sheet poster at roughly 17 x 22 in.

Note that tiling is only useful when a single PDF page needs to print at a size larger than your printer's paper. If you have a multi-page PDF and want to print several slides on one sheet instead, that's [N-up printing](/n-up-pdf), which is the opposite task.

## Method 1: Adobe Acrobat Reader (free desktop app)

Acrobat Reader is the most reliable free option. [Adobe documents the poster-print steps](https://helpx.adobe.com/acrobat/using/printing-pdfs-custom-sizes.html) in its official help pages.

1. Open the PDF in Acrobat Reader. If you don't have it, download the free version from adobe.com.
2. Press **Ctrl+P** on Windows or **Cmd+P** on Mac.
3. Under **Page Sizing and Handling**, click **Poster**.
4. Set **Tile Scale** to 100% to print at the document's actual size. Increase it to print larger.
5. Set **Overlap** to around 0.5 inches so adjacent sheets share a small strip you can use as an alignment guide.
6. Check **Cut marks** to get crop lines on each sheet.
7. Look at the preview to confirm how many sheets you'll need, then click **Print**.

One practical caveat: Adobe has moved some print features behind a subscription in recent versions. If the Poster button is greyed out, skip to Method 2.

## Method 2: Free browser-based tools

These run entirely in your browser, no software to install, and work on Windows, Mac, Chromebook, and Linux.

**Docuslice** is a solid first choice when privacy matters. It processes your PDF on-device and does not upload anything to a server. You pick the paper size and number of columns and rows, and it generates a ready-to-print PDF.

**TileStencil** produces a multi-page PDF with built-in cut marks and overlap guides. The output opens in any browser's standard print dialog, so you don't need any special printing features.

**PDF2Go** is fast and simple. It does upload your file to its server, so use it only for non-sensitive documents. Set the paper size, click convert, and download the tiled PDF.

The browser route is particularly handy on a Chromebook or a locked-down work computer where you can't install software.

## Method 3: PosteRazor (free, open-source desktop software)

[PosteRazor](https://posterazor.sourceforge.net/) is a long-running open-source tool for Windows, Mac, and Linux. It takes an image file and produces a tiled multi-page PDF you can print anywhere.

Since PosteRazor works with images rather than PDFs directly, you need to export the PDF page as a JPEG first. The [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) does that in seconds: upload the PDF, download the image, then open it in PosteRazor.

The extra step adds a minute to your workflow, but PosteRazor gives you fine-grained control over output size in centimeters or inches, and the software runs fully offline.

## How to isolate just the page you need

If your PDF has several pages and you only want to tile one of them, extract it first. The [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) lets you pick a page range and download just those pages as a new file. Upload that single-page PDF to whichever tiling method you choose.

This avoids confusion where the tiling tool spreads every page across multiple sheets instead of just the one you want.

## Assembling the printed tiles

After printing, here's how to put the sheets together cleanly:

1. Lay all sheets on a flat surface in order. Acrobat and most tools print a page number or grid reference in a corner.
2. Working one row at a time, trim the white margin on the edges that meet an adjacent sheet. Leave the overlap strip on one side; cut it away on the other.
3. Align the trim marks and tape the back with clear tape. A light table or a window makes alignment easier.
4. Repeat row by row, then tape the rows together.

A 0.5-inch overlap gives enough room to cut cleanly and still have the image line up without a visible gap.

## What about Mac's Preview app?

Apple's Preview does not include a poster/tiling print mode. On a Mac, your free options are Acrobat Reader (if the Poster option is available in your version) or any of the browser tools above. Chrome and Safari on Mac also lack tiling, so the browser's built-in PDF viewer won't help regardless of which browser you use.

## Not what you need?

If you want to print multiple PDF pages onto fewer sheets (like a 2-up handout), that's a different task. The [Pages Per Sheet tool](/n-up-pdf) puts 2 or 4 pages onto each sheet and works directly in your browser without any print-dialog fiddling.
