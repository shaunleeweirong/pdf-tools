You can convert a PDF to Google Slides for free using two methods: turn each PDF page into an image and insert those images as slides, or convert the PDF to a PowerPoint file first and open that in Google Slides. The image method is faster and keeps your layout exactly as-is. The PowerPoint route takes a few extra steps but gives you editable text inside the slides.

One thing to know upfront: [Google Slides does not support importing PDF files directly](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/2511782). There's no "open PDF" option in Slides itself. Both methods below work around this by converting the PDF into a format Slides does understand.

## Method 1: Convert PDF pages to images, then insert into Slides

This is the best approach for presentations, handouts, scanned pages, or any PDF where you want it to look exactly right on screen. Because each slide shows an image of the page rather than trying to replicate the layout with slide elements, the output matches the original pixel for pixel.

**Step 1: Convert your PDF to JPG images**

Go to the [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) and upload your PDF. The tool runs in your browser and converts each page into a separate high-resolution JPG. Download the images when done. No account needed.

If you only need certain pages rather than the whole document, use the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) first to extract just the pages you want, then convert those.

**Step 2: Open Google Slides and set your slide size**

Go to [Google Slides](https://slides.google.com) and open a new or existing presentation.

Most PDFs are portrait-oriented (taller than wide), but Google Slides defaults to 16:9 widescreen. If you insert a portrait image into a widescreen slide, you'll get gray bars down the sides. To fix this before you start, go to **File > Page setup > Custom** and type in your PDF's page dimensions in inches. A4 is 8.27 by 11.69 inches. US Letter is 8.5 by 11 inches. Click Apply.

**Step 3: Insert the images, one per slide**

Click on the first blank slide. Choose **Insert > Image > Upload from computer** and select the JPG for page 1. Drag the corners of the image to fill the slide area. Repeat for each subsequent page.

To save time on longer PDFs, open the file picker (Insert > Image > Upload from computer) and shift-click to select multiple JPGs at once. Google Slides will create one slide per image and insert them in the order they appear in your file list.

**What you end up with:** Each slide is a faithful image of a PDF page. Text, fonts, colors, and layout are all preserved. The tradeoff is that you can't click on the text inside a slide and edit it, because it's part of the image.

## Method 2: Convert PDF to PowerPoint, then open in Google Slides

Use this route when you need to edit the slide content after importing. [PDF is a fixed page-description format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) not designed for editing, so getting editable elements out of one requires a conversion step that reads the underlying structure.

**Step 1: Convert your PDF to .pptx**

Use a free online PDF-to-PPTX converter (search "pdf to pptx free" for options). Upload your PDF, run the conversion, and download the .pptx file to your computer.

**Step 2: Upload to Google Drive**

Go to [Google Drive](https://drive.google.com), click **New > File upload**, and select the .pptx file. Once it's uploaded, double-click the file and choose **Open with Google Slides**. Drive converts it into a Google Slides presentation automatically.

**Step 3: Review and clean up**

Text should be editable and slide structure mostly intact. Fonts not available in Google's library may substitute, and PDFs with complex multi-column layouts or those exported from design tools can shift noticeably. Budget time to go slide by slide and fix anything that moved.

**What you end up with:** Editable text and slide elements. Best results when the PDF was originally built from a PowerPoint or Word document. Weaker results on scanned documents or design-heavy PDFs.

## Which method should I use?

| Situation | Best method |
|---|---|
| Presenting a report, textbook, or handout | Images (Method 1) |
| Need to edit the slide text | PowerPoint (Method 2) |
| Complex design or visual layout | Images (Method 1, no reformatting risk) |
| PDF was originally made from a PowerPoint | PowerPoint (Method 2, better fidelity) |
| Scanned document | Images (Method 1, PPTX converters can't read scans) |

## Tips for better results

**Reorder pages before you start.** If your PDF's page sequence isn't right, fixing it in Google Slides later means dragging slides around one at a time. Use the [Organize PDF tool](/organize-pdf) to rearrange pages before converting. A few seconds at that stage saves several minutes in Slides.

**Portrait PDFs in a 16:9 presentation.** If you decide to leave the slide dimensions at 16:9 rather than matching the PDF, resize each inserted image to fit the visible area and use a white or colored background to fill the side bars. Go to **Slide > Edit theme** and set a background color, or right-click a slide and choose **Change background**.

**Large PDFs with many pages.** A 30-page PDF becomes 30 slides. For the image method, batch-selecting all 30 JPGs in one file-picker open is the fastest path. If your PDF runs longer, consider whether you need every page. Split out just the relevant section first, convert that, and keep the rest as a separate reference PDF rather than a 100-slide deck nobody will scroll through.

**Image quality for projectors.** The PDF to JPG tool exports at a resolution suited to screen display. On a large projector screen the images will be sharp. If you're presenting on a 4K display and notice softness, this reflects the source PDF's resolution rather than the conversion.

## Converting PDF to Google Slides on a phone

Open your mobile browser and visit the [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg). Upload your PDF, let it convert, and download the resulting images to your phone's camera roll or files app.

Open the Google Slides app, tap the presentation you want to edit, press the plus icon to add a new slide, then tap **Insert > Image > Photo library** (or **Files** on Android) and select the first page image. Repeat for each page.

It's more tapping than on a desktop, but the result is the same. The image method works well on mobile because you're just inserting photos into slides, which is a standard Slides feature on every platform.

For the PPTX method on a phone: upload the .pptx to Google Drive via the Drive app, open it in Slides, and review the result. Heavy layout cleanup is easier on a desktop if you have access to one.
