You can compress a PDF for a scholarship application in your browser, for free, with no account required. Most scholarship portals reject files over 2 MB to 10 MB, and a stack of scanned transcripts and recommendation letters can easily hit that ceiling. The [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) handles it in a few seconds.

Before you start, though, one thing matters more than which tool you use: knowing which documents to compress and which to leave alone.

## Why scholarship portals have file size limits

[PDF files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) grow large when documents are scanned at high resolution. A transcript scanned at 600 DPI produces a file two to four times larger than the same scan at 300 DPI, and most portals don't need print-quality resolution to review an application on screen.

Common limits across scholarship and financial aid portals:

- UNSW scholarships: 10 MB per supporting document
- University of Newcastle (Australia): 5 MB per document type
- Many government and private portals: 2 MB to 5 MB
- Some smaller organizations: as low as 100 KB to 500 KB

Check your portal's upload page or FAQ for the exact limit before you compress. Some portals also specify the accepted format (PDF vs. PDF/A), which matters if your file gets rejected even after it's small enough.

## Which documents should you compress?

This is the step most guides skip. Compression behaves very differently depending on what's inside your PDF.

**Compress these:**
- Scanned transcripts and academic records
- Scanned letters of recommendation (when your referee sent a physical copy that was then scanned)
- Photo ID scans, passport pages, or national ID documents
- Any PDF that started life as a photograph or a physical document you ran through a scanner

**Don't compress these:**
- Typed personal statements and essays
- Word-to-PDF exports of forms or application templates
- Digitally created certificates or award letters

Here's why it matters: our compressor converts each page to a JPEG image. For scanned documents this cuts file size significantly. For a typed document stored as text and vector glyphs, JPEG conversion makes it dramatically larger. A typed 20-page report at 23.5 KB grew to over 1.4 MB after compression, more than 60 times the original size. Don't compress what's already small.

## How to compress a scanned PDF for your scholarship application

1. Go to the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf). Your file stays in your browser; nothing is sent to a server.
2. Click **Select PDF** and open your scanned document.
3. Choose a compression level:
   - **Low (best quality):** Reduces an image-heavy PDF by roughly 23%. Good when you need to keep fine text or signatures crisp.
   - **Medium:** Reduces an image-heavy PDF by about 65%. The right starting point for most transcripts and scanned letters.
   - **High (smallest):** Reduces an image-heavy PDF by about 81%. Use this if Medium doesn't get you under the portal's limit.
4. Click **Compress** and wait a few seconds.
5. Download the result and open it on your phone. If you can read it there, the reviewer can read it too. Submit if it's under the limit.

Start with Medium. If the file is still too large, go back to the **original** and rerun at High. Don't run compression on an already-compressed file. A second pass at the same level makes the file about 90% larger rather than smaller. Always start from the original.

## What if the PDF is still too large?

A few options when compression alone isn't enough:

**Remove pages you don't need.** Use the [Delete Pages tool](/delete-pages) to strip out blank pages, cover sheets, or pages the scholarship portal didn't ask for. Fewer pages means a smaller file, and you can then compress the trimmed version.

**Re-scan at lower resolution.** If you still have access to the original physical document, scan it at 200 to 300 DPI rather than 600 DPI. [The PDF/A standard](https://www.pdfa.org/resource/pdfa-in-a-nutshell/) used for archival documents recommends 300 DPI for scanned text. Anything higher is wasted data for a screen-only submission.

**Check if the portal accepts a ZIP archive.** A few portals let you bundle multiple PDFs in a ZIP file under a combined size limit. Read the submission instructions carefully.

## How to combine multiple scholarship documents into one PDF

Many scholarship portals ask for a single file that bundles your transcript, personal statement, and letters of recommendation. The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) handles this, and merging barely changes the total file size. Three files summing to 971.9 KB merged to 970.5 KB in our tests.

You can compress each scanned document individually and then merge, or merge everything and then compress, depending on your preference. Just remember: only run compression on pages that started as scans or images.

If any of your documents include a PDF form you filled out on screen, use the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) before merging. Flattening converts interactive form fields to static content so the values stay visible in the merged file.

## Before you hit submit: a quick checklist

- Typed documents (essays, personal statements): no compression needed. Submit as-is.
- Scanned documents: compressed at Medium or High from the original file, previewed on a phone.
- Combined file is under the portal's stated file size limit.
- Text and signatures are legible, not blurry.
- Page count matches what the portal expects.
