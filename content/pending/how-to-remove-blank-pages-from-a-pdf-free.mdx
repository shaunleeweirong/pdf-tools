Your file stays on your device throughout.

## Why blank pages end up in a PDF

Before you start deleting, it helps to know where blank pages come from. That way you can stop them appearing again in the next export.

**Duplex scanning** is the most common cause. When a scanner is set to capture both sides of each sheet, a stack of 10 single-sided originals becomes a 20-page PDF: one page of content, one blank back, repeated. Most ADF scanners have a "skip blank pages" setting, but it isn't always on by default. [Duplex scanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duplex_printing) is a useful feature for two-sided documents; it just needs the right setting for one-sided stacks.

**Word and Google Docs exports** often tack on a blank page at the end. A trailing paragraph sitting below the last line of content can push past the page boundary and create an empty page in the exported PDF. You'll see this especially in documents that have been edited many times.

**Merging PDFs** can introduce blank pages between sections. Some tools insert a blank page when combining files to maintain an odd/even page structure, so that each new section starts on a right-hand page. If you [merged PDFs](/merge-pdf) recently and new blank pages appeared between sections, this is likely why.

**Spreadsheets printed to PDF** sometimes include blank pages when empty worksheet tabs are included in the print selection. Deselect unused sheets before exporting to avoid it.

## How to find blank pages fast in a long document

Scrolling through a 50-page PDF looking for blanks is slow. Switch to a thumbnail view instead. Every blank page shows up as a white square with nothing on it, which makes them easy to spot at a glance.

The [Organize PDF tool](/organize-pdf) displays every page as a thumbnail grid. Blank pages stand out immediately. You can also use:

- **Preview on Mac**: View > Thumbnails shows a sidebar with every page.
- **Adobe Reader on Windows**: View > Navigation Panes > Pages opens a similar panel.
- **Chrome and Edge**: the built-in PDF viewer doesn't have a thumbnail sidebar, so a dedicated tool works better for longer documents.

Before you delete anything, note the page numbers. A document with blanks on pages 4, 11, and 23 is quick to clean up once you know the numbers.

## How to remove blank pages step by step

1. Open the [Delete Pages tool](/delete-pages).
2. Click "Choose file" or drag your PDF onto the upload area. Your PDF is processed in your browser only; nothing is sent to a server.
3. A thumbnail grid appears showing every page. Click each blank page to select it. Selected pages are highlighted.

That's the fastest path for a handful of blank pages. If you have many blanks spread through a long document, the [Organize PDF tool](/organize-pdf) gives you a better overview. You can select multiple pages at once from the grid and remove them all in a single step.

## Removing blank pages from a scanned PDF

Scanned PDFs work the same way. A scanned blank page is just a white image, and deleting it follows the same steps as above.

One thing to check: some pages look blank on screen but aren't truly empty. [OCR software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_character_recognition) sometimes places a white or transparent text layer on top of scanned content, and if the layer shifts or the background is misread, a page can appear white while still containing embedded text or images. A genuinely blank scanned page will have a very small file contribution; a page that only looks blank may be much larger. Deleting either one is safe, but it's worth a quick glance before you do.

On a Mac, you can handle scanned PDFs with Preview for free. Open the file, go to View > Thumbnails, click the blank page in the sidebar, and press Delete. Preview saves the change automatically.

## What happens to the rest of your document

Removing a page only removes that page. The [PDF format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) stores each page independently, so deleting one doesn't cause text on other pages to reflow or shift, unlike deleting content in a word processor.

The one thing to watch is page numbering. If your PDF has page numbers printed as text in a header or footer, those numbers are baked into the page content and won't update when you delete a page. A PDF that originally read "Page 4 of 50" will still say that on what is now page 4, even if the page count dropped to 49 after cleanup.

If accurate page numbers matter, use the [Add Page Numbers tool](/add-page-numbers) after cleaning up the blank pages. It inserts consistent numbering across all remaining pages.
