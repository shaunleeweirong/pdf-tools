You can print a PDF double-sided for free using apps you already have: Chrome, Microsoft Edge, or Mac Preview. No paid software is needed. Here's how to do it on each platform, plus a manual method if your printer doesn't support automatic duplex.

## What "double-sided" means (and what your printer needs)

Double-sided printing, often called duplex printing, puts content on both sides of each sheet. Most mid-range and office printers have a built-in duplex unit that feeds the paper back through automatically. Budget and older home printers often do not, but you can still print double-sided manually by flipping the stack yourself.

Before you try any of the steps below, check your printer specs. If the [printer's product page](https://support.hp.com/us-en/document/ish_1565112-1564673-16) or manual lists "automatic duplex" or "duplexing", you can use the automatic settings. If not, skip to the manual method below.

## Print double-sided on Windows (Chrome or Edge)

Both Chrome and Edge come with a built-in PDF viewer and print dialog that exposes duplex settings without any extra software.

1. Open your PDF in Chrome or Edge (drag the file into a browser tab, or right-click and choose "Open with").
2. Press **Ctrl+P** to open the print dialog.
3. Click **More settings** (Chrome) or expand the settings panel (Edge).
4. Under "Print on both sides", choose **Flip on long edge** for a standard portrait document, or **Flip on short edge** for landscape.
5. Set the number of copies and other options, then click **Print**.

If the option does not appear, click **Printer properties** or **Preferences** and look for a **Duplex**, **Two-sided**, or **Double-sided** setting under the Layout or Finishing tab. The exact tab name depends on your printer driver.

### Using the free Adobe Acrobat Reader on Windows

The free [Adobe Acrobat Reader](https://get.adobe.com/reader/) also exposes duplex settings through its print dialog.

1. Open your PDF in Acrobat Reader.
2. Press **Ctrl+P**.
3. Click **Properties** next to the printer name.
4. On the Layout tab, set "Print on Both Sides" to "Flip on Long Edge" or "Flip on Short Edge".
5. Click **OK**, then **Print**.

## Print double-sided on Mac (Preview)

Mac's built-in Preview app supports two-sided printing through a simple checkbox.

1. Open your PDF in Preview (double-click the file, or right-click and choose "Open With > Preview").
2. Go to **File > Print** (or press **Cmd+P**).
3. If you see a small arrow next to the printer name, click it to expand the full options panel.
4. Check the **Two-Sided** checkbox. It may sit below the page range options, or appear after you click the **Layout** option in the settings drop-down.
5. Set the layout to **Long-Edge binding** for portrait, or **Short-Edge binding** for landscape, if the option appears.
6. Click **Print**.

Preview's two-sided option only appears when the selected printer actually supports duplex. If you do not see it, your printer requires the manual method below.

## Flip on long edge vs flip on short edge: which to choose?

This setting determines how the back of each sheet lines up with the front.

- **Flip on long edge** (also called long-side binding): the back of each page is the right way up when you flip the sheet left-to-right, like turning a book page. Choose this for standard portrait-oriented documents.
- **Flip on short edge** (also called short-side binding): the back of each page is the right way up when you flip the sheet top-to-bottom, like flipping a calendar. Choose this for landscape-oriented documents.

Almost everything you print day-to-day is portrait, so flip on long edge is the right default.

## Manual duplex: printing double-sided without auto-duplex support

If your printer has no duplex unit, you can still get two-sided output by printing in two passes.

1. Open the print dialog and look for a way to print only **odd pages** (in Chrome: set a custom page range of 1, 3, 5 and so on; in Acrobat Reader: choose "Odd pages only" under Subset).
2. Print those pages and let them dry for a moment.
3. Take the printed stack and reload it face-down (or face-up, depending on how your printer feeds). You may need to test with a single sheet first to get the orientation right.
4. Print the **even pages** in reverse order if needed, so page 2 lands on the back of page 1. In Acrobat Reader, check "Reverse pages" when printing even pages only.

Chrome simplifies this process. In the print dialog, choosing **Print on both sides** on a printer without auto-duplex will prompt you to manually flip the paper mid-job.

The safest way to test is to print pages 1 and 2 on one sheet first, check the alignment, and adjust before running the full job.

## Saving even more paper: 2 pages per sheet

Double-sided printing cuts paper use in half. You can go further by placing two PDF pages on each side of a sheet, which puts four pages per physical piece of paper. That's often enough for handouts or reference documents where small text is acceptable.

The [Pages Per Sheet tool](/n-up-pdf) does this in your browser. It rearranges the PDF so that each output page contains two original pages side by side. You can then print that rearranged file single-sided and still use half the paper, which is useful if your printer does not support duplex.

## Combining PDFs before printing

If you want to print several PDFs as one double-sided job, merging them first keeps the page order correct. The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) combines multiple files into one PDF in seconds. Once merged, open the result and print it double-sided as described above.

## Why is the duplex option missing or greyed out?

A few common causes:

- **No hardware duplex unit.** Many entry-level home printers cannot print automatically on both sides. The option will not appear no matter what software you use.
- **Generic driver installed.** Windows sometimes installs a basic driver for a printer instead of the full manufacturer driver. The generic driver often hides advanced settings like duplex. Download the full driver from your printer manufacturer's support site (HP, Canon, Epson, Brother, etc.) and reinstall.
- **Wrong print dialog.** On Windows, the browser's simplified print preview does not always surface duplex settings. Click "Print using system dialog" or "More settings > Printer properties" to reach the full driver settings.

For printers confirmed to support duplex, reinstalling the correct driver fixes the greyed-out option in almost every case.
