The fastest free way to combine multiple Word documents into one PDF is a two-step process: export each Word file as a PDF, then merge those PDFs in your browser. No Acrobat, no subscription, no file upload to a third-party server.

## Why the two-step method works better than merging Word files directly

Most tools that claim to merge .docx files directly require you to upload your documents to their servers. That's a problem if your files contain anything sensitive, like a job application, a contract, or client data. Many also cap how many files you can process before asking you to pay or sign up.

The two-step approach sidesteps all of that. You convert each Word document to PDF yourself using software you already have, then merge the resulting PDFs with our [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool, which runs entirely in your browser. Nothing leaves your device during the merge step.

The output size won't surprise you either. Merging doesn't re-encode or inflate your files. The combined PDF ends up essentially the same size as all the input PDFs added together.

## Step 1: Export each Word document as a PDF

How you do this depends on which operating system you're on.

**Windows (Microsoft Word)**

1. Open the Word document.
2. Go to **File > Save As**.
3. Choose a location, then change **Save as type** to **PDF**.
4. Click **Save**.

Alternatively, use **File > Export > Create PDF/XPS** and click **Publish**. Both produce the same result. [Microsoft's official guide covers both paths](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/save-or-convert-to-pdf-or-xps-in-office-desktop-apps-d85416c5-7d77-4fd6-a216-6f4bf7c7c110) if you want the full walkthrough.

Repeat for every document you want to include.

**Mac (Microsoft Word)**

1. Open the Word document.
2. Go to **File > Save As**.
3. In the Format dropdown at the bottom, choose **PDF**.
4. Click **Save**.

You can also go to **File > Print**, click the **PDF** dropdown at the bottom left of the print dialog, and choose **Save as PDF**. That path works in any Mac app, not just Word.

**No Microsoft Word? Use Google Docs or LibreOffice**

If you don't have a Word subscription, you have two solid free options:

- **Google Docs:** Go to [docs.google.com](https://docs.google.com), upload your .docx file, open it, then click **File > Download > PDF Document (.pdf)**. The conversion preserves most formatting for standard documents.
- **LibreOffice:** [LibreOffice](https://www.libreoffice.org/) is free, open-source, and runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux. Open your .docx file in Writer, then go to **File > Export as PDF**. It handles complex Word formatting better than many online converters.

**Chromebook**

On a Chromebook, you likely don't have Word installed. The Google Docs method above is your easiest path. Upload the .docx to Google Drive, open it in Google Docs, and export it as a PDF.

## Step 2: Merge the PDFs in your browser

Once you have a PDF for each Word document, head to the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool.

1. Click **Add files** (or drag and drop your PDFs onto the page).
2. Rearrange the files into the order you want using drag and drop.
3. Click **Merge** and wait a moment.
4. Download your combined PDF.

That's it. No sign-up, no watermarks. The tool processes everything locally in your browser, so your files never leave your device.

## A note on filled-in forms

If any of your Word documents contain a form that someone has already filled in, watch out. When the merge tool combines PDFs, interactive form fields don't survive. The fields and their values are stripped in the process.

The fix is to flatten the filled PDF before merging. Use the [Flatten PDF](/flatten-pdf) tool on that file first. Flattening paints the field values directly into the page so they show up as regular text. Then merge the flattened PDF with the rest of your documents.

## What to do if the combined file is too large

Merging doesn't make files bigger, but if your Word documents contained high-resolution images, the combined PDF might be large enough to cause trouble attaching to an email (most email providers cap attachments at [25 MB](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6584)).

In that case, the [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) tool can help, but only if your documents are image-heavy. Compression works by rasterizing pages to JPEG. That shrinks photo-heavy files significantly, but makes text-only documents larger. Don't compress a document that's mostly text and tables: it'll get bigger, not smaller.

If your combined PDF is text-heavy and still too large for email, the file is probably large because of embedded fonts or a high-resolution logo. Compressing from a lower-quality original or removing the oversized element in Word first is the better fix.

## Common questions

**Do I need to convert the Word documents in a specific order?**

No. You pick the order yourself in the Merge PDF tool by dragging the files before you click Merge. You can also just add them in the order you want.

**What if the fonts look different in the PDF?**

When Word exports to PDF, it embeds the fonts that were used in the document. If a font wasn't available on the machine doing the export, Word substitutes a fallback. To avoid this, install any custom fonts on your computer before exporting. For most standard fonts like Calibri, Arial, or Times New Roman, this isn't an issue.

**Will page numbers carry over correctly?**

If your Word documents each have their own page numbering that starts at 1, the PDF pages will keep those numbers. The Merge PDF tool doesn't renumber pages. If you want continuous page numbering across the combined file, use the [Add Page Numbers](/add-page-numbers) tool after merging.
