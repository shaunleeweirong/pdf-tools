## Why your resume PDF is too large for Indeed

If your PDF is too large for an Indeed job application upload, the fix depends on what is actually inside the file. Most people reach for a generic compression tool and end up with a bigger file, blurry text, or both. Checking what is causing the bloat takes about 60 seconds and saves you from submitting a document that looks nothing like your original.

A well-built text resume exported from [Microsoft Word or Google Docs](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/save-or-convert-to-pdf-or-xps-in-office-desktop-apps-d85416c5-7d77-4fd6-a216-6f4bf7c7c110) is typically under 200 KB. If yours is hitting several megabytes, something specific is inflating it.

## What is Indeed's file size limit?

Based on widely reported user experience, Indeed accepts resume uploads up to around 5 MB, with PDF, DOC, DOCX, RTF, and TXT all supported. Even that limit is generous for a resume. If your file is bumping against it, something unusual is embedded in the document.

For the broadest compatibility, keep your resume under 1 MB. Many [applicant tracking systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applicant_tracking_system) used by companies that post on Indeed have their own tighter limits, and a smaller file also parses faster when a recruiter's system is scanning it for keywords.

## Why do resume PDFs get so large?

Before you change anything, check what is causing the size. The usual culprits:

**A high-resolution photo.** A profile picture or headshot saved at print quality (300 dpi or higher) adds megabytes to a single page. Even a small image can be surprisingly heavy at high resolution.

**A scanned resume.** If you printed a resume and scanned it, the entire document is now a photograph. Scanned PDFs are image files disguised as documents and are far larger than text PDFs of the same content.

**A high-resolution logo or graphic.** Decorative letterhead or company logos from a previous job's template, saved at print resolution, can add significant bulk.

**A sub-optimal export setting.** Microsoft Word has several PDF export modes. The default "Standard" mode produces a larger file than the "Minimum size" option. One setting change can cut the file size noticeably.

**Embedded fonts at full size.** Some PDF creators embed every character of a font rather than just the ones used. Most modern exporters avoid this, but some older tools do not.

## How to tell if your resume is text-based or image-based

Open the PDF in your browser or any PDF reader, then click on a word and try to drag to select it. If the text highlights as individual words, your resume is text-based. If nothing highlights, or a selection box appears around the whole page as one block, the file is a scanned image.

This test takes ten seconds and tells you exactly which fix to use.

## Fix 1: Re-export from Word or Google Docs (text-based resumes)

For most people, this is the right move. If you built your resume in Word or Google Docs, open the original file and export again with the right settings:

1. **In Microsoft Word:** File, then Save As, choose PDF, click Options, and select "Minimum size (publishing online)". Alternatively, File, then Export, then Create PDF/XPS, and switch the Optimize for option to "Minimum size".
2. **In Google Docs:** File, then Download, then PDF Document. Google's export is already compact by default.
3. **Remove embedded images you don't need.** A profile photo or decorative logo is the single easiest thing to cut. Delete the image, re-export, and the file often drops by 80% or more.

Do not run a rasterizing compression tool on a text-based resume. That kind of compressor converts every page to a JPEG image, turning crisp, searchable text into pixels. The result is often much larger than the original, not smaller. As a point of reference, a 23.5 KB text report run through our compressor came out at 1.4 MB, roughly 60 times bigger, because the tool rasterized 20 pages of plain text into 20 JPEG images.

## Fix 2: Compress the file (scanned or image-heavy resumes)

If your resume is a scan, or if it genuinely contains photos or graphics you cannot remove, compression is the appropriate tool.

Use the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) to upload your file and choose a compression level. For a scanned single-page resume, Medium compression typically cuts the file size by more than half while keeping text legible to a human reviewer. Start with Medium. If the result is still above the upload limit, start over from the original and try High.

A few things to keep in mind:

**Do not compress the file twice.** Running the output through compression a second time inflates the size instead of reducing it further. If Medium is not enough, go back to the original and run it at High.

**Check the result before submitting.** Open the compressed file and scroll through it. Your resume is a professional document and you want it looking right. A 30-second check saves an embarrassing submission.

**Compressed text may not be machine-readable.** Rasterization removes the selectable text layer. Some applicant tracking systems parse resume text automatically to pull out your name, skills, and experience. A rasterized file looks like a photograph to a text parser, so it may not be scanned correctly. If the role you are applying for is likely to use ATS screening, re-exporting from source is the safer choice.

## Fix 3: Trim pages with Split PDF (long CVs and portfolios)

If your PDF is long, say a multi-page academic CV or a design portfolio, the right fix may be to pull out the most relevant pages rather than trying to compress the whole thing.

The [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) lets you select page ranges and save them as a new file. Submit your one-to-two page resume, and attach the full portfolio or CV only when a job posting specifically asks for it.

## After you fix the file

Once your PDF is under the limit, upload it to your Indeed profile:

1. Sign in to Indeed and open your profile.
2. Go to the Resume section and click Upload Resume.
3. Select your updated file and confirm.
4. Check the preview Indeed shows you. If text looks scrambled or the formatting is off, that usually means the file converted incorrectly and is worth re-exporting and uploading again.

A clean, under-1-MB resume uploads in seconds, previews correctly, and works with the systems on both sides of the application. That is the outcome worth aiming for.
