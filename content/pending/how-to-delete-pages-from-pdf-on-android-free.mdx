You can delete pages from a PDF on Android for free, right in your browser, without downloading anything. Open the [Delete Pages tool](/delete-pages), pick your PDF, tap the pages you want gone, and download the result. The whole thing takes under a minute.

## The quickest method: delete pages in your browser

The [Delete Pages tool](/delete-pages) works in Chrome, Firefox, and any modern Android browser. It processes your file locally, so nothing gets uploaded to a server.

Here's how to do it:

1. Open [pdf.wtf/delete-pages](/delete-pages) in your Android browser.
2. Tap **Choose file** and select your PDF from your downloads folder, Google Drive, or wherever it's saved.
3. The tool shows a thumbnail of every page. Tap the pages you want to remove. Selected pages get highlighted.
4. Tap **Delete pages**.
5. Tap **Download** to save the updated PDF back to your device.

That's it. The remaining pages keep their original order, and nothing about them changes. If you later want to rearrange those pages, the [Organize PDF tool](/organize-pdf) lets you drag them into any order.

## How to remove multiple pages at once

You don't have to go one page at a time. The Delete Pages tool lets you tap as many pages as you want before confirming. This is useful when you have a scan with a blank back-of-page at the end of every section, or when you want to strip an entire chapter from a document.

Just tap each page you want gone. Tap again to deselect if you change your mind. Then tap **Delete pages** once to remove them all.

## The built-in Android trick (no tool at all)

If you'd rather not use any website, Chrome has a print-to-PDF feature that can get a page removed, though it takes a few more steps and is less reliable for complex documents.

1. Open the PDF in Chrome on your Android phone.
2. Tap the three-dot menu in the top-right corner, then tap **Print**.
3. At the top, tap the printer dropdown and choose **Save as PDF**.
4. Below that, tap **Pages** and enter the page range you want to keep, leaving out the ones you want to delete. For example, to remove page 3 from a 5-page document, you'd enter "1-2, 4-5".
5. Tap the PDF icon to save.

This works without any third-party tool, but it has a couple of limitations. First, [Chrome's print rendering](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/1069425) can reflow text and resize images, so the output may not look exactly like the original. Second, the range entry can be fiddly on a small screen. For anything important, the browser tool gives you a more accurate result.

## What happens to the rest of your PDF?

When you delete a page, the remaining pages are not re-exported or reprocessed. They stay byte-for-byte identical to what they were in the original. This matters because it means text remains selectable, embedded fonts stay intact, and any digital signatures on other pages are not invalidated.

The one thing that doesn't update automatically is any page number text baked into the content itself. If someone typed "Page 3 of 10" directly into the footer of each page, that text won't change after you remove page 2. The [PDF format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) stores page numbers in the document structure separately from any visible numbers rendered in the page content, so the two can get out of sync. If your document uses auto-generated headers and footers, you'd need to regenerate them in the original application.

## When to split instead of delete

Deleting removes pages permanently. If you want to pull a section out into its own file without changing the original, use the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) instead. You can extract a range of pages and save them as a separate document while leaving the original intact.

This is useful when someone sends you a 50-page report and you only need pages 12 to 18. Rather than deleting 44 pages from a copy, you extract the 7 you want in one step.

## Does this work on all Android phones?

Yes. The Delete Pages tool runs in any browser that supports modern JavaScript, which includes Chrome on Android 8 and later, Firefox, Samsung Internet, and Edge for Android. You don't need a specific phone model or a recent flagship. If your browser can load a website, it can run the tool.

Samsung phones also have a built-in option in the Samsung Notes app, but that requires opening the PDF through Samsung Notes specifically, and the steps vary by One UI version. The browser tool works the same way regardless of which Android phone you have.
