You can convert a PDF to Excel free online in a couple of minutes using tools like pdftoexcel.com or a Google Sheets import. The accuracy depends mostly on one thing: whether your PDF contains real, selectable text or is a scanned image.

## Text-based PDFs vs. scanned PDFs: why it matters

A native (text-based) PDF stores content as actual text and vector data. You can click and highlight individual words in any PDF viewer. These files convert to Excel quickly and accurately because the converter reads the characters directly and maps them to spreadsheet cells.

A scanned PDF is a photograph of a page. There's no real text in the file, just pixels. Converting it requires [OCR (optical character recognition)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_character_recognition), which reads the image, recognizes characters, and reconstructs the table structure. The result is usually less accurate, and complex layouts often don't survive the process intact.

To tell which type you have: open the file in your browser or any PDF viewer and try to click and drag to highlight a word. If text highlights, it's a native PDF. If the cursor treats the whole page as an image, it's a scan.

## How to convert a native PDF to Excel for free

For text-based PDFs, these free options work reliably without requiring an account:

**Free online converters.** [pdftoexcel.com](https://www.pdftoexcel.com/) handles multi-page documents with no sign-up. Upload the PDF, wait a few seconds, download an XLSX file. InvestInTech's converter is another solid choice for text-based files.

**Google Sheets import.** Open Google Sheets, go to File > Import, and upload the PDF. Sheets will extract any tables it recognizes. The formatting is rougher than a dedicated converter, but it's convenient if you're already working in Google Workspace and the table is straightforward.

**Copy and paste (for small tables).** Open the PDF in your browser, click and drag to select the table cells, copy, and paste into Excel or Sheets. Adjust column breaks by hand. For a table with a handful of rows, this is often the fastest option and produces clean output with zero conversion errors.

## How to convert a scanned PDF to Excel

Scanned PDFs need OCR. Most free converters now apply it automatically when they detect an image-based file, so you don't need to configure anything manually.

Free options that handle scanned PDFs without requiring an account:

- **pdftoexcel.com** detects scans and applies OCR before converting. No account needed.
- **OnlineOCR.net** lets you convert scanned PDFs to XLSX on a free tier, up to 15 pages per hour.

OCR accuracy depends on scan quality. A sharp, straight scan with high contrast produces clean output. A photo taken at an angle, a faded photocopy, or a document with a colored background will have more errors that need manual correction.

## Will the formatting survive the conversion?

Sometimes, but not always. How much cleanup you'll need depends on the table structure:

- Simple tables with one header row and consistent column widths usually convert cleanly into a usable spreadsheet.
- Tables with merged cells, rows that span page breaks, or complex multi-column layouts will almost always need manual fixes.
- Color coding, row shading, and borders rarely transfer correctly.

The practical approach: treat the converted file as a first draft. Let the tool extract the data, then spend a few minutes fixing column widths, merging cells where needed, and re-applying any formatting in Excel or Sheets.

## What to do when the output is wrong

If the converted spreadsheet looks wrong, here are the most useful next steps in order:

1. **Try a different converter.** Different tools use different extraction engines. pdftoexcel.com, Xodo, and InvestInTech each handle certain PDF layouts better than others. A file that fails in one often works in another.

2. **Check for restrictions.** Some PDFs are locked and block copying. If you can't highlight text in a viewer, the file may be protected. You can [remove those restrictions](/unlock-pdf) first, then try the conversion again.

3. **Extract only the pages you need.** If just a few pages contain the table, pull out those pages and convert only that smaller file. Our [Extract Pages tool](/extract-pages) lets you pick a specific page range and save it as a new PDF. Nothing is uploaded to a server, so your document stays private. A cleaner, focused input gives the converter a much better chance of producing accurate output.

4. **Re-type small tables by hand.** If the table has fewer than 20 rows and 5 columns, typing the data manually takes about five minutes and produces a perfect result with no OCR errors. This is often faster than troubleshooting a bad conversion.

One thing to keep in mind: PDF-to-Excel conversion is a lossy process for anything beyond simple tables. The tools extract data, not design. If you need the data to be pixel-perfect, the fastest path is often to extract the pages, convert them, and then spend five minutes in Excel fixing the output rather than hunting for a perfect conversion tool.
