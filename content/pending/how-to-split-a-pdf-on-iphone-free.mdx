You can split a PDF on iPhone for free without installing any app. There are two practical approaches: a workaround built into the iOS Files app, and a browser-based tool that runs in Safari with no sign-up needed. Both take under a minute for most documents.

## Method 1: Use the Files app (nothing to install)

iOS doesn't have a dedicated PDF splitter, but the system print dialog has a trick that does the job. It lets you select specific pages and save them as a new PDF file.

1. Open the **Files** app and tap your PDF to open it.
2. Tap the **Share** icon (the box with an upward arrow) at the bottom of the screen.
3. Scroll down the share sheet and tap **Print**.
4. In the print preview at the bottom, pinch outward on the thumbnail. This opens a full-page view of your document.
5. Long-press any page thumbnail, then tap **Select All** to grab everything, or tap individual page thumbnails to pick only the pages you want.
6. Tap the **Share** icon that appears and choose **Save to Files**.

iOS saves only the selected pages as a new PDF. Repeat this for each section you want to separate.

This method is free, requires no downloads, and works on any iPhone running [iOS 13 or later](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101662). The main limitation is that it's slow when you need many separate files. If you're splitting a 30-page document into 10 different files, doing it 10 times in the print dialog gets tedious fast.

## Method 2: Use a browser-based tool in Safari (faster, more flexible)

A browser-based splitter is the better choice for anything beyond a quick one-off split. Our [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) runs entirely in Safari on your iPhone, no app install or account required.

1. Open Safari and go to [pdf-tool.com/split-pdf](/split-pdf).
2. Tap **Choose file** and select your PDF from Files or iCloud Drive.
3. Choose your split option: by page range, after every N pages, or into individual pages.
4. Tap **Split** and save the result to Files.

Your PDF is processed in the browser and never sent to a server. That's the main advantage over apps or online tools that upload your document to their infrastructure: your file stays on your device the whole time.

This approach handles situations the Files app method doesn't, including setting specific page ranges (pages 1 to 8 as one file, pages 9 to 20 as another) and splitting into many files in one step.

## Splitting vs extracting pages: what's the difference?

Both terms describe roughly the same idea. "Split" usually means dividing a PDF at a point, producing two or more continuous sections. "Extract pages" means pulling out specific non-consecutive pages and saving them as a new PDF, leaving the rest intact.

For practical iPhone tasks the distinction rarely matters. Use whichever label the tool you're using shows. If you want pages 3, 7, and 11 from a 20-page document, look for "extract" or "extract pages." If you want the first half and the second half as separate files, "split" is the right term.

## How to split a PDF into individual pages on iPhone

If you need one file per page, the Files app method becomes impractical. For a 15-page document you'd have to repeat the process 15 times.

Instead, use the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) in Safari:

1. Upload your PDF.
2. Choose **Split into individual pages** (the option may be labeled "Extract every page").
3. Download the result, which comes as a zip archive with one PDF per page.

Once the zip downloads to your Files app, you can open it, tap individual PDFs inside, and share them one at a time by email, AirDrop, or WhatsApp.

## Should you split or compress?

Before splitting a PDF, it's worth checking whether splitting is actually what you need. A common reason people split is to make a file small enough to email. [Gmail and Outlook](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6584) both cap attachments at 25 MB. Because email encodes attachments in Base64, which adds roughly 33%, the practical ceiling is closer to 18 MB.

If your PDF is already under 18 MB, it'll attach to an email without any changes. Splitting it into pieces just creates more files for the recipient to deal with.

If the PDF is over that limit and it's a scan or image-heavy document, [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) may bring it under the limit in one step, which is cleaner than splitting. That said, if your PDF is mostly text, compression won't help and will likely make it larger. In that case, splitting is the right call.

## How to share a split PDF from iPhone

Once you've saved your split PDF to Files, sharing it is straightforward:

- **Email:** Open the Mail app, start a new message, tap the attachment icon (paperclip), and locate the file in Files.
- **iMessage or WhatsApp:** Tap the attachment or plus icon in a conversation, choose File, and select your PDF.
- **AirDrop:** Long-press the file in the Files app, tap Share, then choose AirDrop and pick a nearby device.
- **Google Drive or Dropbox:** Open the app, tap the upload button, and select your PDF.

If you split into individual pages and got a zip file, open the zip first in Files to see the individual PDFs inside. Tap any one to preview it, then use the Share icon to send it.

## Do I need an app to split PDFs on iPhone regularly?

Not necessarily. The Files app handles simple jobs fine, and the browser-based [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) covers more complex splits without any installation. If you find yourself splitting PDFs daily, a dedicated app might save a few taps. But for occasional use, the browser method works well and adds nothing to your home screen.
