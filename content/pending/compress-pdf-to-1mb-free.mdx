To compress a PDF to 1MB, open the free [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf), drop in your file, choose High or Medium compression, and download the result. This works well for scans and image-heavy PDFs. If your PDF is a typed text document, read the check below first. Compression will make it larger, not smaller.

## Why upload portals enforce a 1MB limit

Job application portals, government forms, visa submissions, and university systems all commonly cap attachments at 1MB. The limit comes down to infrastructure. Many of these platforms were built when storage was expensive and servers less capable, and the ceilings were never raised as technology improved. A system handling tens of thousands of daily submissions also processes faster when every file is small.

Common places you'll see this limit: job application portals, Indian government exam submissions, visa document uploads, bank KYC forms, and local government permit systems. One look at the error message "file must not exceed 1MB" and you're suddenly wondering how to halve a PDF in five minutes.

## Check first: scan or text document?

Before you compress anything, click your cursor on a line of text in the PDF.

**If the text highlights**, it's a typed document. Text PDFs are usually already very small (a 20-page typed report is typically around 20 to 30 KB). Compressing it with an in-browser tool won't help: the tool [rasterizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasterisation) every page into a JPEG image, and text stored as a photo is far larger than text stored as font data. In our own measurements, a 20-page text report at 23.5 KB came out at 1,400.8 KB after compression, roughly 60 times bigger.

**If nothing highlights**, it's a scan or an image-based PDF. Compression is exactly the right tool.

## How to compress an image PDF to under 1MB

1. Open the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf).
2. Drop your file in or click to browse.
3. Pick a compression level. Start with **High** if your file is several megabytes, or **Medium** if it's already close to the limit.
4. Click **Compress PDF**.
5. Download the result and check its size.

The tool runs in your browser. Your file never leaves your device, which matters when you're uploading something sensitive like a payslip, passport copy, or government ID.

Here are the actual results from our measurements on a 6-page image PDF:

| Level | Before | After | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| High (smallest) | 913.4 KB | 175.6 KB | 80.8% |
| Medium | 913.4 KB | 315.3 KB | 65.5% |
| Low (best quality) | 913.4 KB | 703.5 KB | 23.0% |

Based on those ratios, High compression can typically get an image-heavy PDF under 1MB as long as the file starts at roughly 5MB or less. Medium works for files under about 2.9MB. If your file is bigger than that, or if one pass isn't enough, jump to the next section.

## Do not compress the same file twice

A common suggestion is to run the file through compression a second time to squeeze out a bit more. We tested this and it doesn't work.

After one Medium pass, our 913.4 KB image PDF was 315.3 KB. Running it through again at the same level produced 598.9 KB, nearly double. At the next level up, it went to 297.8 KB, a saving of just 5.6%.

The reason is that the already-rasterized page gets re-rendered and re-encoded, which adds data rather than removing it. If one pass isn't enough, go back to the **original** file and compress once at a stronger level.

## What to do when compression alone isn't enough

### Extract only the pages you need

Most portals ask for one specific document: a certificate, a single bank statement page, or one page of a passport. If your PDF has ten pages and you only need two of them, use the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) to extract those pages first, then compress the result. Reducing a file from ten pages to two cuts the starting size dramatically before any compression happens.

### Scan at a lower resolution from the start

If you're creating the PDF by scanning a physical document, scan at 150 [DPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dots_per_inch) rather than 300 or 600. The file will be several times smaller before you open any tool. A clean, well-lit photo at 150 DPI is still readable; a blurry high-resolution scan is not. Scan conditions matter more than the DPI number.

### Check whether the portal actually enforces the limit

Some portals display a limit that was set years ago and is no longer strictly validated. If you're at 1.1MB after compression, try submitting anyway. The error message will tell you quickly if the limit is real, and you'll save time if it isn't.

## Quick tip for scanned IDs and passport copies

Passport copies and national ID cards have to stay legible, so compressing hard isn't always an option. The most effective approach is to start with a small source:

1. Photograph or scan in good, even light at 150 DPI.
2. Crop the image to the document itself, with no desk or background.
3. Run one pass at High.

Starting from a well-cropped, lower-resolution source almost always works better than aggressively compressing a high-resolution original. A clerk reading your passport needs the number and name to be clear; perfect detail on the national seal is not the goal.
