You can sign a PDF in Google Docs for free, but the approach depends on what you need. Google Docs converts your PDF to an editable document first, which can shift the layout. Google's own eSignature feature is only available on paid Workspace plans. This guide covers four options so you can pick the right one for your situation.

## The problem with signing a PDF "in Google Docs"

When you open a PDF in Google Docs, Google converts it into a Docs file. That conversion works well for simple documents but tends to break layouts with multiple columns, tables, headers, or precise positioning. If you are signing a lease, contract, or government form, the reflow can move content around in ways that make the signed version look wrong.

None of that is a reason to avoid Google Docs entirely. If the document is a simple text document and you are okay with minor formatting differences, the drawing method below is quick and free. But it is worth knowing the limitation up front.

## Method 1: Use the Google Docs drawing tool (free, any account)

This is the zero-cost option that works with every free Google account. The steps are simple.

1. Go to [Google Drive](https://drive.google.com) and upload your PDF if it is not already there.
2. Right-click the PDF and choose **Open with > Google Docs**. Wait for the conversion to finish.
3. Review the converted document. Complex tables and multi-column layouts often need manual cleanup at this stage.
4. Click where you want your signature. Then go to **Insert > Drawing > New**.
5. In the drawing toolbar, click the line tool dropdown and select **Scribble**.
6. Draw your signature with a mouse, trackpad, or stylus. Take your time; you can undo with Cmd+Z or Ctrl+Z.
7. Click **Save and Close** to place the signature image into the document.
8. Drag the signature image to position it exactly where you want it.
9. Go to **File > Download > PDF Document (.pdf)** to export the signed document.

The result is a PDF with your signature image embedded in the page. It is free and requires no extra software.

**What gets lost in the round trip:** Any interactive form fields in the original PDF are gone after conversion. If the PDF has fillable boxes, use the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) to complete those first, then handle the signature separately.

## Method 2: Google Workspace eSignature (paid plans only)

If you or your organization has a Google Workspace Business Standard plan or above, you can access Google's built-in eSignature from **Tools > eSignature** in Google Docs, or by opening a PDF in Drive and clicking the three-dot menu.

This tool sends a signature request, tracks who has signed, and generates an audit record. It is the right option for teams that need that kind of workflow.

If you see it greyed out or behind an upgrade prompt, your account is on a free plan. The [Google Workspace eSignature page](https://workspace.google.com/resources/esignature/) lists which plans include it.

## Method 3: A Google Drive add-on (free tier, keeps the layout)

Google Workspace Marketplace add-ons let you open and sign PDFs directly from Drive without converting them to Docs format first. That means the original layout stays intact.

DocHub is the most widely used free option. After installing it once from the [Google Workspace Marketplace](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/), you right-click any PDF in Drive and choose **Open with > DocHub**. You can draw or type a signature and place it anywhere on the page, then download or save back to Drive.

Free tiers on these add-ons typically cap you at a certain number of documents per month, so check the plan details before you start if you expect to sign a lot of documents.

## Method 4: Sign the PDF in your browser (no conversion, no account)

If the PDF is on your computer, you do not need Google involved at all. The [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) runs entirely in your browser. You open the file, draw your signature, position it on the page, and download the result. Nothing is uploaded to a server.

This is the best option when:
- The original layout must stay exactly as-is
- The PDF contains sensitive information (medical records, financial documents, immigration paperwork) that you prefer not to hand to any cloud service
- You do not have a Google account or do not want to log in

If there are interactive form fields to complete before signing, use [Fill PDF Form](/fill-form) first, then sign the downloaded result.

## Comparing the options

| Situation | Best method |
|---|---|
| Simple document, minor layout change is fine | Google Docs drawing method |
| Paid Google Workspace plan | Google eSignature |
| Stay in Drive, keep the original layout | Drive add-on |
| Sensitive document, no accounts, keep layout | Browser Sign PDF tool |

## Getting the signed PDF back into Google Drive

After signing outside of Google (in the browser tool or an add-on), upload the result to Drive with **New > File upload**. You can right-click the original and choose **Manage versions** to keep the signed version alongside the original, or just replace it.

## Are these signatures legally valid?

A drawn electronic signature is recognized as legally binding under the [ESIGN Act](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/electronic-signatures-global-national-commerce-act) in the United States and the [eIDAS Regulation](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eidas-regulation) in the European Union, as long as both parties intend to sign and there is some way to identify who signed.

For most everyday documents, a drawn signature is enough. If you are dealing with real estate transfers, large contracts, or anything with significant legal consequences, ask a lawyer whether a simple electronic signature meets the standard required for that specific document type.

The audit trail and certificate features offered by paid tools like DocuSign or Adobe Acrobat Sign are not legally required for most transactions. They exist to make disputes easier to resolve, which may or may not matter in your situation.
