## What actually makes a PDF merger free?

The best free PDF merger online is one that gives you the result without asking for anything in return: no account, no watermark, and no surprise after download. The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) fits that description. Drop your files in, arrange the order you want, click Merge, and download. That's it.

This sounds obvious, but it rules out a large chunk of tools that call themselves free. Smallpdf and Adobe Acrobat Online both offer free merging, but require an account or cap you at a small number of daily tasks. Others add their branding to the output. A genuinely free tool should hand you back a clean PDF you can send anywhere.

## Browser-based vs. cloud-based mergers

Most well-known PDF merger tools (iLovePDF, Sejda, PDF24, Smallpdf) upload your files to a server, process them there, then let you download the result. That works fine for general documents, and reputable services delete files after a short window.

Browser-based tools work differently. Processing happens in [JavaScript](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript) running in your browser tab. Your files go from disk to memory, get combined, and come back as a download, all without leaving your device. If you're merging contracts, tax returns, medical records, or anything else with personal information, that distinction matters.

The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) is browser-based. If you want more detail on which PDF tools work this way, the post on [PDF tools that don't upload your files](/blog/pdf-tools-that-dont-upload-your-files) covers the full picture.

## Does merging PDFs make the file bigger?

A common worry before merging is that the combined file will be too large to attach to an email or upload to a portal. The answer, based on actual testing, is no.

When three test PDFs totalling 971.9 KB were merged with this tool, the result came out at 970.5 KB, which is 0.1% smaller than the sum of the parts. The small reduction comes from shared [PDF structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) being written once in the output instead of once per file.

So if your files total 15 MB and your email client caps attachments at 25 MB, merging will not push you over. If the combined file is still too large after merging, the problem is usually large images inside one of the source files. Compressing an image-heavy PDF with the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) is the right next step, though keep in mind that compression only helps files with images or scanned pages, not text-based documents.

## What happens to form fields when you merge PDFs?

This is the one thing most guides skip, and it matters.

When you merge a PDF that contains interactive form fields with another document, the merged output loses those fields. The [AcroForm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF#Forms) layer is not carried over during the copy-pages operation. What you get is a file with the right page count but no interactive fields.

Whether the values you typed into a form are still visible after merging is not guaranteed. To be safe: flatten the form first. Flattening converts the typed values into static text painted onto the page, so they can't be lost in a later operation. The [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) does this in seconds before you merge.

If you regularly put together job applications, contracts, or any package that combines a filled form with supporting documents, make flattening a habit before merging. It takes five extra seconds and prevents a headache.

## How many files can you merge at once?

The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) accepts multiple files in a single session. There is no fixed limit on how many PDFs you can add. Processing happens on your device, so very large batches (hundreds of megabytes total) will take a bit longer than small ones, but there's no server quota to exhaust.

## What to watch out for with cloud-based PDF mergers

If you do use a cloud-based merger, a few things are worth checking before you upload.

**File deletion policy.** Reputable services delete uploads within 1 to 24 hours. Read the privacy page before uploading sensitive files.

**Daily limits.** Many free tiers allow only 2 to 5 merges per day or per hour. If you need to run several merges, you'll hit the cap quickly.

**Watermarks.** Some free tiers stamp the output with a logo. Download a sample before processing important files.

**Account requirement.** Signing up gives the service an email address and potentially access to your files' metadata. For occasional merges, a browser-based tool avoids this entirely.

For most people doing an occasional merge, any reputable tool works. For sensitive files or frequent use, a browser-based tool with no account is the cleaner option.
