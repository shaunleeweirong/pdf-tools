Removing a watermark from a PDF for free is possible, but the method depends on one thing: how the watermark was added. Text watermarks like "DRAFT" or "CONFIDENTIAL" can often be selected and deleted directly. Image and burned-in watermarks need a different approach, and being clear on which type you have saves a lot of frustration.

## What Kind of Watermark Do You Have?

PDF watermarks come in three forms, and each needs a different removal method.

**Text overlay watermarks** are the most common type in business documents. These are text strings like "CONFIDENTIAL", "SAMPLE", or "FOR REVIEW ONLY" stored as actual text elements in the PDF. Because they're discrete objects, you can often click on them with an editor and delete them, just like clicking on a text box.

**Image watermarks** are graphic objects (logos, stamps, or semi-transparent images) embedded in the page as image data. They're not selectable as text. Removing them cleanly requires a tool that can identify the watermark area and reconstruct what's underneath.

**Burned-in watermarks** appear in scanned documents where the watermark was physically on the paper when it went through the scanner. The watermark pixels are mixed into every pixel of the page image, and no editing tool can perfectly separate them. AI-based inpainting can sometimes reconstruct the background, but results are mixed when the watermark sits over dense text or tables.

Knowing your type before you start will save you from trying the wrong tool three times in a row.

## How to Remove a Text Watermark from a PDF Free

For text overlay watermarks, the [Edit PDF](/edit-pdf) tool handles this in your browser without uploading your file anywhere. Your document never leaves your device.

**Steps:**

1. Go to [Edit PDF](/edit-pdf) and open your PDF.
2. Click on the watermark text. If it's a text element, it will highlight as a selectable object.
3. Press Delete or Backspace to remove it.
4. Repeat for any other watermark instances across the document.
5. Download the cleaned PDF.

This works for watermarks added programmatically, such as those exported from Word's watermark feature, generated by PDF creation tools, or added by document management systems when they mark a file as "DRAFT".

It won't work if the watermark is an image, a background tile, or part of a scanned page. If clicking on it doesn't select anything, move to the next method.

## How to Remove an Image Watermark from a PDF Free

Image watermarks need an AI-powered tool that can detect and fill the watermarked area. Several free online options handle this:

- **WatermarkRemover.io** uses AI to detect and fill watermarked regions. Upload the PDF, let it process, and download. Works well for translucent logos over solid or simple backgrounds.
- **PicWish** follows a similar flow: upload, auto-remove, download. Free for basic use.
- **Aspose Watermark Remover** removes stamp-style and overlay watermarks, and requires no sign-up.

These tools process your file on their servers. If the document contains sensitive information, weigh that before uploading. For internal business use, an in-browser approach is safer when available.

## Removing a Burned-In Watermark (Scanned Documents)

Watermarks in scanned PDFs are the hardest case. The mark was on the paper, so it's part of every pixel in the scan. No single click removes it.

The most reliable free approach uses image editing:

1. Convert the PDF to high-resolution images using the [PDF to JPG](/pdf-to-jpg) tool, one image per page.
2. Open each image in [GIMP](https://www.gimp.org/) (a free, open-source image editor) and use the Heal Selection or Clone Stamp tool to paint over the watermark area.
3. Convert the cleaned images back to a PDF with [Images to PDF](/jpg-to-pdf).

This takes more effort and works best when the watermark sits over a plain background. If the watermark overlaps body text, removing it cleanly is very difficult, and the result may look patchy.

AI-based inpainting tools are getting better at this, but for critical documents, getting an unwatermarked copy from the source is faster and more reliable.

## Removing a Watermark Without Uploading Your File

For privacy-sensitive documents, staying in-browser matters. The [Edit PDF](/edit-pdf) tool processes everything locally, so the PDF never touches a server. This is the right path for confidential contracts, medical documents, or anything you'd rather not hand to a third-party service.

For image or burned-in watermarks, the free AI tools listed above do require an upload. There currently aren't many free client-side options for image watermark removal.

## Using Word or Google Docs to Remove Watermarks

This workaround sometimes works for text watermarks added via Word's built-in watermark feature, but it has a catch: converting a PDF back to DOCX reformats the document. Tables shift, fonts change, and columns may break. If the original layout matters, skip this.

If layout isn't critical:

1. Open the PDF in Microsoft Word (File > Open). Word warns you about conversion quality.
2. Go to Design, then Watermark, then Remove Watermark.
3. Save back as PDF.

Google Docs works similarly: convert the PDF to DOCX first, open in Docs, then manually delete the watermark element.

## A Note on Legality

Only remove watermarks from documents you own or are authorized to edit. The [U.S. Copyright Act, Section 1202](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap12.html) prohibits removing copyright management information from protected works without authorization. Similar rules apply under the [EU Copyright Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790).

For internal documents your organization created, removing a "DRAFT" or routing stamp is clearly fine. For files you received from someone else, check before editing. If there's any doubt, ask the sender for an unwatermarked copy rather than removing it yourself.
