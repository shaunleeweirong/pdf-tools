You can extract pages from a PDF free, without Acrobat, without uploading your file anywhere. The [Extract Pages tool](/extract-pages) runs in your browser and hands you back a new PDF with just the pages you chose.

## When you'd need to extract pages

A few common situations:

- You received a 40-page report but only need pages 12 to 15 to share with a colleague.
- You want to save one chapter from a long document as its own file.
- You're preparing a submission that asks for specific pages from a larger document.
- You want to send part of a PDF by email but the whole thing is too large to attach.

Extracting is the right move whenever you need a subset of an existing PDF as a standalone document.

## How to extract pages from a PDF free (step by step)

1. Open the [Extract Pages tool](/extract-pages).
2. Click the upload area (or drag your PDF directly onto it).
3. Enter the page numbers you want. You can type a single page ("5"), a range ("3-9"), or individual pages separated by commas ("2, 5, 8").
4. Click Extract (or the equivalent button).
5. Download your new PDF.

That's it. The original file isn't changed. You get a new document containing only the pages you selected.

Your file is processed entirely in your browser using [pdf-lib](https://pdf-lib.js.org/), a JavaScript library that reads and writes PDFs without sending anything to a remote server. The file never leaves your device.

## Extract vs. split: which one do you want?

People often use "extract" and "split" interchangeably, but they do different things.

**Extracting** picks specific pages and packages them into one new PDF. You choose page 1, page 7, and pages 12 to 14 and you get back a single file containing those five pages.

**Splitting** breaks a PDF into multiple separate output files. A common use is splitting every page into its own file, or dividing by page ranges (pages 1 to 10 go to file A, pages 11 to 20 go to file B).

If you want one file with chosen pages, use [Extract Pages](/extract-pages). If you want multiple output files, use [Split PDF](/split-pdf).

## Extracting a single page

Want just page 3 from a 30-page report? Enter "3" in the page field and click extract. You'll download a one-page PDF. The original 30-page file is untouched.

This is also the fastest way to yank a signed signature page, a specific invoice page, or an exhibit from a longer document.

## Extracting a range or non-consecutive pages

You're not limited to one page. You can:

- Enter a range: "5-10" gives you pages 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10 in one file.
- Pick individual pages: "1, 4, 9" pulls those three pages into a single PDF.
- Combine both: "1-3, 7, 11-13" will give you pages 1 through 3, page 7, and pages 11 through 13.

The pages come out in the order they appeared in the original document, not the order you typed them.

## Does extracting change quality or file size?

No quality loss. The tool copies page content streams directly from the original [PDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) structure without re-encoding, re-rendering, or compressing anything. Fonts stay as fonts, images stay at their original resolution, and vector graphics stay as vectors.

File size will be roughly proportional to the pages you pick. If you pull 5 pages out of a 20-page document, expect the result to be around one-quarter of the original, give or take shared resources like embedded fonts that get carried along regardless.

One thing to be aware of: if the original PDF has interactive form fields on the pages you extract, those fields may not carry over correctly into the new document. If you have a filled-out form, [flatten it first](/flatten-pdf) to lock the values into the page before extracting.

## Privacy: does the file get uploaded?

With this tool, no. Many PDF tools advertise "free" but process files on their own servers. That's a problem if the document contains personal data, contracts, or anything confidential.

The [Extract Pages tool](/extract-pages) uses client-side JavaScript. Your browser reads the PDF, does the page-selection, and produces the output file. Nothing is transmitted. If you turn off your Wi-Fi after the page loads and run the tool, it still works.

## What if I want to remove pages instead of keep them?

Extracting keeps the pages you name. If you'd rather delete a few pages and keep the rest, the [Delete Pages tool](/delete-pages) is the faster route. Choose the pages to remove, and you get back the original document with those pages gone.

## Putting extracted pages into another document

Once you have your extracted PDF, you can combine it with other files using the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf). For example: extract the cover page from document A, extract a table from document B, then merge them into one file.

Merging doesn't inflate the result beyond what the parts already add up to, so this workflow won't push you over email size limits.

## Doing this without any tool

If you'd rather not use an online tool, two built-in options work reasonably well.

**macOS Preview:** Open the PDF, go to View, then Thumbnails. Command-click the pages you want. Drag the selected thumbnails to the desktop or to another Finder window. Preview creates a new PDF from those pages. No third-party software needed.

**Chrome's print dialog:** Open the PDF in Chrome, press Cmd+P (Mac) or Ctrl+P (Windows) to print, switch the destination to "Save as PDF," and type specific page numbers in the Pages field. This works, but you lose bookmarks and interactive elements, and the output quality depends on your print settings.

For anything beyond a quick one-off, the dedicated [Extract Pages tool](/extract-pages) is faster and gives you more control over which pages end up in the output.
