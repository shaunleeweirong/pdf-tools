This guide breaks down each option and shows you how to highlight, comment, and draw on a PDF in minutes.

## What annotating a PDF actually means

Annotating a PDF means adding highlights, comments, sticky notes, or drawings on top of the existing content. The underlying text stays untouched. [PDF annotations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF#Annotations) are a formal part of the file format, which is why most viewers can display them even when they were added by a different tool.

This is different from editing. If you highlight a paragraph or leave a "check this figure" note, that's annotation. If you change the words themselves, that's editing. This guide covers annotation: highlighting, comments, notes, drawing, and shapes.

## How to highlight text in a PDF for free

Highlighting is the most common annotation task. Here's how it works in any browser-based tool:

1. Open the annotator in your browser (no install needed).
2. Upload your PDF by clicking the file picker or dragging it onto the page.
3. Select the highlight tool from the toolbar (usually a marker or "H" icon).
4. Click and drag over the text you want to mark.
5. Choose a color if the tool offers more than one option.
6. Repeat for other passages.
7. Click Download or Export to save the annotated file.

You can usually click an existing highlight to change its color or delete it before you download.

## How to add comments and sticky notes to a PDF

Sticky notes let you attach a message to a specific point in the document without blocking the text underneath. The steps are nearly identical to highlighting:

1. Select the sticky note or comment tool (often a speech bubble icon).
2. Click the spot in the PDF where the note should appear.
3. Type your comment in the box.
4. Click outside the box to collapse it. A small icon marks the note's position.
5. Download the file when you're done.

Recipients can open the annotated PDF in any standard viewer, including [Adobe Acrobat Reader](https://get.adobe.com/reader/), Chrome's built-in PDF viewer, or Preview on Mac, and read your comments without needing the same tool you used.

## How to draw or sketch on a PDF for free

Drawing tools are useful for circling sections, marking up diagrams, or leaving visual feedback. Most browser-based annotators include a freehand pen and basic shapes.

1. Select the pen or draw tool in the toolbar.
2. Set the line weight and color if available.
3. Draw directly on the PDF page.
4. Use undo or an eraser to fix mistakes.
5. Download when finished.

## Is it safe to annotate PDFs online?

For study notes, reading materials, or anything non-sensitive, any of the tools above are fine. For anything confidential, you want a tool that processes files locally.

Browser-based annotators that work without a server upload load your PDF into memory in your browser tab. The annotation happens there, and the download goes straight to your device. Nothing passes through an external server.

If that matters for your use case, PDFgear, Annotation.com, and PDFFreeEditor all describe local processing on their pages. Read those descriptions before uploading anything sensitive.

## Do annotated PDFs work in all viewers?

Standard annotations (highlights, sticky notes, shapes) follow the [PDF/A and ISO 32000 specifications](https://pdfa.org/resource/pdf-specification-index/), so they display correctly in Adobe Acrobat Reader, Chrome, Firefox, Preview on Mac, and most other viewers. Freehand drawings are embedded as vector or raster shapes and are similarly compatible.

One practical note: if someone needs to *edit* your annotations rather than just read them, they'll need a PDF editor, not just a viewer.
