## How PDF to Excel conversion works

There are two kinds of PDFs, and the type you have determines how well conversion goes.

A **native PDF** was created by software (exported from Excel, generated by accounting software, or saved from a Word document). Table data is encoded as text coordinates. A converter reads it directly and places it into the right cells.

A **scanned PDF** is a photograph of a document. There is no underlying text, just pixels. A converter needs OCR to recognize the characters before building a spreadsheet. Accuracy depends entirely on scan quality.

Native PDFs convert fast and accurately. Scanned PDFs are hit or miss based on resolution and how cleanly the original was printed and scanned.

One thing to skip before converting: do not compress your PDF first. The in-browser compression tool rasterizes pages, turning text into images. A 23.5 KB text document came out at 1.4 MB after compression. Running that through an Excel converter would mean OCR on every page instead of a clean text extraction.

## What to look for in a free converter

**No sign-up.** A converter that asks for your email before releasing the file is collecting data as a trade. The tools above skip that.

**File deletion.** Any converter handling financial or personal data should state explicitly that it deletes files after conversion. Check before uploading sensitive documents.

**OCR support.** If you have scanned PDFs, you need OCR. Not every free tool includes it.

**File size limits.** Most free tools cap uploads at around 10 to 50 MB. If your PDF is larger, use the [Split PDF](/split-pdf) tool to break it into smaller chunks before converting. Or use [Extract Pages](/extract-pages) to pull out only the pages that contain the tables you need.

## How accurate is free conversion?

For native PDFs with clear table borders, free converters produce good results. Columns land where they should, numbers are correct, and formatting is close. For scanned PDFs, accuracy varies.

The most common problems:

- **Merged columns.** Columns with no visible separator get lumped into one cell.
- **Headers treated as data.** The converter adds the header row as a regular data row instead of a column label.
- **Decimal separators reversed.** European formats (1.000,00 vs. 1,000.00) sometimes get flipped.

If the output needs work, fixing it in Excel is usually faster than trying a different tool. Most conversions need only a few minutes of reformatting.

## When free conversion will not help

Not every PDF is a candidate. Documents where data is spread across the page in a narrative layout (not a structured table) do not convert into usable spreadsheets. The converter sees columns and tries to fit everything into rows, and the result is usually a jumble.

In those cases, copy-pasting from a PDF reader into Excel gives you more control over what lands where.
