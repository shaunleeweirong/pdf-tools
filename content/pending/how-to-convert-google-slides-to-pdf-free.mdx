Converting Google Slides to PDF free takes about three clicks. Open your presentation, go to **File > Download > PDF Document (.pdf)**, and the file lands in your Downloads folder. That covers most cases, but if you need speaker notes, a compact handout layout, or you're working from your phone, there's a bit more to it.

## The fastest way: File > Download

This is the standard path and it works for any presentation:

1. Open the presentation in [Google Slides](https://workspace.google.com/products/slides/).
2. Click **File** in the top menu bar.
3. Hover over **Download**.
4. Click **PDF Document (.pdf)**.

Google converts the presentation and triggers a download. You get one slide per page, no speaker notes, standard slide dimensions. For most sharing and printing purposes, this is exactly what you need.

If your presentation is stored in Google Drive and you do not want to open it, you can right-click the file in Drive and select **Download**. Google Drive converts .gslides files to PDF on the fly and sends you the download.

## How to save Google Slides as PDF with speaker notes

The standard download skips your speaker notes. To include them, you need to go through Print Settings:

1. Click **File > Print Settings and Preview**.
2. In the toolbar that appears, click the layout dropdown (it usually shows "1 slide" by default).
3. Choose **1 slide with notes**.
4. Click the **Download as PDF** button in the top-left corner.

Each page of the PDF shows the slide at the top and the notes below it. This is useful for presenter handouts, study guides, or sharing your talking points with a co-presenter. The notes layout always uses one slide per page, so you cannot combine it with a compact handout view.

## How to export as a handout (multiple slides per page)

For audience handouts where you want several slides on a single page:

1. Click **File > Print Settings and Preview**.
2. Click the layout dropdown.
3. Pick a handout format: **2**, **3**, **4**, **6**, or **9 slides per page**.
4. Click **Download as PDF**.

The 3-slides-per-page option adds a blank column on the right where people can write notes by hand. The 6-per-page layout is more compact and saves paper if you're printing physical copies.

One trade-off: Google Slides does not support handout layouts with speaker notes in the same PDF. If you need both, you'd have to export two separate files.

## How to convert Google Slides to PDF on iPhone or Android

The Google Slides mobile app supports PDF export on both platforms:

1. Open the presentation in the Google Slides app.
2. Tap the **three-dot menu** (top right).
3. Tap **Share & export**, then **Save As**.
4. Choose **PDF Document (.pdf)** and tap **OK**.

On iOS, the file saves to the Files app. On Android, it goes to your device's Downloads folder or Google Drive, depending on your settings.

The mobile app does not have Print Settings and Preview, so you cannot choose handout layouts or include speaker notes from your phone. For those options, open the presentation in a desktop browser and use the File menu there.

## Why convert to PDF instead of sharing the .gslides link?

A [PDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) is a fixed-layout format, meaning fonts, colors, and positions stay exactly as you set them regardless of the device or software the recipient uses. A Google Slides link requires the recipient to have a Google account (or you to set sharing permissions), and anyone with editing access could change the content. A PDF is static, shareable without account friction, and printable with predictable results.

## What to do if your Google Slides PDF is too large to email

Gmail and Outlook.com both cap attachments at 25 MB. Because email clients encode attachments in Base64, which adds roughly 33% overhead, the practical limit for a PDF attachment is around 18 MB. Most presentations export well under that, but a deck full of high-resolution images can push past it.

If your presentation is image-heavy (lots of photos, screenshots, or graphics on each slide), the [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) tool can reduce the file size directly in your browser. In our tests on an image-heavy PDF, the tool reduced a 913.4 KB file to 315.3 KB at Medium quality, a 65.5% reduction. The tool works by rasterizing each page to JPEG, which shrinks image content but makes text-only documents significantly larger. Only use it when images are the reason for the file size.

If you have multiple presentations that you exported as separate PDFs and want to join them into one file, the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool combines them without inflating the output. You can drag in the files, reorder them, and download a single combined PDF.

## Combining multiple Google Slides presentations into one PDF

The workflow goes like this:

1. Export each presentation using **File > Download > PDF Document (.pdf)**.
2. Go to the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool.
3. Upload the exported PDFs and arrange them in the order you want.
4. Download the combined PDF.

Merging does not add to the file size beyond the sum of the parts. If the combined file is still too large to send, use [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) afterward, but again, only if the deck is image-heavy.
