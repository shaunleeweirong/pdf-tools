You can convert a PDF to JPG on iPhone right now by opening the [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) in Safari. No app to install, no account to create. Tap to select your PDF, wait for the conversion, and download each page as a JPG image.

## The fastest way: use a browser-based converter

The simplest approach on iPhone is a browser tool. Open Safari or Chrome, visit a PDF to JPG converter, and the whole thing happens through your browser. You pick the file, the tool processes the pages, and you download the results. Nothing stays on your phone after you close the tab.

Our [PDF to JPG converter](/pdf-to-jpg) is free and doesn't ask you to register. It converts each page of your PDF to a separate JPG, so you can share, upload, or print individual pages without dealing with the full document.

## Step-by-step: convert a PDF to JPG in Safari

1. Open **Safari** (or Chrome) on your iPhone.
2. Go to the [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg).
3. Tap **Select PDF** and choose your file. The iOS file picker opens so you can browse iCloud Drive, Downloads, or any other location the Files app can reach.
4. The tool converts each page to a JPG. A short wait is normal for longer documents.
5. Tap the download button next to each page image to save it, or long-press a preview image to save it directly to Photos.

Your downloaded files land in the **Downloads** folder inside the Files app. From there you can share them, move them, or open them in another app.

## What happens when my PDF has multiple pages?

Every page becomes its own JPG. A 10-page PDF produces 10 separate image files, numbered by page order.

This is worth knowing before you start. If you only need one or two pages from a long document, it's faster to extract those pages first using a [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf), then convert just the excerpt. You end up with fewer files to sort through and a quicker conversion.

## How to save the JPGs to your Camera Roll

Safari gives you two routes:

**Long-press the image** shown in the browser after conversion, then tap **Add to Photos**. The image goes straight to your Camera Roll.

**Download first, then save.** Tap the download button to send the file to your Downloads folder in Files. Open it there, tap the share icon (the box with an arrow), and choose **Save Image**. This also puts it in Photos.

Both work fine. The long-press shortcut is quicker when you only need one page. The download-first method is handy if you want the file in Files as well as Photos.

## Why not just take a screenshot?

Screenshots work in a pinch but have real limits.

A screenshot only captures what's visible on screen at that moment. For a long PDF page you'd need to scroll, screenshot each section, crop each image, and stitch or pick the right piece. The resolution is also tied to your screen's pixel density, which may not be sharp enough for printing or uploading to a site that expects a high-quality image.

Converting with a tool gives you the full page as a complete image, at a consistent resolution, with no manual cropping. It's worth the extra few seconds when image quality actually matters.

## Picking a PDF from email, iCloud Drive, or Google Drive

When you tap **Select PDF** in the converter, iOS shows the standard [Files app](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/find-files-and-folders-in-files-on-iphone-iph432d1430d/ios) picker. From there you can reach:

- **iCloud Drive** and any iCloud folder
- **Google Drive** or **Dropbox** if those apps are installed
- Your **Downloads** folder
- Any folder you've saved locally in Files

If your PDF arrived as an email attachment, open the email, tap the attachment, tap the share icon, and choose **Save to Files**. Once it's in Files, the converter can find it.

## What about Adobe Acrobat on iPhone?

Adobe Acrobat Reader is free on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/adobe-acrobat-reader-pdf-viewer/id469337564), but exporting to JPG is locked behind an [Acrobat Pro subscription](https://www.adobe.com/acrobat/acrobat-pro.html). The free app lets you view and annotate PDFs. To actually convert pages to images, you pay a monthly fee.

A browser tool gives you the same result for nothing. If you're converting one PDF or doing it occasionally, there's no reason to pay for a subscription.

## What if the PDF is very large?

Typical PDFs, a few pages up to a dozen or so, convert quickly. A very large file, say a 50-page illustrated report, will take noticeably longer because each page has to be processed separately.

If speed matters and you only need part of the document, split it first. Pull out the specific pages you want with the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf), then convert just that smaller file. You'll get the same images in less time and with fewer files to manage.

One other thing: very large PDFs (hundreds of megabytes) can be slow to upload on a mobile connection. If your Wi-Fi is fast, that's fine. On a slower cellular connection, it's worth waiting until you're on Wi-Fi before converting a big file.
