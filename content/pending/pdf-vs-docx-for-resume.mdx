The PDF vs. DOCX resume debate comes up for nearly every job application, and the answer is simpler than the discussion suggests: check the job posting first, then default to PDF when the posting says nothing.

## Check the job posting before choosing a format

The strongest rule is also the easiest: do what the employer asks. If the listing says "attach a Word document" or "upload PDF only," follow that instruction exactly. Career portals sometimes validate file types on upload and reject the wrong format before a recruiter ever sees your application.

Check the job ad, any email from a recruiter, and the upload screen on the portal itself. When all three are silent, use the defaults below.

## When to send a PDF resume

PDF is the right default for most situations where no format is specified:

- **Emailing a recruiter directly.** A PDF looks the same whether the recipient opens it in Adobe Acrobat, Apple Preview, a browser, or on their phone. Word documents can shift margins, change fonts, or reflow bullet points depending on the Office version the recipient has installed.
- **Uploading to portals that accept both.** When the portal accepts PDF and DOCX, PDF protects your formatting.
- **Sharing a link.** If you're sharing your resume through a portfolio site, LinkedIn profile, or a QR code on a business card, PDF is universally readable without any extra software.
- **Following up by email after an interview.** A PDF looks intentional and polished.

[PDF is defined by ISO 32000](https://www.iso.org/standard/63534.html), which means a properly-formed PDF renders the same way across devices, operating systems, and years. A Word document has no such cross-platform guarantee.

## When to send a Word (.docx) resume

There are real cases where DOCX is the correct choice:

- **The recruiter or job posting asks for it.** Some staffing agencies edit a candidate's resume before passing it to a client, or they add their own header. Send DOCX when asked.
- **The company provides a template.** If they send you a DOCX template to fill in, return the filled-in DOCX.
- **Older applicant tracking systems.** A small number of legacy ATS platforms parse Word files more cleanly than PDFs. This matters less with modern systems, but when you're applying at a company that might be running older HR software, DOCX slightly reduces the risk.

The practical setup: keep a DOCX source file you can edit quickly, and export a fresh PDF each time you apply when no format is specified.

## Can ATS systems actually read PDF resumes?

Yes, most can. The belief that [applicant tracking systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applicant_tracking_system) cannot read PDFs was accurate for early ATS software and is now largely outdated. Major platforms, including Workday, Greenhouse, and Lever, parse standard text-based PDFs reliably.

What causes parsing failures is not the file type but the layout:

- Multi-column designs where text flows across two or three columns
- Tables used to structure sections (many ATS read across rows rather than down columns)
- Text inside headers, footers, or floating text boxes
- Decorative graphics that contain text the system cannot extract

A single-column, text-based PDF exported from Word or Google Docs will clear nearly any modern ATS. The key phrase is "text-based." A scanned image saved as PDF looks like a PDF but contains no machine-readable text at all. To check: open your resume in a PDF viewer and try to select a word with your cursor. If text selection works, the file is text-based. If it won't select, the file is a scanned image.

## How to convert your resume to PDF for free

1. **Microsoft Word:** File, then Save As, then choose PDF from the format menu. On older versions: File, then Export, then Create PDF/XPS.
2. **Google Docs:** File, then Download, then PDF Document (.pdf).
3. **Apple Pages:** File, then Export To, then PDF.
4. **Mac (any app):** File, then Print, then click the PDF button in the lower-left corner of the print dialog.

No third-party converter or account is needed. The resulting file is text-based and ATS-readable.

## What to do if your resume PDF is too large

A clean, text-only resume should come in under 500 KB and often under 200 KB. If yours is noticeably larger, the common causes are:

- A headshot or photo embedded in the file
- High-resolution logos from a design template
- Decorative backgrounds or graphics in a template
- A scanned paper resume saved as a PDF image rather than exported as text

[Gmail and Outlook.com both cap email attachments at 25 MB](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6584), so a resume is very unlikely to hit that limit when sent by email. Job portal upload limits are sometimes tighter, commonly 5 MB or 2 MB.

If you need to reduce the size of a resume that contains images or a scanned page, a [PDF compressor](/compress-pdf) will help. Do not compress a pure-text resume: the compressor rasterizes every page to JPEG, which turns a small text file into a much larger image file. A better fix for a heavy text resume is to simplify the template at the source and re-export.

## Combining your resume with a cover letter

If you want to send a resume and cover letter as a single tidy attachment rather than two files, you can [merge both PDFs into one document](/merge-pdf) before attaching. The recruiter gets a single file in a logical order, and you have one attachment to track.

## Keep both formats ready

The cleanest approach:

1. Save a **DOCX source file** you can tailor for each role.
2. Export a fresh **PDF** from that DOCX each time you apply when no format is specified.

Export fresh each time rather than compressing an old version. A source-to-PDF export keeps the file small, the text searchable, and the formatting clean.
