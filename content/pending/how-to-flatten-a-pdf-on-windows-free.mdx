Flattening a PDF on Windows is free and takes about ten seconds once you know the right tool. You don't need Adobe Acrobat. The job gets done with a browser-based tool or the Print to PDF feature that Windows already has built in.

## What does "flatten" mean for a PDF?

A PDF stores form fields, checkboxes, signatures, and annotations as separate interactive layers sitting on top of the page content. When you flatten the file, those layers get permanently merged into the page itself. The result looks identical to what you had before, but nothing is editable, movable, or removable anymore.

Think of it like laminating a filled-out form. The data is still there and visible. It just can't be changed.

Flattening is different from compressing. Compression shrinks the file size. Flattening locks down interactivity. The two are unrelated, and you can do one without the other.

## When do you actually need to flatten?

A few situations come up over and over.

**Submitting to a court, government portal, or HR system.** Most court e-filing systems require flat PDFs. They reject files with live form fields because the data could theoretically be modified after submission. The [U.S. Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals instructs filers to flatten before submitting](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/docs/default-source/cm-ecf/how-to-flatten-pdf-forms), and similar guidance appears on federal and state court sites across the country.

**Merging a filled-out form with other documents.** Most merge tools, including the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf), strip interactive form fields when combining documents. Merge a filled form without flattening first and the values disappear. Flatten first, then merge. (More on this below.)

**Sharing a filled form by email.** If someone opens your filled PDF in a different viewer, the fields might appear blank or render incorrectly depending on the app. A flattened file looks the same in every app, on every device.

**Printing.** Some printers and print drivers don't render interactive fields consistently. A flattened PDF prints exactly as it looks on screen.

## How to flatten a PDF on Windows for free

The simplest way is the free [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf). It runs entirely in your browser, nothing to install, no account needed.

1. Open the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf).
2. Click "Select PDF" or drag your file onto the drop zone.
3. The tool processes the file in your browser. Your PDF is not sent to any server.
4. Click the download button to save the flattened file.

That's the whole process. Form fields, annotations, and signatures are now part of the page content.

## The built-in Windows method: Print to PDF

Windows 10 and 11 include a virtual printer called Microsoft Print to PDF. You can use it to flatten any PDF without installing anything extra.

1. Open your PDF in any browser (Chrome, Edge, or Firefox all work).
2. Press **Ctrl+P** to open the print dialog.
3. In the Printer or Destination dropdown, choose **Microsoft Print to PDF**.
4. Click **Print** (or **Save** in Chrome).
5. Choose a location and save the file.

The saved file is a fresh PDF with all interactive layers removed. [Adobe's own guide on flattening](https://www.adobe.com/acrobat/hub/how-to-flatten-a-pdf.html) mentions this as a quick free workaround for Windows users.

One thing to watch: some print dialogs scale the page slightly to fit the print area. If exact page dimensions matter (for a court filing, for example), set scaling to "Actual size" or "100%" before saving.

## Flatten before you merge

This is the step that trips people up most often.

The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf), like most merge tools, copies pages from each document rather than preserving the full document structure. Interactive form fields don't survive that process. Our own measurements confirm it: a form that was merged without flattening first produced an output with zero form fields, even though the page count was correct. The values were simply gone.

So if you have a job application, a contract, or any filled form you want to combine with other documents, do it in this order:

1. Open each filled form in the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) and save the flattened version.
2. Merge the flattened files with the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf).

The field values are safe because they're baked into the page content before the merge happens.

## Do you need Acrobat to flatten on Windows?

No. Adobe Acrobat Pro can flatten PDFs, but it costs money and it's not necessary for this job. The browser-based tool above handles the same task for free. The Print to PDF method works with any app that can print.

[ISO 32000](https://www.iso.org/standard/75839.html), the international standard that defines the PDF format, specifies how interactive form elements and annotations work. When a tool flattens a PDF, it follows that spec to convert those elements into static page content. The output should look identical regardless of which tool does the flattening.
