## How to split a PDF by page number

You can split a PDF by page number for free using a browser-based tool that runs on your device. Open the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf), upload your file, enter the page ranges for each output, and download the separate PDFs. No account, no software to install, and the file never leaves your browser.

This works on Windows, Mac, Chromebook, iPhone, and Android.

## What "split by page number" actually means

When you split a PDF by page number, you're telling the tool where to draw the dividing lines. A 40-page PDF split at page 20 gives you two files: pages 1 to 20 and pages 21 to 40.

You can also split at non-uniform boundaries. If the document has chapters ending at pages 12, 27, and 40, you'd enter three ranges: 1-12, 13-27, and 28-40. The tool produces three separate PDFs, one per range.

No re-encoding happens. Pages are copied from the original into new PDF containers, so text sharpness and image quality stay exactly the same as in the source file.

## Page range notation: how to write it

Most PDF splitters accept the same notation:

- **Contiguous range:** `1-10` gives you pages 1 through 10.
- **Single page:** `7` gives you just page 7.
- **Mixed:** `1-5, 8, 11-15` gives you one file containing pages 1 through 5, page 8, and pages 11 through 15.
- **Multiple output files:** Enter each range separately when you want separate PDFs rather than one combined output.

The notation follows the same convention as [print-range fields in PDF readers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF#Features), so it's familiar if you've ever printed specific pages from a PDF.

## How to find the right page number to split at

If you know roughly where the chapter or section boundary falls but not the exact page number, scroll through the PDF before opening the splitter. Most PDF readers (Adobe Reader, the browser's built-in viewer, Preview on Mac) show the current page number in the bottom toolbar or status bar.

For longer documents, use the page thumbnail panel in your reader. Thumbnails let you scan visually until you spot the right section header or content change, then read off the page number.

If the PDF has a table of contents, check it first. The TOC lists section start pages, which are exactly the split points you need.

## How to split a PDF into individual pages

To get every page as its own separate file, enter each page as a single-page range: `1-1` for the first file, `2-2` for the second, and so on. This is tedious for long documents.

Some PDF splitters have a "split every page" or "burst" mode that automates this. If the Split PDF tool doesn't show that option, the [Extract Pages tool](/extract-pages) lets you select and download individual pages without entering range notation.

Single-page extraction is common when:

- You need one page from a multi-page contract to send separately.
- You're pulling a single exhibit from a legal bundle.
- You want to share one chart or figure from a report without sending the whole document.

## Splitting vs extracting: which tool to use

Both operations work from page numbers, but they serve slightly different goals.

**Split** divides the entire document into named chunks. You define where the breaks go and get a file for each chunk. Good for breaking a long document into chapters or sections.

**Extract** lets you cherry-pick specific pages into a new file while the original stays intact. Good for pulling out a few pages that are scattered through a document.

For a 100-page report where you want chapters 1 to 3 (pages 1 to 30) as one file, use split. For pulling pages 7, 14, and 52 out of a report into a highlights document, use extract.

## Common use cases for splitting by page number

**Academic papers.** A downloaded thesis or course reader often bundles dozens of documents into one PDF. Splitting at chapter boundaries makes it easier to study or share individual sections.

**Legal documents.** Court filings, discovery bundles, and contract packages frequently arrive as a single large PDF. Splitting by exhibit or section lets legal teams route the right pages to the right people without sharing the entire file.

**Technical manuals.** A 300-page service manual with eight sections is easier to distribute when each section is a separate file. Technicians get only what they need for the job at hand.

**Filling in portions of a form.** If a multi-page form needs different people to complete different sections, splitting it by section lets each person receive only their pages, then merge the filled pages back with [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf).

## Splitting a PDF without Adobe Acrobat

Adobe Acrobat Pro can split PDFs, but it requires a paid subscription. Adobe's free online tool requires a sign-in and has file size limits.

Browser-based splitters skip both requirements. The Split PDF tool here uses [pdf-lib](https://www.npmjs.com/package/pdf-lib), an open-source library that runs page extraction inside your browser. No server receives your file. That matters when the document contains anything confidential, such as medical records, contracts, or financial statements.

If you're on a shared computer or a work machine where you'd rather not install or log into anything, a browser-based tool is the practical choice.
