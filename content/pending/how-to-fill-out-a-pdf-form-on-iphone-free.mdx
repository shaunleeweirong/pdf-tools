You can fill out a PDF form on iPhone for free without downloading anything. Open the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) in Safari, tap to pick your file, type into the detected fields, and download the finished form. No app, no account, no waiting.

That covers most standard forms: rental applications, consent forms, HR paperwork, tax documents, anything built with interactive fields. If the form is a flat scan or has no tappable fields, there's a different approach, and this post covers both.

## Fill a fillable PDF in Safari (no app needed)

For PDFs that have form fields built in (what the [PDF specification](https://opensource.adobe.com/dc-acrobat-sdk-docs/pdfstandards/pdfreference1.7old.pdf) calls AcroForm fields), a browser-based tool is the fastest route on iPhone.

1. Open Safari and go to the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form).
2. Tap "Choose file" and pick your PDF from Files, iCloud Drive, or your camera roll.
3. The tool detects the form fields automatically. Tap each one and type your answer.
4. When you've filled every field, tap download to save the completed form back to your device.

Your file stays in your browser the whole time. Nothing is uploaded to a server, and there's nothing to uninstall afterward.

This works well for multi-page forms, checkboxes, and forms with dropdown menus, which can behave inconsistently with iOS's native tools.

## Fill a flat or scanned form with iOS Markup

If you tap a form field and nothing happens, the PDF doesn't have interactive fields. It's a flat document, a scan, or a file that was created by printing to PDF without adding field data. You won't be able to type into it directly, but you can layer text on top.

iOS has a built-in tool for this:

1. Open the PDF in the Files app (or tap "Open in Files" from Mail).
2. Tap the pencil icon in the top-right corner to open Markup.
3. Tap the "+" button and choose "Text."
4. A text box appears on the page. Drag it to the blank line you want to fill.
5. Double-tap the box and type your answer. Use the format bar to adjust the size if the text is too large.
6. Repeat for each field, then tap "Done" to save.

The text sits as a layer on top of the page, so it looks typed when you print or share. Positioning is a little fiddly on small mobile screens, but it's free and needs no third-party tool at all.

For forms with many fields close together, a browser tool may be easier to work with. The [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) is worth a try even on flat PDFs, as it can sometimes recognize field placement from the page layout.

## Using iOS's built-in forms support

iPhones handle interactive PDFs natively in a couple of places, which is handy for quick edits:

- **Files app.** Open a fillable PDF, tap a text field, and a keyboard appears. Tap Done when you're finished.
- **Mail.** Tap a PDF attachment, then tap the Markup pencil icon. Interactive fields are tappable from inside Markup.

This works for simple forms with text fields. Where it falls short: checkboxes don't always toggle reliably, dropdown menus may not open, and forms with JavaScript logic won't behave correctly in a mobile viewer. If native doesn't work, the browser tool is the next step.

## Adding a signature

A lot of forms need a signature, not just typed text. After filling the form, the [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) lets you draw your signature, place it on the page, and download the signed file. You can resize and reposition the signature until it lines up with the signature line.

If you're already in Markup, there's a shortcut: tap "+" in the toolbar and choose "Signature." Draw with your finger, then drag it into position. Markup saves your signature so you can reuse it on future forms.

## Filling a form and then merging or attaching it

If you fill an interactive PDF form and then want to combine it with another document, be careful. Most merge tools, including our [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf), copy the page content but strip the form field layer. The text you typed in an interactive field can disappear in the merged result.

The fix is to flatten the form before merging. Flattening locks the field values permanently into the page, so they become part of the document's content rather than data in a removable field layer. Use the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) on the completed form first, then add it to a merge.

This matters most in workflows where you fill a form and attach it with a cover letter or combine several signed documents into one file. Flatten first, merge second.

## Which method to pick

Here's a quick way to decide:

- **Fillable form, want it done fast:** [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) in Safari.
- **Flat or scanned form, already in Files or Mail:** iOS Markup with text boxes.
- **Need to add a signature too:** [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) after filling, or Markup's signature feature.
- **Merging the filled form with other documents:** [Flatten PDF](/flatten-pdf) the form first.

None of these require creating an account or downloading an app. The browser tools run directly in Safari, and iOS Markup is already on your phone.

For more on [how PDF forms and form fields work under the hood](https://www.adobe.com/content/dam/acom/en/devnet/pdf/pdfs/PDF32000_2008.pdf), Adobe's PDF reference covers the AcroForm specification in detail.
