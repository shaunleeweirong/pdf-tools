The fastest way to combine Word and Excel files into one PDF is a two-step process: export each file as a PDF on its own, then merge those PDFs into a single document for free. No desktop software, no sign-up, and the whole thing takes about two minutes.

## Why you need two steps

Most free online PDF mergers only accept PDF files as input. That means you have to convert your Word document and Excel spreadsheet to PDF first, then bring them together. This is actually the better approach anyway, because each app's own export engine handles its fonts, tables, charts, and formatting far more accurately than a third-party converter would.

## Step 1: Save your Word document as a PDF

**In Microsoft Word (desktop):** Go to **File > Export > Create PDF/XPS**, then click **Publish**. Word converts the file and saves it with your fonts, images, and layout intact.

**In the free Word web app** at office.com: Open the document, go to **File > Save As > Download as PDF**. The download starts immediately.

**On a Mac:** Open the document, press **Command + P**, click the **PDF** button at the bottom-left of the print dialog, and choose **Save as PDF**. This works from any app on macOS.

**In Google Docs:** Go to **File > Download > PDF Document (.pdf)**.

Any of these routes produces a PDF that closely matches your original Word file. Page size, margins, and fonts all carry over.

## Step 2: Save your Excel spreadsheet as a PDF

The export path in Excel is the same as Word: **File > Export > Create PDF/XPS** on the desktop, or **File > Download > PDF Document** in the web app.

Before you export, two settings can save you a lot of frustration:

**Set a print area.** If your sheet has data in columns A through H but empty columns beyond that, Excel may include the empty area in the PDF and produce a sprawling, hard-to-read result. Select the cells you actually want, then go to **Page Layout > Print Area > Set Print Area**. Only that range will appear in the PDF.

**Use Scale to Fit.** In the **Page Layout** tab, find **Scale to Fit**. Set **Width** to **1 page** and leave **Height** on automatic. This shrinks your columns to fit across a single PDF page. For large datasets, 2 or 3 pages wide is fine, but "1 page wide" is a sensible default for typical reports. Microsoft's own guide to [saving Office documents as PDF](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/save-as-pdf-d85416c5-7d77-4fd6-a216-6f4bf7c7c110) covers these options in more detail.

**Workbooks with multiple sheets:** Right-click any sheet tab and choose **Select All Sheets**, then export. This packages every sheet into one PDF. If you want only certain sheets, hold **Ctrl** on Windows (or **Cmd** on Mac) and click each tab before exporting.

Google Sheets handles this well too. Go to **File > Download > PDF Document**. The dialog that opens lets you choose a single sheet or the whole workbook, and adjust scaling before downloading.

## Step 3: Merge the two PDFs into one

With both PDFs ready, open the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf). Drop in your Word PDF and your Excel PDF, drag them into the order you want, and click **Merge**. The combined file downloads in seconds.

**A note on file size:** Merging does not inflate the result. The output stays very close to the sum of the two input files, so if your Word PDF is 280 KB and your Excel PDF is 120 KB, the merged result will be around 400 KB.

If that merged file is still too large to email, keep in mind that Gmail and Outlook.com both cap email attachments at 25 MB. Because email clients encode attachments using [Base64 encoding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base64), which adds roughly 33% overhead, the practical upper limit for a PDF attachment is around 18 MB. If you are over that, try the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf). One caution: compression is most effective on PDFs that contain photos or scanned images. For Word reports and Excel spreadsheets that are mostly text and numbers, compression can actually make the file larger rather than smaller, so test it first.

## Check the result before sending

Open the merged PDF and scroll through it before you share it:

- **Broken Excel layout:** If a table or chart is cut off or wraps in odd places, go back to Excel, adjust the print area or scaling settings, re-export, and re-merge. It takes only a minute.
- **Missing sheets:** If you expected multiple Excel tabs but only see one, the workbook exported the active sheet only. Re-export with all tabs selected.
- **Page order:** If the Excel section should come before the Word section, drag the files into the correct order in the merger before clicking Merge.

## Doing this on a phone

**iPhone or iPad:** Open the Word or Excel file in the Microsoft app. Tap the three-dot menu and choose **Print**. On the print preview, pinch outward on the preview thumbnail to open the file as a PDF, then tap the share icon to save it to Files.

**Android:** Open the file in the Microsoft app, choose **Print** from the menu, select **Save as PDF** as the destination printer, and save. Once both PDFs are saved to your device, open the Merge PDF tool in your mobile browser and follow the same steps as on desktop.
