Merging a PDF and a Word document is a two-step process: export the Word file as a PDF, then combine both PDFs with a free merge tool. The whole thing takes under two minutes and doesn't require Adobe Acrobat or any paid software.

## Why you need to convert the Word file first

PDF and Word are fundamentally different file formats. A Word document (.docx) stores editable content, paragraph styles, and fonts in a structured package. A PDF stores a fixed-layout snapshot of a page, closer to what you'd get from a printout. Most merge tools only accept one format at a time, so the first step is getting everything into PDF.

Converting is free and built into Microsoft Word, Google Docs, and LibreOffice.

## Step 1: Export your Word document as a PDF

**In Microsoft Word (Windows or Mac):**
1. Open the document.
2. Go to **File > Save As** (or **File > Export** on Mac).
3. Choose **PDF** from the format dropdown.
4. Click **Save**.

**In Google Docs:**
1. Open the document in Google Docs.
2. Go to **File > Download > PDF Document (.pdf)**.
3. The PDF saves to your Downloads folder.

**In LibreOffice Writer (free, open-source):**
1. Open the document.
2. Go to **File > Export as PDF**.
3. Click **Export**, then **Export PDF**.

[LibreOffice](https://www.libreoffice.org/) is a free, open-source office suite available on Windows, Mac, and Linux. It opens .docx files without a Microsoft account, which makes it useful if someone sent you a Word file and you don't have Word installed.

All three methods produce a PDF that matches the original document's layout, fonts, and images.

## Step 2: Combine the PDFs with the Merge PDF tool

Once you have both files as PDFs, use the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool to combine them:

1. Open the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool.
2. Click **Select PDF files**, or drag both PDFs onto the page.
3. Drag the files up or down to set the order you want.
4. Click **Merge** and download the combined file.

The tool runs entirely in your browser. Your files are never uploaded to a server, so nothing leaves your device.

File size after merging stays close to the combined size of your inputs. In our tests, files totaling 971.9 KB merged to 970.5 KB, a difference of less than 0.1%. Combining files will not push a document over an email limit if the individual parts already fit under it.

## Watch out: interactive form fields don't survive a merge

If either PDF has fillable form fields (the kind you type values into), merging will strip those fields. The page content stays intact, but the interactive fields are gone, and any values you entered may not carry over to the output.

To keep your filled data, flatten the PDF first. The [Flatten PDF](/flatten-pdf) tool burns the typed values into the page as permanent, visible text. After flattening, the values survive the merge just fine.

This matters most on workflows like:
- Attaching a completed job application form to a cover letter
- Combining a signed liability waiver with a booking confirmation
- Merging a filled invoice with a terms-and-conditions document

Fill the form, flatten it, then merge.

## Doing this on a phone

The process works on mobile browsers. You just need to export the Word file to PDF on your phone first.

**On iPhone with the Microsoft Word app:**
Tap the three-dot menu, choose **Export**, then **Send a Copy > PDF**.

**On Android with the Microsoft Word app:**
Tap **File > Save a Copy**, choose your location, and select **PDF** as the format.

**On either platform with Google Docs:**
Tap the three-dot menu, choose **Share & export > Save as > PDF Document**.

Once you have the PDF, open the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool in your mobile browser, upload both files, and download the result.

## If you received the Word file from someone else

If you don't have Word installed, Google Docs handles .docx files well. Upload the .docx to Google Drive, open it in Google Docs, and export it as a PDF. No installation needed, just a Google account.

If you prefer a desktop option that doesn't require an account, [LibreOffice](https://www.libreoffice.org/) opens .docx files and exports them to PDF for free.

## A note on format

PDF is almost always the right format for a final combined document. It looks identical on every device and doesn't require the recipient to have Word or any other software installed. The [PDF format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) is an open standard published as ISO 32000 by the International Organization for Standardization.

If the recipient needs to edit the document after receiving it, send the original Word file separately. Extracting editable text from a merged PDF is more work than it's worth for a straightforward exchange.
