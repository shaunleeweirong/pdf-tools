You can rotate a PDF online without uploading it to any server, and it's free. The [Rotate PDF](/rotate-pdf) tool processes your file entirely inside your browser. Your document stays on your device, and nothing is sent to a remote computer.

## Why "without uploading" is worth caring about

Most online PDF tools work by sending your file to their servers, processing it there, and sending the result back. That's fine for many files. But if your PDF is a contract, a medical record, a tax return, or an immigration document, you probably don't want it traveling to a third-party server you can't audit.

Browser-based tools avoid this entirely. They use [WebAssembly](https://webassembly.org/) and JavaScript to do the work right inside your browser tab. Your file goes nowhere. You can even turn off your Wi-Fi after the page loads and the tool still works.

This isn't a privacy claim you have to take on faith. Open your browser's developer tools, go to the Network tab, and drop a PDF into the Rotate PDF tool. You'll see requests for fonts and scripts, but no request that carries your PDF as a payload.

## How to rotate a PDF without uploading it

1. Go to the [Rotate PDF](/rotate-pdf) tool.
2. Drop your PDF onto the page, or click to open the file picker.
3. Choose a rotation angle: **90° clockwise**, **180°**, or **270° clockwise**.
4. Click **Rotate PDF** and then download the result.

The whole thing takes about ten seconds for a typical document. The downloaded file has "rotated" added to its name so you can tell it apart from the original.

## What each rotation angle does

- **90° clockwise** fixes a page that looks sideways when you have to tilt your head to the right to read it. This is the most common fix for scanned pages.
- **270° clockwise** (equivalent to 90° counterclockwise) fixes a page that needs a left tilt to read.
- **180°** flips every page upside down. Use this when a document was scanned or photographed wrong-side-up.

If you're not sure which angle to pick, 90° clockwise is usually correct for pages from a phone camera. Try it, and if the result looks worse, undo and try 270°.

## How to rotate just one page, not the whole document

The Rotate PDF tool applies the chosen angle to every page at once. That works great when the whole document is sideways, but not when a 30-page PDF has one sideways page in the middle.

For that, use [Organize PDF](/organize-pdf). It shows a thumbnail of every page in your document and lets you rotate pages individually before saving. You can also delete or reorder pages in the same step.

## Does rotating a PDF hurt quality?

No, and the reason is worth understanding. PDFs store rotation as a metadata entry called `Rotate` in each page's dictionary, as defined in the [PDF specification (ISO 32000)](https://opensource.adobe.com/dc-acrobat-sdk-docs/pdfstandards/PDF32000_2008.pdf). When a viewer opens a rotated page, it applies the rotation during rendering. The underlying content (vector text, fonts, embedded images) is never touched, re-encoded, or degraded.

This applies to scanned PDFs too. A scanned page stores the scan as an embedded image (usually JPEG). Rotating the page updates the `Rotate` flag but leaves the JPEG itself alone. No re-compression, no added artifacts.

## Does rotating change the file size?

Barely. The tool writes a tiny metadata change to each page. For a typical ten-page PDF, the size difference is less than a kilobyte. You won't notice it.

## Rotating a PDF on a phone

The browser-based tool works on mobile just as well as on a desktop. On an iPhone, open Safari, go to the Rotate PDF page, tap the file picker, and choose your PDF from the Files app. On Android, use Chrome and pick from your Downloads folder or wherever the file lives.

This is handy for scans taken with a phone camera. Photos taken in portrait mode often get embedded in PDFs with the wrong orientation because the camera captures them horizontally. A 90° clockwise rotation usually corrects it.

## What if the PDF still looks sideways in your viewer?

A small number of PDF readers ignore the `Rotate` entry and always display pages in their raw, unrotated orientation. This is a bug in the viewer, not a problem with your file. If the result looks wrong in one app, try another. Chrome's built-in PDF viewer, macOS Preview, and Adobe Acrobat Reader all respect the rotation flag.

If you specifically need the rotation embedded at the pixel level rather than as a flag (for example, because you're sending the file to an old viewer you can't control), you can print the PDF to a new PDF using your operating system's print dialog. That re-renders each page at its displayed orientation and produces a new file where the rotation is baked in.

## When to use something other than a rotation tool

Rotation fixes orientation. It doesn't fix:

- **Upside-down or mirrored text from a bad scan.** That's a scanner driver or scan app problem.
- **A PDF that's too large to send.** Try the [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) tool if the file is image-heavy, or split it with [Split PDF](/split-pdf) if it's long.
- **Pages in the wrong order.** Use [Organize PDF](/organize-pdf) to reorder them.
- **Form fields that disappeared after editing.** That's a flattening issue, not rotation.

Knowing what the tool does and doesn't do saves you from a frustrating loop of re-processing a file that has a different problem.
