## How to compress a PDF to 50 KB

To compress a PDF to 50 KB, open the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf), upload your file, select **High** compression, and download the result. High compression squeezes an image-heavy PDF the most. In testing, a six-page image deck at 913.4 KB came out at 175.6 KB at High, an 80.8% reduction.

Whether you actually hit 50 KB depends on what's inside the PDF and how many pages it has. The rest of this guide covers exactly when it works and what to do when it doesn't.

## When compression can reach 50 KB

The compressor rasterizes each page as a [JPEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG). That works in your favour when pages are already photographs or scans. Compressing an image of a photo is efficient; compressing text stored as vector data is the opposite of efficient.

Some rough benchmarks based on actual measurements:

- A 6-page image deck at 913.4 KB reached 175.6 KB at High (about 29 KB per page on average).
- A 2-page scanned document starting around 250 KB would likely land around 50 to 75 KB at High.
- A 1-page scan at around 150 KB could get to 30 to 50 KB.

If your document is longer than two or three pages, split it first. Use the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) to pull out only the pages needed for the submission, then compress that smaller file.

## When compression will not help

If your PDF is mainly text, tables, or vector graphics (contracts, invoices, forms, Word exports), skip the compressor. The tool converts every page to a JPEG photograph of the text. A 20-page text report at 23.5 KB grew to 1.4 MB after Medium compression. That's not a typo. Text stored as vectors is tiny; the same text rendered as a photograph is not.

For text-based PDFs that are too large for an upload limit, the useful fixes are:

- **Remove unnecessary pages.** Use [Split PDF](/split-pdf) or [Delete Pages](/delete-pages) to keep only what the form requires.
- **Re-export from the source.** If you created the PDF from Word, PowerPoint, or Google Docs, try exporting again at a lower image quality or removing large embedded images first.
- **Check for hidden layers.** Some PDFs carry revision history or hidden annotations that inflate the file. Opening in a PDF editor and saving a flattened copy can help.

## Step-by-step: compress a scanned PDF to 50 KB

1. Go to the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf).
2. Drag your file onto the upload area or click to browse.
3. Select **High** compression (the smallest file size option).
4. Click **Compress PDF**.
5. Download the result and check its size.

If the file is still over 50 KB, open the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf), extract only the pages the form actually asks for, and compress that smaller file. Most submissions that require 50 KB limits want a single supporting document, not a full multi-page report.

## Why so many portals cap uploads at 50 KB

Government job portals and exam bodies like [SSC and UPSC](https://ssc.gov.in/) set 50 KB or 100 KB limits to keep server storage low and ensure documents load reliably on slow mobile connections. Banking recruitment systems (IBPS, RRB) apply the same logic. Visa and scholarship portals typically want a scanned photo, signature, or certificate, each under 50 KB.

The limit usually applies per document, not to the whole application. A single scanned page, properly sized, should fit under 50 KB with High compression as long as the scan isn't enormous to begin with.

## Before you compress: check what your PDF contains

The quickest way to tell whether compression will help:

**Text is crisp and selectable.** Zoom into the PDF and try to select a word. If you can highlight individual characters, the text is stored as vectors. Compression will make the file larger. Skip it.

**Text looks slightly fuzzy or pixelated.** The PDF is already a scan or a rasterized image. Compression will help. Go ahead.

**Check size per page.** Divide the file size by the page count. Under 20 KB per page usually means vector text. Over 100 KB per page usually means image content.

## Compressing twice does not help

Some guides suggest running compression a second time to squeeze out more size. It makes things worse. Feeding a previously compressed file back through Medium compression measured at 315.3 KB (already compressed once) came out at 598.9 KB. Re-encoding JPEG data adds artifacts and size, not savings.

Pick the right level the first time. If High is not enough, start again from the original file rather than chaining passes. The [PDF compression standard](https://www.pdfa.org/resource/iso-32000-pdf/) does not include a lossless path for raster content, so there is a floor below which JPEG cannot go.

## What to do if nothing works

A very large multi-page scanned PDF may genuinely be impossible to compress to 50 KB without major quality loss. In that case:

1. Split it down to the single page the portal actually requires, not the whole document.
2. Try scanning at a lower resolution (150 DPI rather than 300 DPI) if you have access to the original scan.
3. Check whether the portal will accept a JPEG or PNG directly, then export the page as an image using the [PDF to JPG tool](/pdf-to-jpg) and submit that instead.

JPEGs at 72 DPI for a single passport-style page can sit well under 50 KB while still being readable.
