If your PDF is too large for Google Forms, the fix depends on what type of file it is. For scanned documents and image-based PDFs, the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) can shrink it significantly, free, in your browser with no account needed. For typed documents, invoices, or digital forms, compression will actually make the file larger. For those, use [Split PDF](/split-pdf) to break it into smaller pieces instead.

## What is the file size limit in Google Forms?

The person who built the form controls it. [Google Forms](https://workspace.google.com/products/forms/) lets form creators set a maximum file size per upload: 1 MB, 10 MB, 100 MB, 1 GB, or 10 GB. When a form is first built, the default is often 1 MB per file, which is a tight limit for anything involving scanned pages or photos.

When you upload to a Google Form, the file goes into the form creator's Google Drive. If the form says your file is too large, the problem is that per-file size cap, not the creator's overall Drive storage.

If you see a different error about "limit reached," the form's total collected storage cap may be full. That's a separate issue that only the form creator can fix by archiving old responses or expanding storage.

## Why your PDF might be larger than you expect

PDFs vary in size for very different reasons, and knowing which applies to yours tells you the right fix.

**Scanned documents.** When you photograph a document with your phone or use a scanner app, each page becomes a high-resolution image. A single scanned page can be 1 to 3 MB at full resolution. A four-page assignment scan can easily reach 6 to 10 MB even though it contains nothing but handwritten text you could read on a phone screen.

**PDFs with embedded photos or graphics.** Reports, portfolios, and presentation slides that include photos get large quickly. A single full-bleed photograph at print resolution can be 2 to 3 MB before it even goes into a PDF container.

**Typed or digital documents.** Word documents saved as PDFs, invoices, contracts, and fillable forms are usually small, often under 1 MB for several pages. If one of these is large, the culprit is typically an embedded font package, a large logo inserted at the wrong resolution, or a photo dropped in without being resized first.

## The fastest fix for image-heavy and scanned PDFs: compress it

If your PDF is made of scanned pages or photos, compression is the right first step. The [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) converts each page to a JPEG and re-encodes it at a lower quality, which cuts file size significantly for image-based content.

In our own measurements, a 913 KB image deck compressed to 315 KB at Medium quality (a 65.5% reduction) and to 176 KB at High quality (an 80.8% reduction). A scanned document that's 8 MB would follow a similar pattern, landing under 3 MB at Medium or under 2 MB at High.

How to compress a scanned PDF for Google Forms:

1. Go to the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf). Your file is never sent to any server.
2. Drag your PDF onto the page, or click to select it.
3. Pick a compression level. Medium is a good starting point for scanned homework, lab reports, or submitted documents. If the result is still over the limit, go back to your original and try High.
4. Download the compressed file.
5. Upload it to the Google Form.

The whole process takes under a minute for most files.

One important thing to avoid: don't run the same file through compression twice. Re-compressing an already-compressed PDF at the same quality setting makes it larger, not smaller. In our testing, a file that had been compressed once at Medium grew by 90% when put through compression again at the same level. If Medium doesn't get you under the limit, start fresh from your original file and pick High.

## Do not compress text-based PDFs

This is worth being direct about. Compressing a typed document, a digital invoice, a filled form, or anything that started life as digital text will make it larger. Sometimes much larger.

Text PDFs store characters as [vector data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_graphics), which is extremely compact. A compression tool that rasterizes pages converts those compact characters into a photograph of the page instead, and photographs of text are far heavier than the text itself.

In our testing, a 23.5 KB text report grew to 1.4 MB after compression at Medium quality. That's roughly 60 times larger. Compressing a text PDF that's already near the upload limit would likely push it well over.

If your PDF is typed or digital, two options actually help:

**Split it.** The [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) lets you divide a large document into smaller parts. If the Google Form accepts multiple file uploads, submit each part separately. If it only takes one file at a time, check whether the form creator can be reached to raise the limit.

**Ask the form creator to raise the limit.** They can go into the file upload question settings and change the maximum file size to 10 MB or higher in a few clicks. For forms that need to receive professional documents, 10 MB or 100 MB is a more practical limit.

## If the form creator set a very low limit

A 1 MB limit is tight enough that even a modest scanned document will fail. If you've compressed your file and it's still over the cap, the most reliable path is to reach out to the person who built the form. They can change the maximum file size without affecting any other part of the form. It's one of the most common adjustments form creators make after getting feedback from respondents.

If you can't reach the form creator and need to stay under 1 MB, your best shot is High compression on a scanned PDF, or splitting a larger document into single-page files and submitting them one at a time.

## A note on privacy

If you're uploading a document with sensitive information, such as a medical form, tax document, or legal filing, you may not want to send it through a third-party service. The [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) and [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) on this site run entirely in your browser using [WebAssembly](https://webassembly.org/). Your file is never sent to any server. Everything happens locally on your device, so you can process sensitive documents without them leaving your machine.
