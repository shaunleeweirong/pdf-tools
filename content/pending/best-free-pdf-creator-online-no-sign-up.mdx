The best free PDF creator online runs entirely in your browser. No sign-up, no software to install, and no file uploads to a third-party server. This guide covers every way to create a PDF for free, from turning a pile of images into a document to combining existing files into one.

## What does "creating a PDF" actually mean?

"PDF creator" covers a few different tasks that people often group together:

- **Images to PDF**: You have photos, screenshots, or scanned pages and want to turn them into a single PDF file.
- **Merge documents**: You already have PDFs and want to combine them into one.
- **Add text or notes**: You have a blank or partially filled PDF and want to type on it.
- **Protect the result**: You want to lock the finished PDF with a password.

Each of these has a different tool. Starting with the right one saves you time.

## Turn images into a PDF (the most common case)

If your starting point is JPG or PNG files (scanned pages, photos, screenshots), the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) handles this directly. Drop your files in, drag them into the order you want, and download.

A few things worth knowing:
- JPG and PNG are both supported. HEIC (iPhone photos) needs to be converted to JPG first; most phones and operating systems can do this with a free built-in export option.
- There's no cap on the number of images per PDF. Add as many as you need.
- The tool runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, which matters for scanned contracts, medical records, or ID documents.

The [PDF format itself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF) was designed to preserve the appearance of a document across different devices and operating systems. That's why converting images to PDF is often the right move when you need to share something that must look exactly right on the recipient's screen.

## Combine existing PDFs into one document

Got multiple PDFs you want to package into one? The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) combines them in whatever order you choose. You can drag to rearrange before merging.

One thing worth knowing from actual measurement: merging does not inflate the file. Three PDFs totaling 971.9 KB merged to 970.5 KB, barely any change. If your goal is to fit under an email size limit, combining files won't push you over it in any meaningful way.

There's one catch: merging strips interactive form fields. If any of your PDFs has a filled-out form (with typed values in fields), those fields won't survive the merge intact. To keep the values, flatten the form first with the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf), which burns the typed values into the page as plain text. Then merge. This is the right workflow for job applications and contract packages where a filled form needs to travel alongside a cover letter.

## Add text, boxes, or annotations to a PDF

If you need to type on an existing PDF or add sticky notes and boxes, the [Edit PDF tool](/edit-pdf) lets you do this without any software. Click to place a text box, type your content, and move it where it belongs on the page.

This works well for:
- Filling in a PDF that doesn't have form fields
- Adding your name, date, or address to a form that isn't interactive
- Marking up a document before sending it back

If the PDF already has fillable form fields, the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) handles those directly. It detects the fields automatically and lets you tab through them.

## Password-protect a PDF you just made

Once you've created your PDF, you can lock it with the [Protect PDF tool](/protect-pdf). It uses AES-256 encryption and runs locally in your browser, meaning the password is never transmitted anywhere. The output comes back to you as an encrypted PDF file.

Password protection adds minimal overhead to the file size. On a 23.5 KB document, the encrypted version was 24.6 KB, so it won't push you over any size limit.

One thing it does not do: it doesn't prevent someone who knows the password from copying or printing the file. If you need that level of control, you'd need a tool that sets owner permissions separately, which most free online tools don't expose. For most purposes, a good password is enough.

## When you need to create a PDF from Word or Excel

The tools on this site work with images and existing PDFs. If you're starting from a Microsoft Word, Excel, Google Docs, or Google Sheets file, the simplest free path is your operating system's Print to PDF feature:

- **Windows**: Open the file, press Ctrl+P, select "Microsoft Print to PDF" or "Save as PDF" from the printer list, and click Print.
- **Mac**: Open the file, press Cmd+P, click the PDF button at the bottom left, and choose "Save as PDF."
- **Google Docs / Sheets**: Go to File, then Download, then PDF Document.

This produces a PDF you can then bring into any of the tools above. Print to PDF is free on every major operating system and doesn't require any third-party tool at all.

The [ISO 32000 standard](https://www.iso.org/standard/75839.html) defines how PDF files are structured, and one consequence is that a PDF produced by any compliant tool can be opened by any compliant reader. You don't need to worry about compatibility between how you create the file and how someone else opens it.

## No sign-up, no watermarks, nothing to install

Every tool above runs in your browser. Files are processed locally, which means nothing is sent to a server and you don't need an account. There are no watermarks on downloads, no trial period, and no cap on how many PDFs you can create.

If you run into a PDF that's too large to email after combining, the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) can reduce the size, though it works best on image-heavy PDFs. A text-heavy document won't benefit from compression and may actually get larger, because the tool rasterizes pages to JPEG. For oversized text documents, splitting is usually the better path.
