Compressing a PDF for an ATS portal sounds simple, but the right fix depends entirely on what is making your file large in the first place. If you compress the wrong kind of document, the file gets bigger rather than smaller.

## Why ATS portals reject large PDFs

[Applicant Tracking Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applicant_tracking_system) like Workday, Greenhouse, Taleo, and iCIMS handle thousands of applications daily. Each has a cap on how large an uploaded file can be. Typical limits:

| ATS Platform | Upload Limit | Parsing Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Taleo | ~2 MB | 2 MB |
| Workday | ~5 MB | 5 MB |
| USAJobs (federal jobs) | 5 MB | 5 MB |
| Greenhouse | 10 MB | ~2.5 MB |
| iCIMS | 1 to 5 MB (varies) | varies |

The Greenhouse case is especially frustrating: the portal will accept a 4 MB resume without complaint, but internally it stops parsing content above 2.5 MB. A recruiter gets your file, but the ATS has extracted none of your job titles, companies, or skills. For maximum safety across all platforms, aim for under 500 KB.

## What actually makes a resume PDF large

A text-only, one-page resume should weigh somewhere between 50 KB and 200 KB. If yours is larger, one of these is usually responsible:

**Embedded photos or logos.** A headshot or high-resolution company logo can add 500 KB to 2 MB on its own. This is the most common culprit in designer-built resumes.

**Design tool exports.** PDFs exported from Canva, Adobe InDesign, or similar tools often embed fonts and raster assets even when the design looks text-heavy. The same resume content can be 40 KB out of Microsoft Word and 4 MB out of Canva.

**Scanned pages attached alongside your resume.** Some applicants attach a scanned cover letter, reference list, or transcript alongside a typed resume and combine them into one PDF. Scanned pages are photo content and can be large.

**Revision history or hidden metadata.** Less common, but some PDF creation tools write revision trails that accumulate over multiple saves.

## When the Compress PDF tool actually helps

Our [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) rasterizes every page to JPEG, which makes it very good at shrinking files whose pages are already images: scanned documents, photo portfolios, and mixed-content files where photos dominate.

If your application package includes a **scanned reference letter** or **scanned supporting document**, compression can cut that file down significantly. A 913 KB image deck compresses to 175 KB at the High setting, a reduction of about 81%.

That is the right use case. Open your document in a PDF viewer and look at it. If the pages are photographs of paper (blurry edges, slight skew, gray background), compression will help.

## When compression makes things worse

If your resume is **typed text from Word, Google Docs, or Pages**, do not compress it. The tool converts each page to a JPEG of that page, which turns a compact vector file into a rasterized image. A 20-page text report measured at 23.5 KB grew to 1.4 MB after one compression pass. A single-page resume would show a similar blowup.

There is no partial exception here. Even a mostly-text document with a small logo grew from 35 KB to 171 KB after compression, a 5x increase.

## How to actually shrink a typed resume PDF

**Step 1: Remove the profile photo.**
If your resume has a headshot, delete it from the source document. Depending on the image, this alone can cut 500 KB to 2 MB.

**Step 2: Re-export directly from your word processor.**
If you built the resume in Canva or a design tool, recreate it in Google Docs, Word, or Pages and use File > Export as PDF or Print > Save as PDF. This typically reduces size by 80 to 95%.

**Step 3: Reduce image DPI in embedded graphics.**
If you keep a logo, resize it to roughly 150px wide in the source document before exporting. A large logo embedded at 300 DPI can add several hundred KB.

**Step 4: Flatten any fillable form before merging.**
If you are combining a filled application form with your resume into one file, flatten the form first with the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) so the field values are baked into the page. Then combine them with the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf). Merging does not inflate file size beyond the sum of its parts, so the total will be roughly what the individual files add up to.

## Step-by-step: reducing a scanned application document

1. Open the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf).
2. Upload the scanned document (cover letter, reference letter, transcript, or similar).
3. Choose **High (smallest)** for maximum size reduction, or **Medium** to preserve more detail.
4. Download the output. Check the file size before uploading to the portal.
5. Do not run a second compression pass on the output. A second pass re-encodes already-compressed JPEGs and makes the file larger, not smaller.

If you also need to combine this with your typed resume, upload both to the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) afterward.

## Which file format should I use?

PDF is almost always the right choice. Most ATS platforms parse PDF content reliably, and PDF preserves your layout so a recruiter sees what you see. Word (.docx) is also accepted everywhere, but formatting can shift between systems.

[Adobe Acrobat's research on PDF file formats for resumes](https://www.adobe.com/acrobat/resources/resume-pdf.html) confirms that a cleanly-exported PDF avoids the parsing problems that plague design-heavy documents.

## Quick checklist before uploading

- [ ] Resume is a typed document, not a scan
- [ ] No profile photo embedded
- [ ] Exported from Word or Google Docs, not a design tool
- [ ] File size is under 500 KB
- [ ] If a scanned page is included, it has been compressed separately first
- [ ] Filled form fields have been flattened before merging

A plain, text-first resume PDF built from a word processor will pass every ATS portal's size limit without any compression at all.
