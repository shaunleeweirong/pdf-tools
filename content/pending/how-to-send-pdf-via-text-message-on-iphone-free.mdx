Sending a PDF via text message on iPhone is straightforward when both people use iMessage, but it can fail silently when the conversation falls back to regular SMS or MMS. Here's how to send a PDF via text message on iPhone, what limits to know about, and how to fix the "file too large" problem without installing anything.

## How to send a PDF via text from the Files app

This is the fastest method and works for any PDF stored on your iPhone or in iCloud Drive.

1. Open the **Files** app.
2. Find your PDF and tap it to open it.
3. Tap the **Share** button (the box with an arrow pointing up).
4. Tap **Messages** in the share sheet.
5. Type the recipient's name or number, add an optional message, and tap the blue **Send** arrow.

That's it. The PDF attaches directly to the conversation.

## How to send from another app

If your PDF lives in Mail, Safari, or a notes app, you don't need to move it to Files first. Open the PDF in whatever app holds it, tap the share icon, then choose Messages from the options. The share sheet works the same way.

## iMessage vs MMS: why the bubble color matters

When you text another iPhone user and both phones have iMessage enabled, your messages show up with **blue bubbles**. That conversation runs over iMessage, which [supports attachments up to 100 MB](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255157456). Most PDFs fit under that ceiling without any changes.

When you text an Android user, or when iMessage is off, the chat uses MMS (Multimedia Messaging Service), shown with **green bubbles**. [MMS attachment limits](https://www.texty.pro/help-center-articles/what-is-the-file-size-limit-for-mms-text-messages) are set by each carrier, not by Apple. In the US, most carriers cap MMS attachments at somewhere between 300 KB and 1.5 MB. A PDF you scanned or exported from a presentation is almost always larger than that.

The confusing part: iOS doesn't always warn you when a send fails. The message looks sent on your end, but the recipient never gets the attachment.

## What to do when a PDF is too large to text

There are two practical routes depending on what the other person expects.

### Option 1: Compress the PDF first

If the PDF contains scanned pages, photos, or images, you can often shrink it enough to go through MMS. Use the free [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) in your browser. It runs entirely on your device, so no file is uploaded to a server. After compressing, save the result to Files and send it as described above.

One thing to know: the compressor works by rasterizing each page to a JPEG image. It reduces file size well for image-heavy PDFs, but it will actually make a text-only PDF larger. If your PDF is a short text document, skip compression and use the cloud link option below instead.

### Option 2: Share a cloud link

Upload the PDF to [Google Drive](https://drive.google.com), iCloud Drive (Files app), or Dropbox, then share the link in your text. The recipient taps the link and downloads the file. This sidesteps the MMS size limit entirely and works whether the recipient has an iPhone or not.

This also works when the PDF is huge (a hundred pages or more) and you'd rather not ask someone to wait for a download over cellular.

### Option 3: Break it up with Split PDF

If you only need the other person to see part of a large document, the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) lets you extract specific pages and save them as a smaller file. A 40-page report often has one or two relevant pages, and a 300 KB excerpt will go through MMS where the full file never would.

## Sending from photos or scans

If you just photographed something and want to send it as a PDF, you have two choices. If the other person just needs to read it, you can send the photos directly (JPEGs have no trouble with MMS limits). If they specifically need a PDF, use the [Images to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf) to combine your photos into one file, then compress it before sending.

## A quick checklist before you send

- Blue bubbles? You're on iMessage and the 100 MB limit applies. Most PDFs will go through fine.
- Green bubbles? Assume a ~1 MB cap. Check your file size in Files (long-press the PDF and choose Info). If it's over 1 MB, compress or use a link.
- File still failing after compression? Share a Google Drive or iCloud link instead. It always works and the recipient gets the full-quality file.
- Sending a scanned or photo-based PDF? Compression should help. Sending a text contract or form? Skip compression and share a link.

There's no single right answer here because the limit depends on your carrier and the recipient's device, but this checklist covers the cases that trip people up most often.
