The best free Adobe Acrobat alternatives are browser-based PDF tools that handle merging, compressing, signing, protecting, and converting PDFs with no subscription and no sign-up. [Adobe Acrobat Pro costs $19.99 per month on an annual plan](https://www.adobe.com/acrobat/pricing.html), roughly $240 a year, for tasks that free tools cover just as well.

The free Acrobat Reader opens PDFs, adds annotations, and fills in signature fields. Everything else, merging, editing, compressing, protecting, requires a paid plan. Here's what to use instead.

## What tasks do most people actually need from Acrobat?

Before picking a replacement, it helps to be honest about which features you use day to day. For most people it comes down to five things:

1. Merging two or more PDFs into one file
2. Shrinking a large PDF to send by email or submit via a web form
3. Signing a document or filling in a form
4. Protecting a file with a password
5. Converting a PDF to JPG (or vice versa)

Every one of these is free in your browser, with no account.

## Merging PDFs without Adobe Acrobat

The [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) combines files in the order you drag them in, with no limit on how many you add. Merging doesn't add bulk to the result. In our tests, three files totalling 971.9 KB produced a 970.5 KB output, coming in slightly under the combined size because shared PDF structure is written once. You won't push yourself over an attachment limit just by combining files.

One thing worth knowing before you merge: PDF forms lose their interactive fields in the process. If you've filled in a form and want to include it in a combined document, flatten it first with the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf). That bakes the values into the page so they survive the merge intact.

## Compressing PDFs without Adobe Acrobat

This is where most guides get it wrong, so it's worth being direct.

Our [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) rasterizes every page to JPEG. On a PDF made of photos or scanned images, that works extremely well. A 913.4 KB image deck shrank to 175.6 KB at the highest compression level, a reduction of 80.8%. At Medium quality it came down to 315.3 KB (65.5% smaller). Real, useful numbers.

The same approach makes text PDFs larger. A 23.5 KB text-only document grew to 1.4 MB after compression, because vector text is compact and a JPEG photograph of that text is not. Don't compress invoices, contracts, slides with lots of text, or anything where the content is mostly typed characters. If a text PDF is too large for email, the cause is usually an embedded font or an oversized logo, and compression won't fix it.

One more rule: running the same file through compression twice doesn't help. A second pass at Medium nearly doubled the size (315.3 KB became 598.9 KB). If Medium isn't small enough, go back to the original and try High.

## Signing PDFs and filling forms without Adobe Acrobat

The [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) lets you draw a signature and place it anywhere on the page. The [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) detects existing form fields and lets you type into them. Both tools run in your browser with no upload and no account. There's no watermark on the downloaded file.

If the sender placed form fields in the document, Fill PDF Form handles it. If you just need a handwritten signature in a specific spot on a page that has no fields, Sign PDF is the right tool.

## Protecting PDFs without Adobe Acrobat

The [Protect PDF tool](/protect-pdf) encrypts your file with [AES-256](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Encryption_Standard) and a password you set, entirely in your browser. The [Unlock PDF tool](/unlock-pdf) removes a known password from a PDF you own.

Both tools run client-side. Your file is never sent to a server.

Encryption adds almost no size. A 23.5 KB text file became 24.6 KB after protection, an overhead of about 1 KB. On a 913 KB image file, the overhead was 1 KB as well. Encrypting a PDF won't change whether it fits inside an email attachment limit.

## Where Acrobat still has the edge

Free browser tools handle most of what Acrobat does. A few areas are harder to replace:

**OCR.** Turning a scanned image into searchable, selectable text requires heavy computation. Most in-browser tools don't offer it. If you regularly receive scanned documents and need to search or copy text from them, Acrobat's OCR is a real advantage that browser tools don't match.

**Advanced text editing.** Acrobat Pro lets you edit a PDF's text in a way that reflows the surrounding paragraph. In-browser editors let you modify text on the page, but layout reflow typically works differently. For light corrections, browser tools are fine. For heavy editing, converting back to the source format and re-exporting is usually cleaner.

**Certified e-signatures.** For legally binding signatures with timestamps and certification chains, a purpose-built e-signature service is more appropriate than a general PDF tool.

For the everyday tasks most people actually do, the free tools above cover the ground without a subscription.
