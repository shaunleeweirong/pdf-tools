Merging PDF files stored in OneDrive is free if you're willing to do a quick download-merge-upload cycle. OneDrive does have a built-in PDF combine option, but it sits behind a paid Microsoft 365 subscription. If your account is the free tier, a browser-based tool gets the job done without any cost or sign-up.

## Does OneDrive have a built-in PDF merger?

Yes, but not for free accounts. Microsoft added [PDF merge and extract features to OneDrive and SharePoint](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/onedriveblog/feature-deep-dive-using-pdfs-in-onedrive-and-our-journey-thus-far/4403628) for subscribers with a Microsoft 365 Basic, Personal, or Family plan. On a free OneDrive account (the 5 GB tier), the merge option either doesn't appear or is greyed out.

When the paid version works, it's convenient: open a PDF stored in OneDrive on the web, click the "..." menu, select "Merge," pick the other files from your OneDrive storage, set the order, and save a combined PDF back to your folder. You never leave the browser tab.

But if you don't have an M365 subscription, or you'd rather not pay for PDF merging alone, the download-merge-upload method below is just as fast.

## How to merge PDF files from OneDrive (step by step)

This works on any computer, any browser. No account needed beyond what you already use for OneDrive.

1. **Open OneDrive** in your browser and find the PDFs you want to combine.
2. **Download each file.** Click the three-dot menu next to a PDF and choose "Download." Repeat for each file you need.
3. **Go to [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf)** and drag all the downloaded files into the upload area. Drag the page thumbnails to set the order.
4. **Click Merge.** The tool combines the files into a single PDF.
5. **Download the merged file** to your computer.
6. **Upload it back to OneDrive.** Drag the file into your OneDrive browser window, or click "Upload" > "Files."

For most files, the whole process takes two to three minutes.

**About file size:** merging won't push you over a storage limit. In our testing, three files totalling 971.9 KB came out as a 970.5 KB merged PDF. The result runs slightly smaller than the sum of the parts, because shared PDF structure gets written once. You won't gain a noticeable size penalty.

**About order:** if you need the pages from one file to appear before another, arrange the file cards in the tool before you click Merge. You can also rearrange or remove individual pages inside the tool if you want to leave certain pages out of the combined file.

## How to merge OneDrive PDFs on your phone

The same workflow runs on iPhone and Android with the mobile browser.

1. Open the **OneDrive app** on your phone and find the PDFs.
2. Tap the three-dot menu next to each file and tap "Download." On iPhone, the file saves to the Files app. On Android, it goes to your Downloads folder.
3. Open Safari or Chrome on your phone and visit [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf).
4. Tap the upload area, choose your downloaded PDFs from the Files app or Downloads folder, and arrange them in order.
5. Tap Merge, then download the result.
6. Re-open the OneDrive app, tap the "+" button, and upload the merged file.

The one friction on mobile is finding the downloaded files. On iPhone, look under "On My iPhone" > "Downloads" in the Files app. On Android, check the Downloads folder through your file manager. If a file doesn't appear, it may have gone to a different location depending on your browser's download settings.

## What to do about form fields

If any of your PDFs has interactive form fields (fillable text boxes, checkboxes, signature fields), those fields won't survive a merge. The page content stays intact, but the fields themselves disappear. A filled-in form will look blank in the merged document.

The fix is to [flatten the form first](/flatten-pdf). Flattening bakes the current field values permanently into the page as static text. After flattening, the values won't be lost when you merge. Run each filled form through the flatten tool before combining them.

This matters most on job applications, contracts, and government forms where someone has already typed their responses. Don't merge directly from the original interactive version.

## After merging: keeping OneDrive tidy

Once the merged PDF is back in OneDrive, a couple of quick steps make it easier to find later:

- **Rename it right away.** Click the three-dot menu on the file and choose "Rename." A name like "Application-bundle-Sept.pdf" is easier to locate than "merged-output.pdf."
- **Move it to the correct folder.** Drag it or use "Move to" so it lives where the rest of the related files are.
- **Delete the originals if you no longer need them.** The merged file contains all the pages, so you can remove the separate files to free up storage space.

If the merged file still ends up too large to attach to an email (most providers cap attachments at around 25 MB), and the file is image-heavy such as a scanned document or a photo deck, running it through [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) can reduce the size. Skip compression if the content is mostly text (reports, contracts, invoices) because the tool rasterizes each page and makes text files larger, not smaller.

## Does the order of files in OneDrive matter?

No. The order of PDFs in your OneDrive folder has nothing to do with how they'll appear in the merged file. When you drop multiple files into the [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool, you set the order yourself by dragging the thumbnails. You can put any file first, last, or anywhere in between, regardless of how they're arranged in OneDrive.

If you use OneDrive's native merge (with an M365 subscription), you can also drag and drop the file tiles to rearrange them before saving the result.
