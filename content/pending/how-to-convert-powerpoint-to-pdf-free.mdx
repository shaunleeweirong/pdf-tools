You can convert a PowerPoint presentation to PDF for free without buying any software. PowerPoint itself has a built-in Save As PDF option, and if you don't own PowerPoint, Google Slides or LibreOffice both handle the conversion at no cost.

Here are four methods, from fastest to most flexible.

## Method 1: Save as PDF directly in PowerPoint

This is the most reliable option if you have [Microsoft PowerPoint](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/save-your-presentation-as-a-pdf-file-1a3fa2b5-6bce-4e76-8946-2b80b5c34a5c) installed. It preserves fonts, layout, and image quality without uploading anything.

**On Windows:**

1. Open your presentation.
2. Click File > Save As.
3. In the "Save as type" dropdown, choose PDF.
4. Click Save.

Alternatively, use File > Export > Create PDF/XPS for the same result with a few extra options (quality settings, include or exclude speaker notes).

**On Mac:**

1. Open your presentation.
2. Go to File > Export.
3. Choose PDF from the file format menu.
4. Click Export.

Or use File > Print, then click the PDF button in the bottom-left corner and choose "Save as PDF". Both routes produce the same output.

**One thing to know about animations:** PDF is a static format. Slide transitions and animation effects don't carry over. Each slide becomes a still image of whatever the slide looks like at its final state. If your deck uses animation to reveal bullet points one at a time, each bullet will already be visible in the PDF. Duplicate slides before exporting if you need the animated sequence preserved step by step.

## Method 2: Use Google Slides (no software needed)

This method works on any computer, including Chromebooks, and only requires a Google account.

1. Go to [Google Drive](https://drive.google.com) and click New > File upload.
2. Upload your .pptx file.
3. Right-click the file and choose "Open with > Google Slides".
4. In Google Slides, go to File > Download > PDF Document (.pdf).

The file downloads straight to your computer. Google does a solid job rendering fonts and layouts, though it may handle some complex animations or custom fonts differently from the desktop app. If the result looks off, compare it with Method 1.

## Method 3: Use LibreOffice Impress (free, offline)

[LibreOffice](https://www.libreoffice.org/) is a free, open-source office suite that runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux. It opens .pptx files and exports them as PDF without an internet connection.

1. Download and install LibreOffice if you don't have it.
2. Open your .pptx file with LibreOffice Impress.
3. Go to File > Export as PDF.
4. Choose your quality settings and click Export.

The advantage over online tools is privacy: your file never leaves your machine. The disadvantage is that LibreOffice handles very complex PowerPoint formatting (custom themes, embedded 3D objects) less faithfully than PowerPoint itself.

## Method 4: Print to PDF from your browser (last resort)

Most operating systems include a virtual PDF printer. Open the .pptx in Google Slides or Office for the web, then use File > Print and choose "Save as PDF" or "Microsoft Print to PDF" as the printer. This works in a pinch, but the output quality and layout fidelity are lower than the dedicated export options above.

## How to include speaker notes in the PDF

A standard conversion drops your speaker notes. To keep them:

**In PowerPoint on Windows:** Go to File > Print. Under "Settings", change "Full Page Slides" to "Notes Pages". Then print to PDF (choose "Microsoft Print to PDF" as the printer).

**In PowerPoint on Mac:** File > Print, then expand the layout picker (it says "Show Details" if collapsed) and choose "Notes". Save as PDF from the PDF button.

**In Google Slides:** File > Print settings and preview, then switch from "1 slide without notes" to "1 slide with notes". Download as PDF.

Each page in the output shows a thumbnail of the slide at the top and the note text below it, which is handy for handouts.

## Why is my PDF larger than the original .pptx?

PowerPoint packs fonts, images, and slide data efficiently in its own format. When it exports to [PDF (ISO 32000)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF), it embeds fonts fully and often upsamples images to print resolution (around 150 to 220 DPI by default). A 5 MB .pptx that mainly contains text can produce a 10 to 15 MB PDF.

To reduce the size before exporting, go to File > Options > Advanced > Image Size and Quality in PowerPoint on Windows, and set the default resolution to 96 or 150 PPI instead of the higher default. Then export again.

## How to reduce the PDF size after converting

If your presentation is image-heavy (photos, diagrams, background images), our [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) can cut the file size substantially. It works by rasterizing each page to JPEG, which is exactly what image-heavy presentations respond well to.

One important caveat: the compressor rasterizes everything, including text. For presentations that are mostly text with simple graphics, compression will make the file larger, not smaller. Based on our measurements, a 6-page image deck compressed to 315.3 KB from 913.4 KB at Medium quality (a 65.5% reduction), but a 20-page text report grew from 23.5 KB to over 1.4 MB after the same process. Use the compressor only when your slides contain real images.

If you're getting a too-large-to-email error after converting a text-heavy presentation, the better fix is to lower the export resolution in PowerPoint's settings before converting (as described above), or split the presentation into smaller chunks and share parts separately.
