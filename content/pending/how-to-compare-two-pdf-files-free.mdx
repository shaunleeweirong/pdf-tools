If you need to compare two PDF files to spot what changed between versions, you don't need Adobe Acrobat or any desktop software. Browser-based tools can highlight the differences in seconds, and the better ones never upload your files to a server.

This guide covers when visual comparison is all you need, when text comparison gives you more, and how to handle the cases that trip most people up.

## What PDF comparison actually does

A PDF comparison tool shows you the differences between two documents. Depending on the tool, it does this in one of two ways:

**Visual comparison** renders both pages as images and checks them pixel by pixel. Any part of the page that looks different gets highlighted. This works with any PDF, including scans, design files, and image-heavy documents.

**Text comparison** extracts the text content from both PDFs and runs a word-by-word diff. It shows exactly which sentences were added, removed, or reworded. This doesn't work on scanned pages (no text to extract), but gives clearer results for typed documents.

Knowing which type fits your situation saves a lot of time.

## When visual comparison is the right choice

Visual comparison is the better option when:
- The PDF contains scanned pages or images with no text layer
- You want to confirm a signature page, stamp, or visual element wasn't changed
- You're comparing forms, layouts, or design files where position matters
- You need a fast first check before deciding whether a closer look is worth it

The result looks like the original page with changed areas highlighted in red and unchanged areas dimmed. You see at a glance whether differences are major (a whole paragraph moved) or minor (a date changed).

## When text comparison gives you more

If you're reviewing a contract revision, a legal document, or any typed report where exact wording matters, a text diff is more useful. It tells you which clauses were reworded, which numbers changed, and what was added or deleted, without requiring you to stare at two pages side by side.

The limit: text comparison only works on PDFs with a real text layer. A scanned document is just an image of paper, and text extraction will either fail on it or return nothing. If you have scans, visual comparison is your only free option short of running [OCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_character_recognition) first to add a text layer.

The [Compare PDF tool](/compare-pdf) does a visual pixel comparison of the first pages of two PDFs. It runs entirely in your browser using PDF.js and canvas rendering, so your files never leave your device.

**Steps:**
1. Open the [Compare PDF tool](/compare-pdf).

Because the files stay on your device, this is a good fit for confidential documents such as contracts, financial statements, and medical records, where you'd rather not send files to a third-party server.

**One thing to know:** the tool compares the first page of each PDF. If you need to check a page deeper in the document, there's a workaround in the next section.

## What if the PDFs are password-protected?

Comparison tools can't read an encrypted PDF. You'll need to remove the password first. If you know the password, the [Unlock PDF tool](/unlock-pdf) removes it in your browser and returns a readable file. If you don't know the password, you can't unlock it. That's what encryption is for.

## Scanned PDFs

Scanned pages are photographs of paper. There's no underlying text, so text comparison tools return no results or throw an error. Visual comparison handles scans well because it compares image data, not text content.

One thing worth knowing: two scans of the same page made minutes apart on the same scanner can look slightly different due to alignment shifts, compression artifacts, or lighting changes. Small visual differences on scans may just be scan noise, not actual content changes.

## Tools for full-document text comparison

Our tool covers first-page visual comparison. For a full document text diff across all pages, two free options work well:

- [Draftable](https://www.draftable.com/compare) compares entire documents with word-level redlining, no sign-up needed
- [PDF24 Compare](https://tools.pdf24.org/en/compare-pdf) handles multi-page PDFs with a free online interface

Both of these upload your files to their servers, which is fine for non-sensitive documents. For anything confidential, the browser-based approach keeps files off the server entirely.

## What PDF comparison doesn't do

A few things worth knowing before you compare:

**It doesn't change your files.** The comparison tool reads both PDFs to produce the highlighted result but doesn't modify either document. Both originals stay exactly as they were.

**It doesn't track changes like Word does.** Word's "track changes" is a live editing feature built into the document format itself. PDFs don't work that way. If someone edited a PDF without leaving a revision history, comparison is the only way to find what changed.

**It doesn't catch invisible changes.** Metadata edits, changes to form field values, or embedded content that doesn't affect the rendered page won't show up in a visual comparison. A visual diff catches visual alterations, not structural or metadata changes.
