To reduce the number of pages in a PDF, you have three main options: delete the pages you don't need, extract only the ones you do, or split the file into smaller pieces. All three are free, run in your browser without any upload, and don't need Adobe Acrobat. There's also a separate approach if your goal is printing on fewer sheets rather than editing the document itself.

## What does "reduce the number of pages" actually mean?

Before picking a method, it helps to be clear about which problem you're solving.

**Fewer pages in the document:** You want the PDF file to contain fewer pages. A 40-page report should become a 12-page summary. An application pack with 10 pages should become a 3-page submission. This permanently changes the file.

**Fewer sheets of paper when printing:** The document itself stays the same length, but you want it to use less paper. Printing two PDF pages per sheet cuts your paper use in half. Nothing in the file changes.

Both are reasonable goals. The methods are different, so knowing which one you need saves time.

## How to delete pages from a PDF free

Deleting pages is the most direct approach. You mark the pages you want gone, and the tool removes them from the file.

1. Open the [Delete Pages tool](/delete-pages).
2. Drop in your PDF.
3. Click each page thumbnail you want to remove. Selected pages are highlighted.
4. Click **Delete selected pages** and download your new file.

The result is a PDF containing only the pages you kept. Your original file isn't changed because the tool processes everything locally in your browser and hands you a fresh download.

This works best when you have a long document and only need to cut a handful of pages. If you'd rather visually reorder pages before deleting, try the [Organize PDF tool](/organize-pdf) instead. It gives you a drag-and-drop view of all pages so you can rearrange and remove them at the same time.

## How to keep only the pages you need

Extracting pages is the reverse of deleting: instead of marking what to remove, you mark what to keep. For a 30-page document where you only need pages 4, 7, and 12 through 18, extracting is faster than manually deleting the 20-odd pages you don't want.

1. Open the [Extract Pages tool](/extract-pages).
2. Upload your PDF.
3. Enter the page numbers or ranges to keep (for example: 4, 7, 12-18).
4. Download your shorter PDF.

Extraction is also useful when you need to give someone a partial version of a sensitive document. You hand them only the relevant pages without exposing anything else.

## How to split a PDF into several smaller files

Sometimes the goal isn't one shorter document but several. A 60-page manual might need to become six 10-page chapters. A batch of scanned forms might need to be separated into individual records, one file per person.

The [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) handles this. You choose where to cut: by page range, by a fixed interval (every N pages), or by extracting every page as its own separate file.

1. Open the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf).
2. Upload your PDF.
3. Set your split points or interval.
4. Download the resulting files individually or as a single ZIP.

Splitting also solves email size problems without compressing the content. If a PDF is too large to send in one email, splitting it into two parts often gets both under the attachment limit without touching image quality.

## How to print a PDF on fewer sheets of paper

If your goal is saving paper rather than editing the file, [N-up printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-up) is the right approach. An N-up layout places two or four PDF pages side by side on each printed sheet. Printing 2-up cuts paper use in half; 4-up cuts it to a quarter.

Many printers offer this natively. Check your print dialog for a "Pages per sheet" or "Multiple pages" option. If you want a compacted layout you can share digitally, or if your printer doesn't support it, you can generate the N-up PDF in advance.

1. Open the [Pages Per Sheet tool](/n-up-pdf).
2. Upload your PDF.
3. Choose 2-up or 4-up.
4. Download and print, or share the result.

Every word and image in the document stays intact. The pages are just arranged more compactly on each sheet.

One tool that won't help here: the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf). Compression reduces file size on disk, but it doesn't remove pages. A compressed 30-page PDF is still 30 pages. Use compression after you've already cut the page count, not as a substitute for cutting it.
