## How to add a watermark to a PDF on Android

You can add a watermark to a PDF on Android for free, right in your phone's browser, without installing any app. Open the [Watermark PDF tool](/watermark-pdf) in Chrome, upload your file, type the text you want stamped on every page, and download the result.

Here are the steps in full:

1. Open Chrome (or any modern Android browser) on your phone or tablet.
2. Go to the [Watermark PDF tool](/watermark-pdf).
3. Tap **Choose file** and pick the PDF from your Downloads folder, Google Drive, or wherever it is saved.
4. Type the watermark text in the input field. Common choices are "CONFIDENTIAL", "DRAFT", or your name.
5. Tap the button to apply the watermark and wait a moment while the tool processes your file.
6. Tap **Download** to save the watermarked PDF back to your device.

The whole process takes under a minute for most PDFs. You do not need to create an account or give an email address.

## What watermark text should I use?

The text you use depends on why you are watermarking the document in the first place.

**CONFIDENTIAL** is the most common choice for business documents. It signals to anyone who receives the file that the contents should not be shared further. Legal contracts, internal reports, and personnel records are all typical candidates.

**DRAFT** is useful when you are sending a document for review before it is finalized. Stamping every page makes it obvious that this is not the signed or approved version, which prevents confusion if someone files or forwards the file later.

**DO NOT COPY** or **SAMPLE** works well when you are sharing work as a proof or a preview, for example a designer sending a mock-up to a client before payment clears.

**Your name or company name** is common when sharing creative work like writing samples, photographs saved as PDFs, or design portfolios. It marks the work as yours without blocking the recipient from reading it.

Some people also use a URL or a short copyright notice. The tool lets you type whatever you want, so there are no restrictions on the text.

## What devices and browsers work?

The tool works on any Android phone or tablet running a modern browser. Chrome is the most widely tested, but Firefox for Android, Samsung Internet, and Microsoft Edge for Android all work fine.

You do not need a specific Android version. If your browser can open other web tools and handle file uploads, it will handle watermarking too. Older mid-range devices may take a few extra seconds on large PDFs, but the process completes without issue.

The same tool works on iPhone and iPad as well, so you can share the link with someone on iOS and they will get the same result.

## Does my file stay private?

Yes. The [Watermark PDF tool](/watermark-pdf) processes your file entirely in your browser using client-side code. Your PDF is never uploaded to a server, which means no one else can access it, and nothing is logged or stored on Anthropic's end.

This matters more than it might seem. Many online PDF tools upload your file to a remote server for processing. For personal documents, that might be fine. For contracts, HR files, medical records, or anything marked confidential, you probably do not want the file to leave your device at all.

The [PDF format is defined by ISO standard 32000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF), and watermarks in PDFs are stored as content drawn on each page rather than as a separate removable layer. This is why any PDF reader on any device shows the watermark correctly.

## Watermarking vs. password protection

A watermark is visible text stamped on the page. It tells the reader something ("this is a draft", "this is confidential") but it does not stop them from opening, copying, or editing the document.

If you need to control access to the file, use [password protection](/protect-pdf) instead. That encrypts the PDF so it cannot be opened without the right password. The [AES-256 encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Encryption_Standard) used by the Protect PDF tool is the same standard used to protect banking and government data.

The two features serve different goals. A watermark labels a document. A password restricts who can open it. For sensitive files where you both want to mark the content and limit access, you can watermark it first and then password-protect the result. The two steps work together.

## Can I remove a watermark I added?

If you still have the original PDF, that is your cleanest option. Re-export from the source or re-watermark the original with corrected text.

If you do not have the original, removing a stamped watermark is difficult. Because [PDF watermarks are drawn directly into the page content](https://www.adobe.com/acrobat/hub/how-to/add-watermark-to-pdf.html) rather than stored in a separate layer, there is no universal "remove watermark" command that works reliably across all PDFs. Some tools can attempt it by redacting those areas, but the results vary depending on the PDF.

The practical lesson: keep your original files. A watermarked PDF is meant to be a distributed copy, not your working copy.

There is no meaningful difference in the result. The tool produces the same output whether you run it on a phone, a tablet, or a laptop. The step-by-step process is slightly different on a phone because you are tapping instead of clicking, and the file picker looks different, but the watermarked PDF you get back is identical.

If you work across multiple devices, you can start the process on your phone and the downloaded PDF will open on any desktop reader without issue. The watermark is part of the PDF itself.
