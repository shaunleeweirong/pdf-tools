Merging PDFs on a Chromebook is straightforward, your browser is the app. Since Chromebooks are built around Chrome, a browser-based [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) works perfectly and requires no download, no account, and no subscription. Here's how to get it done.

## The quickest way: use a browser-based Merge PDF tool

Chromebook doesn't ship with a built-in PDF merger the way macOS ships with Preview. But that's fine, a browser tool runs natively on Chromebook and is just as fast.

1. Open Chrome and go to the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf).
2. Click **Select files** or drag your PDFs into the tool.
3. Reorder the files by dragging if you need a different sequence.
4. Click **Merge** and download the combined PDF.

The whole process takes under a minute. Because the merging happens locally in your browser, your files never get uploaded to any server, they stay on your Chromebook.

This works on every Chromebook running Chrome 90 or later (which is essentially all of them at this point). No Android apps, no Linux environment, no extensions needed.

## Can I merge PDFs on Chromebook using Google Drive?

Google Drive doesn't have a built-in PDF merge feature, but there are two ways to combine PDFs through the Google ecosystem.

**Option 1: Google Workspace Marketplace add-on**

1. Open [Google Drive](https://drive.google.com) and click the grid icon (top right) > **More apps**.
2. Search for "Merge PDF" in the Workspace Marketplace and install a free add-on.
3. Select your PDF files in Drive, open them with the add-on, and merge.

This approach keeps everything in Drive and is useful if the PDFs you need to combine are already stored there. The trade-off: most add-ons require you to grant access to your Drive, and some have file size or daily merge limits.

**Option 2: Download from Drive, merge in browser, re-upload**

1. Download the PDFs from Drive to your Chromebook (Files app > Downloads).
2. Merge them with the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) in Chrome.
3. Upload the combined file back to Drive.

This takes an extra step but gives you full control and keeps your data private.

## Can I merge PDFs on Chromebook without uploading them anywhere?

Yes. A client-side browser tool processes everything in your browser tab, the PDF files are read from your local storage and the merged output is written back to your Downloads folder, without any data crossing the network. Our [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) works this way.

If you're dealing with sensitive documents (medical records, contracts, financial files), this matters: your files are never on someone else's server.

The work is done by [pdf-lib](https://pdf-lib.js.org/), an open-source JavaScript library, running inside the tab. It is fast and it does not inflate the result: merging three of our test files totalling 971.9 KB produced a 970.5 KB output in around 20 milliseconds, slightly under the sum of the inputs because shared structure is written once.

## What if the merged file is too big?

A Chromebook handles large merges fine, but the destination might not. If the combined PDF is over an email or upload limit, you have two options.

If the pages are **scans or photos**, run the result through the [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf). If the pages are **typed documents**, do not: that tool works by converting pages to images, which makes a text PDF considerably larger. Send fewer pages instead.

## What about Chrome extensions for merging PDFs?

Extensions like "Merge PDF" in the Chrome Web Store work fine on Chromebook. Some run fully offline after installation. The main advantage over a browser tool is that you don't need to navigate to a URL each time, the extension is always a click away in the toolbar.

The downside is that extensions need to be explicitly trusted with access to your files, and you'll want to read their privacy policies. A tool that runs from a known URL is often the simpler choice.

## How do I combine specific pages from different PDFs on Chromebook?

The basic merge combines whole files. If you want to pull individual pages from multiple PDFs and assemble them into one document:

1. Use the browser [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) to combine the relevant complete PDFs first.
2. Then open the result in Chrome's built-in PDF viewer and check the output.

For more granular page selection (choose page 1 from file A, pages 3-5 from file B), look for a browser-based PDF organizer tool that lets you reorder and delete pages before downloading.
