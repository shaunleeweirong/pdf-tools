You can merge PDFs on Mac for free without installing anything — macOS ships with two built-in tools that handle it in seconds. Here are three ways to combine PDF files on a Mac, from fastest to most flexible.

## Method 1: Finder Quick Action (fastest, 2 clicks)

If you just need to smash several PDFs into one file in the same order they sit in Finder, this is the quickest route.

1. Open Finder and navigate to your PDF files.
2. Hold **Command** and click each file to select them — select them in the order you want them to appear in the merged result.
3. Control-click (right-click) the selection and choose **Quick Actions > Create PDF**.
4. macOS creates a new combined PDF in the same folder, named after the first file you selected.

That's it — no opening an app, no export step.

**If "Create PDF" doesn't appear in your Quick Actions menu:** Open System Settings > Privacy & Security > Extensions > Finder, and tick the box next to "Create PDF."

This method is best when you want to combine complete PDFs. If you need to pick specific pages or reorder individual pages, use Preview instead.

## Method 2: Preview App (most control over pages)

Preview is macOS's built-in PDF viewer, and it doubles as a surprisingly capable PDF editor. Use it when you want to select individual pages from multiple files rather than merging whole documents.

1. Open your first PDF in Preview (double-click it).
2. Go to **View > Thumbnails** to open the page thumbnail sidebar.
3. To add an entire second PDF: drag the PDF file from Finder directly into the thumbnail sidebar, dropping it where you want it inserted.
4. To add only specific pages from a second PDF: open that PDF in a separate Preview window, show its thumbnails, then drag individual page thumbnails across into the first document's sidebar.
5. Once the pages are in the order you want, go to **File > Export as PDF** and save the new file.

**Important:** Use **Export as PDF**, not Save. If you hit Save or close the window, Preview auto-saves changes to the original file — which can overwrite it. Export creates a brand new file and leaves your originals untouched.

If you want to keep your originals safe before you start, go to **File > Duplicate** on each PDF first to work from copies.

## Method 3: Merge PDF in your browser (no install, any Mac)

If you're on a Mac you don't own, or you'd prefer not to touch Preview at all, a browser-based [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool gets the job done in seconds — for free, with no account required.

1. Open the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) in Safari or Chrome.
2. Drag your PDF files into the tool, or click to select them from your Mac.
3. Drag to reorder them if needed.
4. Click **Merge** and download the combined PDF.

Because this tool processes everything locally in your browser, your files never leave your Mac. You get the same result as Preview, without touching the app.

## Does merging PDFs in Preview reduce quality?

No. Preview's Export as PDF writes the pages out at their original resolution — it doesn't re-compress images or degrade text. The merged file is visually identical to the originals, just combined.

## Can I merge more than two PDFs at once on Mac?

Yes, with all three methods. In Finder Quick Action, select as many files as you like before right-clicking. In Preview, you can drag multiple PDFs into the thumbnail sidebar one at a time or select multiple page thumbnails with Command-click and drag them in bulk. The browser tool accepts multiple files at once too.

## Will merging PDFs on Mac make the file bigger?

Usually yes — a merged PDF is roughly the sum of the originals. If the combined file is too large, run it through the [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) tool afterward to bring the size down without noticeable quality loss.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Mac come with a free PDF merger?

Yes. macOS includes Preview (which can merge PDFs page by page) and a Finder Quick Action called "Create PDF" that combines whole files in two clicks. Neither requires Acrobat or any paid software.

### How do I merge PDFs on Mac without Acrobat?

Use Preview or the Finder Quick Action — both are free and built into every Mac. Alternatively, use a browser-based [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) that requires no download at all.

### Why can't I see "Create PDF" in my Finder Quick Actions?

The feature exists on macOS Mojave (10.14) and later but can be disabled. Go to System Settings > Privacy & Security > Extensions > Finder, and enable "Create PDF." On older macOS versions the feature isn't available — use Preview instead.

### Is it safe to use an online tool to merge PDFs on Mac?

It depends on the tool. Tools that run entirely in your browser (client-side processing) never upload your files to a server — your documents stay on your Mac. Look for tools that say "processed in your browser" or "files never leave your device." Our [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) tool works this way.

### Can I merge a PDF and a Word document on Mac?

Not directly — you'll need to convert the Word document to PDF first. On Mac, open the Word file, go to File > Print, and click the PDF button in the bottom-left to save it as a PDF. Then merge the two PDFs using any of the methods above.

### How do I reorder pages after merging on Mac?

Open the merged PDF in Preview, go to View > Thumbnails, and drag the page thumbnails into the order you want. Then use File > Export as PDF to save the reordered file.
