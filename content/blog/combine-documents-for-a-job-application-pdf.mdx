Combining documents for a job application PDF means merging your resume, cover letter, and any supporting files, references, certifications, writing samples, into one file you can submit in a single upload. Many job portals accept only one attachment, and a combined PDF is the cleanest way to make all your materials fit.

## When should you combine documents for a job application?

Read the application instructions before combining anything. The answer is usually clear:

**Combine when:**
- The portal has only one upload slot
- The posting says "attach all documents as a single PDF" or "submit one file"
- You're emailing your application and want to keep it to one attachment

**Keep them separate when:**
- The portal has separate upload fields for "Resume" and "Cover Letter", use the dedicated fields
- The posting doesn't specify, separate files are the professional default, since applicant tracking systems (ATS) parse each document on its own
- You're submitting to a large company where recruiters screen many applications; a clearly labeled, standalone resume file is faster to open than a multi-page combined document

When the instructions are silent, separate files are safer. A combined PDF sent to a portal with separate fields won't disqualify you, but it's unnecessary friction.

## What order should the documents go in?

The standard order for a combined job application PDF:

1. **Cover letter**, sets context before the recruiter sees your resume
2. **Resume / CV**, the main document
3. **References**, a single page listing three to five contacts with name, title, company, phone, and email
4. **Supporting documents**, certifications, transcripts, writing samples, or portfolio pages (if requested)

Cover letter first: it introduces you. Resume second: it backs up the introduction. Everything else follows.

## How to combine your documents into one PDF

You can merge everything in your browser in about 30 seconds with the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf), no account, no software, no upload to any server:

1. **Open the Merge PDF tool.**
2. **Click "Add files"** and select your cover letter, resume, and any other documents. All files must already be in PDF format (see the FAQ if yours are in Word or Google Docs).
3. **Drag to reorder** if needed, cover letter at the top, resume second, references and supporting documents after.
4. **Click "Merge"**, the combined PDF downloads to your device.

If the pages end up in the wrong sequence after merging, the [Organize PDF tool](/organize-pdf) lets you drag individual pages into the right order before downloading.

**If any of your documents is a filled-in PDF form, flatten it first.** Merging combines pages, but interactive form fields live at the document level and do not survive. We tested this on our own merge tool: a PDF with a text field reported that field before merging and none afterwards. Application packs often include a filled form, so this is a real risk. Run it through the [Flatten PDF tool](/flatten-pdf) first, which paints your answers permanently into the page, then merge.

## How to make the combined PDF look professional

A few things to check before you submit:

**Consistent formatting.** Your cover letter header should match your resume header, same font, same name and contact details at the top. A recruiter who sees the same layout across pages knows the documents belong together. Mismatched fonts or contact details signal a rushed application.

**No blank pages.** Some word processors add a blank page at the end of a document on export. Check the page count before merging: a 1-page cover letter, 1-page resume, and 1-page reference list should produce exactly 3 pages. Open your PDFs individually before merging and delete any trailing blank pages.

**File name.** Name the combined file something specific: `FirstName-LastName-Application.pdf` or `FirstName-LastName-RoleName.pdf`. A file named "Resume.pdf" or "Document1.pdf" is harder for recruiters to identify in a folder full of submissions.

**File size.** Most portals accept up to 5 MB, and a combined application of typed documents is typically well under 500 KB, so this rarely bites. Merging itself adds nothing: we measured a merge of three files totalling 971.9 KB producing a 970.5 KB result, marginally under the sum of its parts.

If scanned certificates or portfolio pages push you over a limit, resist the urge to run the whole thing through a compressor. In-browser compression works by turning each page into an image, which would make your typed resume larger and, worse, unreadable to the applicant tracking system, since there would no longer be any text to extract. Instead, drop the heavy pages out with the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) and link to the portfolio separately, or re-export the scans at a lower resolution before merging.

## Does a combined PDF hurt your ATS score?

This is a common concern. The short answer: probably not, if your resume is formatted cleanly.

Most modern ATS platforms (Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, iCIMS) parse the text of whatever file you upload. A cover letter on page 1 doesn't prevent the system from extracting resume content from pages 2-3. What actually causes ATS parsing failures is complex resume formatting: multi-column layouts, tables, graphics, text boxes, and headers/footers all interfere with text extraction.

Keep your resume text simple and clean, and a combined PDF should parse correctly. If you're uncertain about a specific system, use separate files where the portal allows it, that's always the safest approach.

## What about portfolios and work samples?

If the application requests a portfolio or writing samples, check whether they want them in the same file or as a separate attachment.

For design, writing, and marketing roles, a separate portfolio PDF is often preferred, it keeps the application file short and lets the recruiter open the portfolio when they're ready to review work, rather than scrolling through it while trying to screen your resume.

If you're adding portfolio pages to the application file, put them last (after references) and add a simple divider, a page with just "Work Samples" or "Portfolio" as a heading so the recruiter knows where the application ends and the work begins.

## How to convert Word or Google Docs files to PDF first

Before merging, every document needs to be in PDF format.

**Microsoft Word:** File → Save As → choose PDF from the format dropdown. [Microsoft's own guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/save-or-convert-to-pdf-or-xps-in-office-desktop-apps-d85416c5-7d77-4fd6-a216-6f4bf7c7c110) covers the options if your version looks different.

**Google Docs:** File → Download → PDF Document (.pdf).

**LibreOffice:** File → Export As → Export as PDF.

Once all files are PDFs, drop them into the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) and combine.
