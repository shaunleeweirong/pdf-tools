You can sign a PDF without printing by using a free browser-based tool, the apps already on your Mac or iPhone, or Adobe Acrobat Reader on Windows. No printer, no scanner, no paid software.

The quickest path: open the [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf), upload your document, draw or type your signature, and download the signed file. Everything runs in your browser, so the file never leaves your device.

## Why do people still print PDFs just to sign them?

Habit, mostly. The print-sign-scan loop dates back to when that was the only option. The other reason is that most people don't know their device already has free signing built in. Every major platform has for years, and browser tools cover whatever gaps remain.

## How to sign a PDF in your browser (no account, no upload)

The [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) processes your document entirely inside the browser tab. Nothing gets sent to a server.

1. Open the [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf).
2. Click **Choose file** and pick your PDF.
3. Choose how to create your signature: draw with your mouse or trackpad, type your name, or upload a photo of your handwritten signature.
4. Drag the signature to the right spot on the page.
5. Click **Download** to save the signed PDF.

This works on any operating system and any device with a modern browser. It's also the best option for documents with sensitive personal information, since nothing leaves your machine.

## How to sign a PDF on Mac (using Preview)

macOS comes with [Preview](https://support.apple.com/guide/preview/fill-out-and-sign-pdf-forms-prvw35725/mac), which has had built-in signature capture since OS X Mountain Lion. No extra app to install.

1. Open your PDF in Preview (double-click the file, or right-click and choose **Open with > Preview**).
2. Click the **Show Markup Toolbar** button (the pen icon near the top right of the window).
3. Click the **Sign** button (it looks like a handwritten signature).
4. Pick how to capture your signature:
   - **Trackpad:** sign your name with your finger.
   - **Camera:** hold a signed piece of white paper in front of your webcam.
   - **iPhone:** draw on your iPhone screen, and it syncs into Preview automatically via Continuity.
5. Your saved signature appears in the Sign menu from now on. Click it to insert it, then drag it to position.
6. Save with **Cmd+S**.

Preview saves your signature for future documents, so you only go through setup once.

## How to sign a PDF on Windows

**Adobe Acrobat Reader (free):**

1. Open your PDF in Adobe Acrobat Reader.
2. Go to **Tools > Fill & Sign**, or click **Fill & Sign** in the right panel.
3. Click **Sign yourself**, then **Add Signature**.
4. Type your name, draw it with the mouse, or upload an image of your signature.
5. Click **Apply**, place the signature on the page, then save.

**Microsoft Edge (no extra software):**

Edge opens PDFs natively and has a **Draw** tool in the toolbar. Click the pencil icon, select a pen, and draw directly on the page. This is freeform ink rather than a formatted signature box, so it works best for quick initialing or informal sign-offs.

## How to sign a PDF on iPhone or iPad

Signing is built into iOS through the Markup tool. No app download needed.

1. Open the PDF in the Files app or in a Mail attachment.
2. Tap the **Markup** icon (a pen inside a circle, usually in the top right corner).
3. Tap the **+** button at the bottom, then tap **Signature**.
4. Draw your signature with your finger. Tap **Done**.
5. Drag and resize the signature block to the correct position on the page.
6. Tap **Done** to save.

If the PDF came as an email attachment, you can sign it directly in Mail and tap **Reply** to send back the signed version without ever saving a separate file.

## How to sign a PDF on Android

Android doesn't have a single universal PDF signer built in, but Adobe Acrobat Reader is free and works well:

1. Install [Adobe Acrobat Reader](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adobe.reader) from the Play Store (free, no subscription needed for basic signing).
2. Open your PDF in the app.
3. Tap the pencil/edit icon, then tap **Fill & Sign**.
4. Tap the **Signature** tool and draw your signature with your finger.
5. Tap **Done**, position the signature on the page, then save or share.

## Are electronic signatures legally binding?

Yes, for the vast majority of documents. In the United States, the [ESIGN Act (15 U.S.C. 7001)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001) and the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA, adopted by 49 states) give electronic signatures the same legal standing as handwritten ones. The European Union's [eIDAS regulation](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/esignature) covers the same ground across EU member states.

The narrow exceptions: wills, court orders that explicitly require wet-ink signatures, and certain real estate or government filings. If a form requires a wet-ink signature, it will say so clearly. For standard contracts, HR paperwork, rental agreements, and business documents in general, an e-signature is fully valid.

## What's the difference between an electronic signature and a digital signature?

An **electronic signature** is any digital representation of your intent to sign: a drawn name, a typed name in a script font, or an uploaded image. It's the everyday method and what most people mean when they say "e-sign."

A **digital signature** is a cryptographically verified signature, where a certificate authority confirms your identity and the document is tamper-evident. Tools like Adobe Sign and DocuSign can produce these. For most contracts and everyday paperwork, a regular electronic signature is all you need.
