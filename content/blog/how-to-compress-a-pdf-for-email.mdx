To compress a PDF for email, open a free compress tool, drop your file in, and download the smaller version. It takes seconds and, with an in-browser tool, your file is never uploaded anywhere. One thing first, though, because most guides skip it: compression only helps if your PDF is full of images. On a text document it can make the file dramatically bigger.

## What the email size limit actually is

Both [Gmail](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6584) and [Outlook.com](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/sending-limits-in-outlook-com-279ee200-594c-40f0-9ec8-bb6af7735c2e) cap a message at 25 MB, and that cap covers everything in the message, not just the attachment.

There is a catch that trips people up. Attachments are encoded as [Base64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base64) before they travel, which inflates them by roughly a third. So a 20 MB PDF arrives at the limit as about 26 MB and gets rejected, even though 20 is visibly less than 25. Work backwards from about 18 MB and you will stay clear.

## First, work out what is inside your PDF

This is the step that decides everything else. Open the file and try to select a line of text with your cursor.

- **The text highlights.** It is a real text document, exported from Word, Google Docs, or similar. Compression is the wrong tool.
- **Nothing highlights, or you select the whole page as one block.** It is a scan or photos. Compression will help a lot.

The reason this matters is that in-browser compression works by rasterizing: every page is redrawn as a JPEG image. When the page is already a photograph, that is a big win. When the page is text stored as font data, replacing it with a photograph of the same text is a large step backwards.

## What we measured

We ran three documents through our own [Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf) in Chromium and recorded the exact output size. Full method and corpus are in our editorial notes.

| Document | Level | Before | After | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6-page image deck | High | 913.4 KB | 175.6 KB | 81% smaller |
| 6-page image deck | Medium | 913.4 KB | 315.3 KB | 66% smaller |
| 6-page image deck | Low | 913.4 KB | 703.5 KB | 23% smaller |
| 20-page text report | Medium | 23.5 KB | 1400.8 KB | 60x **bigger** |
| 3-page text plus logo | Medium | 35.0 KB | 171.2 KB | 5x **bigger** |

The pattern is stark. Image-heavy files shrink by two thirds or more. Text files do the opposite, and they do it silently, so nobody thinks to check the size afterwards.

## The fastest way to compress an image-heavy PDF

1. Open the [free Compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf).
2. Drop your PDF into the box, or click to browse for it.
3. Pick a level. Medium is the sensible default. High squeezes hardest and softens fine detail, which matters on small print in a scanned contract.
4. Click **Compress PDF** and wait a moment while it processes in your browser.
5. Download the smaller file, check the size, and attach it.

Because the tool runs entirely in your browser, the document never leaves your device. That is worth something when the PDF is a contract, an invoice, or a set of medical records you would rather not hand to a stranger's server.

## If it is a text PDF and still too big

A text PDF that is too large is large for a different reason, so it needs a different fix.

- **Something raster got embedded.** A logo dropped in at full camera resolution, a signature image scanned at 600 DPI, a screenshot pasted on the cover page. One oversized image can account for nearly all of the file.
- **Fonts are embedded in full.** Exporting with subsetting turned on keeps only the characters the document actually uses.
- **The file carries revision history.** Re-saving or printing to PDF flattens it.
- **It is simply long.** A 400-page manual is big because it is 400 pages.

For the last case, send less of it. Use the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf) to pull out the pages the recipient asked for. Nobody wants the whole manual anyway.

## If it is still too big after compressing

- **Split it into parts.** Two or three smaller PDFs with the [Split PDF tool](/split-pdf), sent across two messages.
- **Send a link instead.** Both Gmail and Outlook will offer to swap an oversized attachment for a cloud link, and that path has no practical size ceiling.
- **Compress at High rather than Medium.** On a scan this is often the difference between 5 MB and 2 MB, at the cost of some sharpness.

If you are combining several files before sending, merge first with the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf), then compress the result. Merging does not inflate anything: in our testing the merged output came out marginally smaller than the sum of its inputs, because shared structure gets written once.
