You rotated the PDF in your browser, saved it, reopened it, and it's sideways again. That's not a bug in your file. It's how PDF viewers work, and the fix takes about thirty seconds once you know which tool to use.

## Why rotating in Adobe Reader or Chrome doesn't stick

Free PDF viewers, including Adobe Acrobat Reader, Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, and Firefox, are built to display files, not edit them. When you click the rotate button in the toolbar, the viewer tilts the display for your current session. It doesn't write anything to the file itself.

PDF page orientation is stored as a `/Rotate` entry in the [PDF page dictionary](https://opensource.adobe.com/dc-acrobat-sdk-docs/pdfstandards/PDF32000_2008.pdf). A viewer that only reads PDFs skips writing that entry back to disk. So every time you close and reopen the file, the viewer reads the original orientation and shows it as-is.

To make a rotation permanent, you need a tool that actually writes the updated `/Rotate` value back into the file. That's what the [Rotate PDF tool](/rotate-pdf) does, and it runs entirely in your browser so nothing gets uploaded anywhere.

## How to rotate a PDF and save it permanently (step by step)

1. Open the [Rotate PDF tool](/rotate-pdf).
2. Drop your PDF onto the page or click to select it from your device.
3. Click the rotate arrow on any page thumbnail to turn it 90 degrees clockwise or counterclockwise. Click again to rotate another 90 degrees.
4. To rotate all pages at once, use the "Rotate all" button above the thumbnails.
5. Click **Download** when you're done.

The file you download has the rotation baked in. Open it in any viewer on any device and it'll show in the correct orientation.

## Can I rotate just one page in a PDF?

Yes. The tool shows a thumbnail for each page. You rotate pages individually, so you can fix a single sideways scan while leaving the rest of the document untouched. This is useful when you've scanned a batch of documents and one or two pages came out at the wrong angle.

If you want to go further and rearrange pages at the same time, the [Organize PDF tool](/organize-pdf) lets you reorder, rotate, and delete pages in one step.

## Does rotating a PDF reduce quality?

No, and unlike most claims of this kind it is easy to check. Rotation does not touch the image or text data at all. The tool changes one orientation value per page, so the content, photos, text, vector graphics, comes through untouched.

We measured it on two very different files. A 913.4 KB image-heavy document came back at 913.4 KB, a change of 0.00%. A 23.5 KB text report came back at 23.5 KB, a change of 0.02%, which is a handful of bytes of bookkeeping. Both took well under a tenth of a second.

That is the tell for whether a tool is rotating properly or quietly re-rendering your pages. If a "rotate" tool hands back a file that is noticeably larger or smaller than the one you gave it, it has rebuilt your pages as images rather than setting the orientation flag, and your text is no longer text.

## How to rotate a PDF on Mac without Acrobat

macOS Preview can rotate PDF pages and save permanently. Open the PDF in Preview, choose **View > Thumbnails**, click a page thumbnail to select it, then go to **Tools > Rotate Left** (or Right). Save with Cmd+S. Unlike Chrome or Safari's reader view, Preview writes the change to the file.

The in-browser [Rotate PDF tool](/rotate-pdf) also works on Mac in any browser, so you don't need to find Preview if you're already in a browser tab.

## How to rotate a PDF on Windows without Acrobat

Microsoft Edge has a rotate icon in the PDF toolbar, but like other viewers it's view-only and won't save permanently. The simplest free option on Windows is to use the browser-based [Rotate PDF tool](/rotate-pdf): upload, rotate, download. No install, no account.

Alternatively, if you have LibreOffice Draw installed, you can open the PDF and rotate individual pages there, though the workflow is slower than using an online tool.
