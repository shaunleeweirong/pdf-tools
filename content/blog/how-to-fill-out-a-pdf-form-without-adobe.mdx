You can fill out a PDF form without Adobe Acrobat, free, in your browser, with no account required. The [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) handles both interactive fields and flat scanned forms, and your file never leaves your device.

## Two types of PDF forms (and why it matters)

Not every PDF form works the same way. There are two types:

**Interactive forms** (also called [AcroForms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF#Interactive_elements)) have clickable fields built into the file. Click a blank box and a cursor appears. Most government, tax, and legal forms ship in this format.

**Non-interactive or flat forms** look like a form but have no real fields. They're often scanned paper forms or printable PDFs where the "lines" are just images. Adobe Reader (the free version) cannot type into these at all.

The distinction matters because some tools only handle interactive forms. A browser-based filler that overlays text and checkmarks directly on the PDF image works on both types, so you don't have to figure out which kind you have before you start.

## How to fill out a PDF form in your browser

1. Open the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form). No download or account needed.
2. Drag your PDF onto the page, or click to pick a file.
3. The tool detects interactive fields automatically. Click a field and start typing.
4. For flat forms, use the text tool to place a text box wherever you need to write. Drag it to line up with the printed lines on the form.
5. Tick checkboxes by clicking them. For flat forms without real checkbox fields, use the checkmark tool to drop a checkmark symbol over the printed box.
6. Click **Download** when you're done. The completed PDF saves to your device.

The process takes under two minutes for most standard forms. Because everything runs in your browser, your file is never sent to a server.

## What if the form has locked or read-only fields?

Some forms are locked by their creator to prevent edits. If a tool throws an error when you try to click a field, the PDF has edit restrictions set on it.

In that case, a tool that renders the PDF as an image and overlays your typed text can still work. The result looks correct when printed or viewed, even though the underlying fields stay locked. If the form explicitly requires a certified digital signature, you'll need to use software that can verify certificates, such as Adobe Acrobat or a dedicated signing platform. For most everyday forms, a typed-text overlay is fine.

## How to fill out a PDF form on iPhone or Android

No app needed. Open your phone's browser (Safari on iPhone, Chrome on Android) and go to the [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form). Tap the upload button, then pick your file from Files on iOS or from Downloads or Google Drive on Android.

The mobile experience works the same as on desktop. Tap a field to type, pinch to zoom in on small text boxes, and tap Download when you're finished. The filled PDF goes to your downloads folder.

If you also need a handwritten signature, the [Sign PDF tool](/sign-pdf) lets you draw one with your finger and place it anywhere on the page. Many people fill the form first, download it, then open it in the sign tool to add a signature.

## Is it safe to fill out a form in a browser?

That depends entirely on where the processing happens.

Most online PDF tools upload your file to a server, process it there, and send the result back to your browser. For a form containing personal information (a tax return, a medical intake form, a lease agreement), that upload creates a real privacy exposure. You're trusting the server operator not to log or store the data.

The [Fill PDF Form tool](/fill-form) works differently. It runs in your browser using [PDF.js](https://mozilla.github.io/pdf.js/), Mozilla's open-source PDF rendering engine. Your file never travels over a network. All processing is local to your device, and the downloaded file is generated client-side. There is no server receiving your form data.

If you're filling out anything with personal details, look for a tool that specifically states "in-browser" or "client-side" processing, and read the privacy policy before uploading.
